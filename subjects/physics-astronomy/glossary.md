# Physics and Astronomy glossary

Terms (`{{id|text}}` pop-ups) and footnotes (`^[id]`). Each entry has a title, a definition, and an optional `concept:` link to the full page.

---

## Terms

### equation
- title: Equation
- def: Two expressions with an equals sign between them, saying they are the same number. $3x + 5 = 20$ is an equation.
- concept: rearranging-formulas

### inverse-op
- title: Inverse operation
- def: The operation that undoes another. Subtracting undoes adding, dividing undoes multiplying, and a square root undoes squaring.

### formula
- title: Formula
- def: An equation that connects quantities, written with letters. $v = \frac{d}{t}$ connects speed, distance and time.

### variable
- title: Variable
- def: A letter that stands for a number, often one that can change or that you want to find.

### power-of-ten
- title: Power of ten
- def: 10 multiplied by itself some number of times, written $10^n$. $10^3 = 1000$, and $10^{-2} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$.

### sig-fig
- title: Significant figures
- def: The digits in a measurement that carry real information about its size. 0.0250 has three: 2, 5 and the final 0.
- concept: significant-figures

### precision
- title: Precision
- def: How fine a measurement is, shown by its last digit. 14.63 cm is more precise than 14.6 cm.

### unit
- title: Unit
- def: The fixed amount a measurement counts in, like the metre or the second. "2 m" means two metres.
- concept: units

### si
- title: SI units
- def: The International System of Units, the set of units scientists everywhere agree on: metre, kilogram, second and a few more. SI stands for its French name, Système international.
- concept: units

### prefix
- title: Prefix
- def: A word put in front of a unit to multiply it by a power of ten. Kilo means 1000, so a kilometre is 1000 metres.

### derived-unit
- title: Derived unit
- def: A unit built by multiplying or dividing base units. The unit of speed, m/s, is metres divided by seconds.

### dimensional
- title: Checking the units
- def: Testing a formula by working out the units on each side. If they do not match, the formula is wrong. Also called dimensional analysis.

### conversion-factor
- title: Conversion factor
- def: A fraction worth exactly 1 that changes units, like $\frac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}$. The top and bottom are the same amount written in different units.
- concept: unit-conversion

### proportional
- title: Directly proportional
- def: Two quantities are directly proportional when one is always the same number times the other, $y = kx$. Double one and the other doubles.
- concept: proportional-reasoning

### constant-k
- title: Constant of proportionality
- def: The fixed number k in a proportion like $y = kx$ or $y = \frac{k}{x}$. It stays the same while x and y change.

### quadratic
- title: Quadratic equation
- def: An equation whose highest power of x is $x^2$, like $2x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$.
- concept: quadratic-formula

### discriminant
- title: Discriminant
- def: The part $b^2 - 4ac$ under the square root in the quadratic formula. Its sign tells you whether there are two, one or no solutions.

### completing-square
- title: Completing the square
- def: Adding the right number to both sides so that one side becomes a perfect square, like $(x + 3)^2$, which can then be undone with a square root.

### system
- title: System of equations
- def: Two or more equations about the same unknowns, which must all be true at once.
- concept: systems-of-equations

### substitute
- title: Substitute
- def: Replace a letter with something equal to it, such as a number or another expression.

### right-angle
- title: Right angle
- def: A square corner, exactly 90°. It is a quarter of a full turn.

### hypotenuse
- title: Hypotenuse
- def: The longest side of a right triangle, across from the right angle.

### pythagoras
- title: Pythagorean theorem
- def: In a right triangle, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, where c is the hypotenuse. Named after the Greek thinker Pythagoras, though others knew it earlier.
- concept: pythagorean-theorem

### vector
- title: Vector
- def: A quantity with a size and a direction, like 5 m/s north or 10 N downward. Drawn as an arrow.
- concept: vectors

### scalar
- title: Scalar
- def: A quantity with a size only and no direction, like a mass of 3 kg or a time of 20 s.

### magnitude
- title: Magnitude
- def: The size of a vector, ignoring its direction. Always zero or positive. The magnitude of (3, 4) is 5.

### component
- title: Component
- def: The part of a vector that points along one direction, such as across or up. A vector at an angle has an across component and an up component.
- concept: vector-components

### resultant
- title: Resultant
- def: The single vector you get by adding two or more vectors. It has the same effect as all of them together.
- concept: vector-addition

### origin
- title: Origin
- def: The zero point you choose to measure positions from. Positions on one side are positive, on the other side negative.

### displacement
- title: Displacement
- def: The change in position, end minus start. A vector: it has a size and a direction, and it ignores the path taken.
- concept: position-displacement

### velocity
- title: Velocity
- def: How fast position changes, and in which direction: displacement divided by time, in m/s. A vector.
- concept: velocity

### speed
- title: Speed
- def: How fast something moves, with no direction. The size of the velocity. Average speed is total distance divided by time.

### acceleration
- title: Acceleration
- def: How fast velocity changes: change in velocity divided by time, in m/s². Speeding up, slowing down and turning are all acceleration.
- concept: acceleration

### free-fall
- title: Free fall
- def: Motion where gravity is the only influence. Near the Earth, everything in free fall accelerates downward at about 9.8 m/s².
- concept: free-fall

### frame
- title: Frame of reference
- def: The point of view you measure motion from, such as the ground, a moving train or a plane. Velocities depend on the frame.
- concept: relative-motion

### projectile
- title: Projectile
- def: Anything thrown, kicked or fired that then moves under gravity alone, like a ball in flight.
- concept: projectile-motion

### force
- title: Force
- def: A push or a pull. It has a size, measured in newtons (N), and a direction, so it is a vector.
- concept: force

### newton
- title: Newton (N)
- def: The SI unit of force. One newton gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². A small apple weighs about 1 N.

### free-body
- title: Free-body diagram
- def: A sketch of one object on its own, with an arrow for every force acting on it, showing each force's direction and size.

### net-force
- title: Net force
- def: The total of all the forces on an object, added as vectors. It decides how the object's motion changes.

### inertia
- title: Inertia
- def: An object's tendency to keep doing what it is doing: stay still, or keep moving at the same velocity. More mass means more inertia.
- concept: newtons-first-law

### mass
- title: Mass
- def: How much matter an object has, in kilograms. It measures inertia and does not change from place to place.
- concept: weight

### force-pair
- title: Third-law pair
- def: Two forces, equal in size and opposite in direction, that two objects exert on each other. They act on different objects, so they never cancel.
- concept: newtons-third-law

### weight
- title: Weight
- def: The force of gravity on an object, $W = mg$, in newtons, pointing toward the centre of the planet.
- concept: weight

### field-strength
- title: Gravitational field strength
- def: The pull of gravity on each kilogram, in N/kg. On Earth it is about 9.8 N/kg, the same number as g in m/s².

### normal-force
- title: Normal force
- def: The push a surface gives on an object pressing on it, at right angles to the surface.
- concept: contact-forces

### tension
- title: Tension
- def: The pull of a rope, string or cable, along its length. A rope can pull but not push.
- concept: contact-forces

### friction
- title: Friction
- def: The force between two surfaces that resists them sliding over each other. It acts along the surfaces.
- concept: friction

### coefficient-friction
- title: Coefficient of friction
- def: A number, written μ, that says how grippy two surfaces are together. Friction is μ times the normal force. It has no units.

### static-friction
- title: Static friction
- def: Friction between surfaces that are not sliding. It matches the force trying to slide them, up to a maximum of $\mu_s N$.

### centripetal
- title: Centripetal
- def: Pointing toward the centre of a circle. Centripetal acceleration is $\frac{v^2}{r}$; the centripetal force is the net inward force that causes it.
- concept: circular-motion

### period
- title: Period
- def: The time for one complete lap, swing or cycle, in seconds.

### spring-constant
- title: Spring constant
- def: The number k in Hooke's law, $F = kx$, in N/m. It says how stiff a spring is: the force needed per metre of stretch.
- concept: hookes-law

### elastic-limit
- title: Elastic limit
- def: The furthest a spring or material can be stretched and still return to its original shape when let go.

### joule
- title: Joule (J)
- def: The unit of work and energy. One joule is the work done by a 1 N force moving something 1 m. Lifting a small apple 1 m takes about 1 J.
- concept: work

### kinetic-energy
- title: Kinetic energy
- def: The energy an object has because it is moving, $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
- concept: kinetic-energy

### potential-energy
- title: Potential energy
- def: Stored energy, waiting to be used. Gravitational potential energy, $mgh$, is stored by lifting something.
- concept: potential-energy

### conservation-energy
- title: Conservation of energy
- def: The law that energy is never made or destroyed, only changed from one form to another, so the total stays the same.
- concept: conservation-of-energy

### thermal-energy
- title: Thermal energy
- def: The energy of the jiggling of atoms and molecules, which we feel as heat. Friction turns motion into thermal energy.

### elastic-pe
- title: Elastic potential energy
- def: Energy stored in something stretched, squashed or bent, like a spring or a drawn bow. For a spring, $\tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.
- concept: spring-energy

### watt
- title: Watt (W)
- def: The unit of power: one joule every second. A 60 W lamp uses 60 J each second.
- concept: power

### kwh
- title: Kilowatt-hour (kWh)
- def: A unit of energy used on electricity bills: 1000 W for one hour, which is 3.6 million joules.
- concept: power

### momentum
- title: Momentum
- def: Mass times velocity, $p = mv$. It measures how hard a moving thing is to stop, and points the way it moves.
- concept: momentum

### impulse
- title: Impulse
- def: A force times the time it acts, $F\Delta t$. It equals the change in momentum it causes.
- concept: impulse

### conservation-momentum
- title: Conservation of momentum
- def: The law that the total momentum of a group of objects stays the same when no outside force acts on them.
- concept: conservation-of-momentum

### elastic-collision
- title: Elastic collision
- def: A collision in which the total kinetic energy is the same before and after. The objects bounce apart.
- concept: collisions

### inelastic-collision
- title: Inelastic collision
- def: A collision in which some kinetic energy turns into heat, sound or bending. If the objects stick together, it is perfectly inelastic.
- concept: collisions

## Footnotes

### n_two_roots
- title: Two square roots
- def: Both $7 \times 7$ and $(-7) \times (-7)$ give 49, so $x^2 = 49$ has two answers, 7 and −7. Math writes this as $x = \pm 7$. In physics the context usually rules one out, such as a length or a speed, which cannot be negative.

### n_e_notation
- title: E on a calculator
- def: The E stands for "times ten to the". 3.2E-5 means $3.2 \times 10^{-5}$. It is not the number e used in exponential functions.

### n_exact_numbers
- title: Exact numbers
- def: A counted number (5 students) or a defined one (1 km = 1000 m exactly) has no measuring error. Treat it as having as many significant figures as you need.

### n_other_si
- title: The other two base units
- def: The mole (mol) counts amounts of a substance, mostly used in chemistry. The candela (cd) measures how bright a light looks. That makes seven SI base units in all.

### n_mars_orbiter
- title: The lost Mars probe
- def: NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter, 1999. Software from one team reported thruster pushes in pound-force seconds; the navigation team expected newton-seconds. The probe flew too close to Mars and was lost.

### n_complex
- title: Numbers beyond the real ones
- def: Mathematicians invented "imaginary" numbers, with $i^2 = -1$, so that every quadratic has answers. They matter in electronics and quantum physics, but no measured length or time is imaginary, so this web does not need them.

### n_no_solution
- title: Parallel lines
- def: Each equation in two unknowns is a straight line on a graph, and the solution is where the lines cross. Parallel lines never cross, so there is no solution. If the two equations are really the same line, every point on it works.

### n_3d_pythagoras
- title: Three directions
- def: In space, add a third direction: the distance is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$. It comes from using the theorem twice.

### n_arc_names
- title: Why "arc"
- def: On a circle of radius 1, an angle in radians equals the length of the arc it cuts off. So arcsin(0.5) means "the arc whose sine is 0.5".

### n_inverse_range
- title: Which angle the calculator gives
- def: Many angles share a sine: $\sin 30° = \sin 150° = 0.5$. So $\sin^{-1}$ always gives the one between −90° and 90°, $\cos^{-1}$ the one between 0° and 180°, and $\tan^{-1}$ the one between −90° and 90°. In a right triangle the angle is always between 0° and 90°, so the calculator's answer is the one you want.

### n_area_integral
- title: Area and integrals
- def: Adding up thinner and thinner strips is exactly what an integral does. In calculus terms, displacement is the integral of velocity over time. The Calculus web explains this on its definite integral page.

### n_suvat
- title: "suvat"
- def: Many textbooks call these the "suvat" equations, after the five letters s, u, v, a and t. Some books write $v_0$ or $v_i$ for the start velocity and $\Delta x$ or d for the displacement.

### n_g_varies
- title: g is not quite the same everywhere
- def: g is about 9.78 m/s² at the equator and 9.83 m/s² at the poles, and a little smaller on high mountains. 9.8 m/s² is a good average. On the Moon it is about 1.6 m/s².

### n_galilean
- title: When adding velocities fails
- def: Adding velocities works for everything in daily life. Near the speed of light it breaks down: light moves at the same speed for every observer. The page on special relativity explains what replaces it.

### n_air_resistance
- title: Real balls and air
- def: Air pushes back on a moving ball, so real throws fall short of these numbers, and the best angle is usually a bit below 45°. For a heavy, slow object over a short distance, the effect is small.

### n_galileo_newton
- title: Who worked it out
- def: For nearly 2000 years people followed Aristotle, who taught that moving things need a constant push. Galileo, in the early 1600s, rolled balls on ramps and argued that motion continues unless something stops it. Isaac Newton made it his first law in 1687.

### n_scales
- title: What bathroom scales measure
- def: A bathroom scale measures how hard you press on it, a force, then divides by 9.8 to show kilograms. Take it to the Moon and it would show about a sixth of your mass, which is wrong. A balance that compares you against known masses would still be right.

### n_centripetal_proof
- title: Where v²/r comes from
- def: In a short time t, the object goes a distance $vt$ around the circle, so its direction turns through an angle $\frac{vt}{r}$ in radians. The velocity arrow, length v, turns by that angle, so it changes by about $v \times \frac{vt}{r}$. Divide by t to get the acceleration: $\frac{v^2}{r}$.

### n_hooke_sign
- title: The minus sign in Hooke's law
- def: $F = kx$ gives the size of the force. Writing $F = -kx$ also gives its direction: if x is a stretch to the right (positive), the spring pulls to the left (negative).

### n_conservative
- title: Forces where the path does not matter
- def: Gravity and springs are called conservative forces: the work they do depends only on where you start and finish, not the route. Friction is not like this. A longer route means more friction and more work against it.

### n_joule_heat
- title: Heat is energy
- def: In the 1840s James Joule let a falling weight turn a paddle in water and measured the water warming. The same amount of work always gave the same warming. That showed heat is a form of energy, and the unit of energy is named after him.

### n_newton_momentum
- title: Newton's own version
- def: Newton wrote his second law as "the change of motion is proportional to the force", where by "motion" he meant mass times velocity. $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ also works when the mass changes, as for a rocket burning fuel; $F = ma$ assumes a fixed mass.

### n_elastic_solve
- title: Solving the elastic collision
- def: Momentum: $m_1u = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$. Energy: $\tfrac{1}{2}m_1u^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$. Rearranging and dividing one by the other gives $u + v_1 = v_2$, a simple straight-line equation. Putting that into the momentum equation gives the two formulas.
