# Physics and Astronomy glossary

Terms (`{{id|text}}` pop-ups) and footnotes (`^[id]`). Each entry has a title, a definition, and an optional `concept:` link to the full page.

---

## Terms

### equation
- title: Equation
- def: Two expressions with an equals sign between them, saying they are the same number. $3x + 5 = 20$ is an equation.
- concept: rearranging-formulas

### inverse-op
- title: Inverse operation
- def: The operation that undoes another. Subtracting undoes adding, dividing undoes multiplying, and a square root undoes squaring.

### formula
- title: Formula
- def: An equation that connects quantities, written with letters. $v = \frac{d}{t}$ connects speed, distance and time.

### variable
- title: Variable
- def: A letter that stands for a number, often one that can change or that you want to find.

### power-of-ten
- title: Power of ten
- def: 10 multiplied by itself some number of times, written $10^n$. $10^3 = 1000$, and $10^{-2} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01$.

### sig-fig
- title: Significant figures
- def: The digits in a measurement that carry real information about its size. 0.0250 has three: 2, 5 and the final 0.
- concept: significant-figures

### precision
- title: Precision
- def: How fine a measurement is, shown by its last digit. 14.63 cm is more precise than 14.6 cm.

### unit
- title: Unit
- def: The fixed amount a measurement counts in, like the metre or the second. "2 m" means two metres.
- concept: units

### si
- title: SI units
- def: The International System of Units, the set of units scientists everywhere agree on: metre, kilogram, second and a few more. SI stands for its French name, Système international.
- concept: units

### prefix
- title: Prefix
- def: A word put in front of a unit to multiply it by a power of ten. Kilo means 1000, so a kilometre is 1000 metres.

### derived-unit
- title: Derived unit
- def: A unit built by multiplying or dividing base units. The unit of speed, m/s, is metres divided by seconds.

### dimensional
- title: Checking the units
- def: Testing a formula by working out the units on each side. If they do not match, the formula is wrong. Also called dimensional analysis.

### conversion-factor
- title: Conversion factor
- def: A fraction worth exactly 1 that changes units, like $\frac{1000\ \text{m}}{1\ \text{km}}$. The top and bottom are the same amount written in different units.
- concept: unit-conversion

### proportional
- title: Directly proportional
- def: Two quantities are directly proportional when one is always the same number times the other, $y = kx$. Double one and the other doubles.
- concept: proportional-reasoning

### constant-k
- title: Constant of proportionality
- def: The fixed number k in a proportion like $y = kx$ or $y = \frac{k}{x}$. It stays the same while x and y change.

### quadratic
- title: Quadratic equation
- def: An equation whose highest power of x is $x^2$, like $2x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$.
- concept: quadratic-formula

### discriminant
- title: Discriminant
- def: The part $b^2 - 4ac$ under the square root in the quadratic formula. Its sign tells you whether there are two, one or no solutions.

### completing-square
- title: Completing the square
- def: Adding the right number to both sides so that one side becomes a perfect square, like $(x + 3)^2$, which can then be undone with a square root.

### system
- title: System of equations
- def: Two or more equations about the same unknowns, which must all be true at once.
- concept: systems-of-equations

### substitute
- title: Substitute
- def: Replace a letter with something equal to it, such as a number or another expression.

### right-angle
- title: Right angle
- def: A square corner, exactly 90°. It is a quarter of a full turn.

### hypotenuse
- title: Hypotenuse
- def: The longest side of a right triangle, across from the right angle.

### pythagoras
- title: Pythagorean theorem
- def: In a right triangle, $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, where c is the hypotenuse. Named after the Greek thinker Pythagoras, though others knew it earlier.
- concept: pythagorean-theorem

### vector
- title: Vector
- def: A quantity with a size and a direction, like 5 m/s north or 10 N downward. Drawn as an arrow.
- concept: vectors

### scalar
- title: Scalar
- def: A quantity with a size only and no direction, like a mass of 3 kg or a time of 20 s.

### magnitude
- title: Magnitude
- def: The size of a vector, ignoring its direction. Always zero or positive. The magnitude of (3, 4) is 5.

### component
- title: Component
- def: The part of a vector that points along one direction, such as across or up. A vector at an angle has an across component and an up component.
- concept: vector-components

### resultant
- title: Resultant
- def: The single vector you get by adding two or more vectors. It has the same effect as all of them together.
- concept: vector-addition

### origin
- title: Origin
- def: The zero point you choose to measure positions from. Positions on one side are positive, on the other side negative.

### displacement
- title: Displacement
- def: The change in position, end minus start. A vector: it has a size and a direction, and it ignores the path taken.
- concept: position-displacement

### velocity
- title: Velocity
- def: How fast position changes, and in which direction: displacement divided by time, in m/s. A vector.
- concept: velocity

### speed
- title: Speed
- def: How fast something moves, with no direction. The size of the velocity. Average speed is total distance divided by time.

### acceleration
- title: Acceleration
- def: How fast velocity changes: change in velocity divided by time, in m/s². Speeding up, slowing down and turning are all acceleration.
- concept: acceleration

### free-fall
- title: Free fall
- def: Motion where gravity is the only influence. Near the Earth, everything in free fall accelerates downward at about 9.8 m/s².
- concept: free-fall

### frame
- title: Frame of reference
- def: The point of view you measure motion from, such as the ground, a moving train or a plane. Velocities depend on the frame.
- concept: relative-motion

### projectile
- title: Projectile
- def: Anything thrown, kicked or fired that then moves under gravity alone, like a ball in flight.
- concept: projectile-motion

### force
- title: Force
- def: A push or a pull. It has a size, measured in newtons (N), and a direction, so it is a vector.
- concept: force

### newton
- title: Newton (N)
- def: The SI unit of force. One newton gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². A small apple weighs about 1 N.

### free-body
- title: Free-body diagram
- def: A sketch of one object on its own, with an arrow for every force acting on it, showing each force's direction and size.

### net-force
- title: Net force
- def: The total of all the forces on an object, added as vectors. It decides how the object's motion changes.

### inertia
- title: Inertia
- def: An object's tendency to keep doing what it is doing: stay still, or keep moving at the same velocity. More mass means more inertia.
- concept: newtons-first-law

### mass
- title: Mass
- def: How much matter an object has, in kilograms. It measures inertia and does not change from place to place.
- concept: weight

### force-pair
- title: Third-law pair
- def: Two forces, equal in size and opposite in direction, that two objects exert on each other. They act on different objects, so they never cancel.
- concept: newtons-third-law

### weight
- title: Weight
- def: The force of gravity on an object, $W = mg$, in newtons, pointing toward the centre of the planet.
- concept: weight

### field-strength
- title: Gravitational field strength
- def: The pull of gravity on each kilogram, in N/kg. On Earth it is about 9.8 N/kg, the same number as g in m/s².

### normal-force
- title: Normal force
- def: The push a surface gives on an object pressing on it, at right angles to the surface.
- concept: contact-forces

### tension
- title: Tension
- def: The pull of a rope, string or cable, along its length. A rope can pull but not push.
- concept: contact-forces

### friction
- title: Friction
- def: The force between two surfaces that resists them sliding over each other. It acts along the surfaces.
- concept: friction

### coefficient-friction
- title: Coefficient of friction
- def: A number, written μ, that says how grippy two surfaces are together. Friction is μ times the normal force. It has no units.

### static-friction
- title: Static friction
- def: Friction between surfaces that are not sliding. It matches the force trying to slide them, up to a maximum of $\mu_s N$.

### centripetal
- title: Centripetal
- def: Pointing toward the centre of a circle. Centripetal acceleration is $\frac{v^2}{r}$; the centripetal force is the net inward force that causes it.
- concept: circular-motion

### period
- title: Period
- def: The time for one complete lap, swing or cycle, in seconds.

### spring-constant
- title: Spring constant
- def: The number k in Hooke's law, $F = kx$, in N/m. It says how stiff a spring is: the force needed per metre of stretch.
- concept: hookes-law

### elastic-limit
- title: Elastic limit
- def: The furthest a spring or material can be stretched and still return to its original shape when let go.

### joule
- title: Joule (J)
- def: The unit of work and energy. One joule is the work done by a 1 N force moving something 1 m. Lifting a small apple 1 m takes about 1 J.
- concept: work

### kinetic-energy
- title: Kinetic energy
- def: The energy an object has because it is moving, $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
- concept: kinetic-energy

### potential-energy
- title: Potential energy
- def: Stored energy, waiting to be used. Gravitational potential energy, $mgh$, is stored by lifting something.
- concept: potential-energy

### conservation-energy
- title: Conservation of energy
- def: The law that energy is never made or destroyed, only changed from one form to another, so the total stays the same.
- concept: conservation-of-energy

### thermal-energy
- title: Thermal energy
- def: The energy of the jiggling of atoms and molecules, which we feel as heat. Friction turns motion into thermal energy.
- concept: temperature

### elastic-pe
- title: Elastic potential energy
- def: Energy stored in something stretched, squashed or bent, like a spring or a drawn bow. For a spring, $\tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$.
- concept: spring-energy

### watt
- title: Watt (W)
- def: The unit of power: one joule every second. A 60 W lamp uses 60 J each second.
- concept: power

### kwh
- title: Kilowatt-hour (kWh)
- def: A unit of energy used on electricity bills: 1000 W for one hour, which is 3.6 million joules.
- concept: power

### momentum
- title: Momentum
- def: Mass times velocity, $p = mv$. It measures how hard a moving thing is to stop, and points the way it moves.
- concept: momentum

### impulse
- title: Impulse
- def: A force times the time it acts, $F\Delta t$. It equals the change in momentum it causes.
- concept: impulse

### conservation-momentum
- title: Conservation of momentum
- def: The law that the total momentum of a group of objects stays the same when no outside force acts on them.
- concept: conservation-of-momentum

### elastic-collision
- title: Elastic collision
- def: A collision in which the total kinetic energy is the same before and after. The objects bounce apart.
- concept: collisions

### inelastic-collision
- title: Inelastic collision
- def: A collision in which some kinetic energy turns into heat, sound or bending. If the objects stick together, it is perfectly inelastic.
- concept: collisions

### torque
- title: Torque
- def: The turning effect of a force: the force times its distance from the pivot, when it acts at right angles. Measured in N·m.
- concept: torque

### pivot
- title: Pivot
- def: The fixed point something turns around, like a door's hinge or the middle of a seesaw.

### equilibrium
- title: Static equilibrium
- def: Staying perfectly still, because the forces cancel and the turning effects (torques) cancel too.
- concept: equilibrium

### centre-of-gravity
- title: Centre of gravity
- def: The point where an object's whole weight can be treated as acting. For a uniform plank or ball, it is the middle.

### radian
- title: Radian
- def: A way to measure angles: arc length divided by radius. A full turn is $2\pi$ radians, and one radian is about 57°.
- concept: rotational-motion

### angular-velocity
- title: Angular speed
- def: How fast something spins: the angle it turns through each second, in radians per second (rad/s). Written ω.
- concept: rotational-motion

### angular-acceleration
- title: Angular acceleration
- def: How fast the spin rate changes, in rad/s². Written α.

### moment-of-inertia
- title: Moment of inertia
- def: How hard something is to spin up or slow down. It depends on the mass and how far it is from the axis: $mr^2$ for a small mass.
- concept: angular-momentum

### angular-momentum
- title: Angular momentum
- def: The spinning version of momentum, $L = I\omega$. With no outside twist, it stays the same.
- concept: angular-momentum

### gravitational-constant
- title: Gravitational constant (G)
- def: The number in Newton's law of gravitation, $6.67 \times 10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg². Its tiny size is why gravity between everyday objects is too weak to notice.
- concept: gravitation

### inverse-square
- title: Inverse-square law
- def: A rule where something gets weaker with the square of the distance: twice as far, a quarter as strong. Gravity, light and sound all spread out this way.
- concept: gravitation

### orbit
- title: Orbit
- def: The path of one object around another, held by gravity. A satellite in orbit is falling all the time, but moving sideways fast enough to keep missing the planet.
- concept: orbits

### weightlessness
- title: Weightlessness
- def: The floating feeling in free fall. Gravity still pulls, but you and everything around you fall together, so nothing pushes on you.

### ellipse
- title: Ellipse
- def: A stretched circle, the shape of a planet's orbit. It has two special points inside, the foci; the Sun sits at one of them.
- concept: keplers-laws

### astronomical-unit
- title: Astronomical unit (AU)
- def: The Earth's average distance from the Sun, about $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m (150 million km). Used to measure distances in the solar system.
- concept: keplers-laws

### escape-velocity
- title: Escape velocity
- def: The launch speed needed to get away from a planet or star for good, with no further push: $\sqrt{2GM/R}$. About 11.2 km/s for the Earth.
- concept: escape-velocity

### shm
- title: Simple harmonic motion
- def: A steady back-and-forth swing caused by a force that always pulls toward the middle, in proportion to the distance from it. Springs and pendulums move this way.
- concept: simple-harmonic-motion

### amplitude
- title: Amplitude
- def: The biggest distance from the middle in an oscillation or wave: how big the swing, or how tall the crest, is.

### frequency
- title: Frequency
- def: How many swings, or wave crests, happen each second. Measured in hertz (Hz): 1 Hz is once per second. $f = \frac{1}{T}$.
- concept: simple-harmonic-motion

### wave
- title: Wave
- def: A travelling pattern of oscillation that carries energy from place to place, while the material it moves through stays where it is.
- concept: waves

### transverse
- title: Transverse wave
- def: A wave in which the material moves at right angles to the direction the wave travels, like ripples on water or a shaken rope.

### longitudinal
- title: Longitudinal wave
- def: A wave in which the material moves back and forth along the direction the wave travels, like sound.

### wavelength
- title: Wavelength
- def: The distance from one crest of a wave to the next. Written λ (lambda).
- concept: waves

### sound
- title: Sound
- def: A longitudinal wave of squeezed and stretched air (or another material). About 343 m/s in air.
- concept: sound

### doppler
- title: Doppler effect
- def: The change in frequency of a wave when its source moves toward you (higher) or away from you (lower).
- concept: doppler-effect

### superposition
- title: Superposition
- def: The rule that where waves overlap, they add up: the total is the sum of each wave on its own.

### interference
- title: Interference
- def: Waves adding up where they meet: in step they make a bigger wave (constructive); crest on trough they cancel (destructive).
- concept: interference

### density
- title: Density
- def: How much mass is packed into each bit of space: mass divided by volume, $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$. Water is 1000 kg/m³.
- concept: density

### pressure
- title: Pressure
- def: Force spread over an area, $P = \frac{F}{A}$. A small area makes a big pressure.
- concept: pressure

### pascal
- title: Pascal (Pa)
- def: The unit of pressure: one newton on each square metre. Air pressure is about 101,000 Pa.
- concept: pressure

### buoyancy
- title: Buoyant force (upthrust)
- def: The upward push a fluid gives anything in it, equal to the weight of the fluid pushed aside.
- concept: buoyancy

### archimedes
- title: Archimedes' principle
- def: The upthrust on an object in a fluid equals the weight of the fluid it displaces (pushes out of the way).
- concept: buoyancy

### absolute-zero
- title: Absolute zero
- def: The coldest possible temperature, −273°C or 0 K, where particles have the least energy they can.

### kelvin
- title: Kelvin (K)
- def: The temperature scale that starts at absolute zero. Its degrees are the same size as Celsius ones: K = °C + 273.
- concept: temperature

### specific-heat
- title: Specific heat capacity
- def: The energy needed to warm 1 kg of a material by 1°C. For water, about 4200 J.
- concept: specific-heat

### latent-heat
- title: Latent heat
- def: The energy needed to melt or boil 1 kg of a material without changing its temperature.

### conduction
- title: Conduction
- def: Heat passing through a material as jiggling particles bump their neighbours. Metals conduct well; still air conducts badly.
- concept: heat-transfer

### convection
- title: Convection
- def: Heat carried by a moving fluid: warm fluid rises, cool fluid sinks, setting up a current.
- concept: heat-transfer

### radiation-heat
- title: Thermal radiation
- def: Heat carried by electromagnetic waves, mostly infrared. It needs no material, so it crosses space.
- concept: heat-transfer

### mole
- title: Mole
- def: A counting unit for particles: one mole is $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ of them.

### thermodynamics
- title: Thermodynamics
- def: The study of heat, work and energy, summed up in four laws. The best known: energy is kept, and heat flows by itself only from hot to cold.
- concept: thermodynamics

### entropy
- title: Entropy
- def: A measure of how spread out energy is. Left alone, energy spreads out, and entropy goes up.

### charge
- title: Electric charge
- def: A property of matter that makes it push or pull on other charges. It comes as positive (protons) or negative (electrons); likes repel, opposites attract.
- concept: electric-charge

### coulomb
- title: Coulomb (C)
- def: The unit of charge. One coulomb is the charge of about $6.25 \times 10^{18}$ electrons.
- concept: electric-charge

### conductor
- title: Conductor
- def: A material that lets charge flow through it easily, like a metal. One that does not, like plastic, is an insulator.

### electric-field
- title: Electric field
- def: The space around a charge where other charges feel a force. Its strength E is the force per coulomb, in N/C.
- concept: electric-field

### voltage
- title: Voltage (potential difference)
- def: The energy given to or taken from each coulomb of charge moving between two points. Measured in volts.
- concept: voltage

### volt
- title: Volt (V)
- def: The unit of voltage: one joule per coulomb.
- concept: voltage

### current
- title: Electric current
- def: The flow of charge, measured as coulombs passing a point each second.
- concept: current

### ampere
- title: Ampere (A)
- def: The unit of current, often called an amp: one coulomb per second.
- concept: current

### circuit
- title: Circuit
- def: A complete loop that charge can flow round, from one side of a power supply back to the other.
- concept: circuits

### resistance
- title: Resistance
- def: How much a component opposes the flow of current: the voltage needed per amp, $R = \frac{V}{I}$.
- concept: ohms-law

### ohm
- title: Ohm (Ω)
- def: The unit of resistance: one volt per amp.
- concept: ohms-law

### magnetic-field
- title: Magnetic field
- def: The space around a magnet or a current where magnetic forces act. Its strength B is measured in teslas.
- concept: magnetic-fields

### tesla
- title: Tesla (T)
- def: The unit of magnetic field strength. The Earth's field is about 0.00005 T; a strong lab magnet is about 1 T.

### induction
- title: Electromagnetic induction
- def: Making a voltage in a wire by changing the magnetic field through it.
- concept: induction

### em-wave
- title: Electromagnetic wave
- def: A wave of linked electric and magnetic fields that travels through empty space at the speed of light. Light, radio and X-rays are all examples.
- concept: em-waves

### normal
- title: Normal
- def: An imaginary line at right angles to a surface, where a ray hits it. Angles of reflection and refraction are measured from it.
- concept: reflection

### focal-point
- title: Focal point
- def: The point where rays arriving parallel meet after a curved mirror or converging lens. Its distance from the mirror or lens is the focal length.
- concept: lenses

### refraction
- title: Refraction
- def: The bending of light (or any wave) as it crosses into a material where it travels at a different speed.
- concept: refraction

### refractive-index
- title: Refractive index
- def: How many times slower light travels in a material than in a vacuum, $n = \frac{c}{v}$. Water is 1.33; glass about 1.5.
- concept: refraction

### total-internal-reflection
- title: Total internal reflection
- def: When light inside a slower material hits the surface beyond the critical angle, none escapes; it all reflects back inside. Optical fibres use it.
- concept: refraction

### diffraction
- title: Diffraction
- def: The spreading of waves through a gap or round an edge, strongest when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength.
- concept: diffraction

### photon
- title: Photon
- def: A single packet of light energy, $E = hf$. Light travels as a wave but gives up its energy one photon at a time.
- concept: photons

### energy-level
- title: Energy level
- def: One of the fixed energies an electron in an atom is allowed to have, like a rung on a ladder.
- concept: energy-levels

### spectrum
- title: Spectrum
- def: Light spread out into its separate wavelengths, like a rainbow. Its pattern of bright or dark lines reveals which elements are present.
- concept: spectra

### blackbody
- title: Blackbody
- def: An ideal object that absorbs all light falling on it, so it glows only because of its temperature. Stars are close to blackbodies.
- concept: blackbody-radiation

### time-dilation
- title: Time dilation
- def: The slowing of a moving clock, as seen by someone it moves past. It only becomes noticeable near the speed of light.
- concept: special-relativity

### isotope
- title: Isotope
- def: A version of an element with a particular number of neutrons. Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.
- concept: nuclear-physics

### binding-energy
- title: Binding energy
- def: The energy holding a nucleus together: the energy released when it formed, equal to its mass defect times $c^2$.
- concept: nuclear-physics

### radioactivity
- title: Radioactive decay
- def: An unstable nucleus breaking down by itself, giving out alpha, beta or gamma radiation.
- concept: radioactive-decay

### half-life
- title: Half-life
- def: The time for half the nuclei in a radioactive sample to decay. It is fixed for each isotope.
- concept: radioactive-decay

### fission
- title: Fission
- def: Splitting a heavy nucleus, such as uranium, into two smaller ones, releasing energy.
- concept: fusion-fission

### fusion
- title: Fusion
- def: Joining light nuclei, such as hydrogen, into a heavier one, releasing energy. It powers the Sun.
- concept: fusion-fission

## Footnotes

### n_two_roots
- title: Two square roots
- def: Both $7 \times 7$ and $(-7) \times (-7)$ give 49, so $x^2 = 49$ has two answers, 7 and −7. Math writes this as $x = \pm 7$. In physics the context usually rules one out, such as a length or a speed, which cannot be negative.

### n_e_notation
- title: E on a calculator
- def: The E stands for "times ten to the". 3.2E-5 means $3.2 \times 10^{-5}$. It is not the number e used in exponential functions.

### n_exact_numbers
- title: Exact numbers
- def: A counted number (5 students) or a defined one (1 km = 1000 m exactly) has no measuring error. Treat it as having as many significant figures as you need.

### n_other_si
- title: The other two base units
- def: The mole (mol) counts amounts of a substance, mostly used in chemistry. The candela (cd) measures how bright a light looks. That makes seven SI base units in all.

### n_mars_orbiter
- title: The lost Mars probe
- def: NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter, 1999. Software from one team reported thruster pushes in pound-force seconds; the navigation team expected newton-seconds. The probe flew too close to Mars and was lost.

### n_complex
- title: Numbers beyond the real ones
- def: Mathematicians invented "imaginary" numbers, with $i^2 = -1$, so that every quadratic has answers. They matter in electronics and quantum physics, but no measured length or time is imaginary, so this web does not need them.

### n_no_solution
- title: Parallel lines
- def: Each equation in two unknowns is a straight line on a graph, and the solution is where the lines cross. Parallel lines never cross, so there is no solution. If the two equations are really the same line, every point on it works.

### n_3d_pythagoras
- title: Three directions
- def: In space, add a third direction: the distance is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$. It comes from using the theorem twice.

### n_arc_names
- title: Why "arc"
- def: On a circle of radius 1, an angle in radians equals the length of the arc it cuts off. So arcsin(0.5) means "the arc whose sine is 0.5".

### n_inverse_range
- title: Which angle the calculator gives
- def: Many angles share a sine: $\sin 30° = \sin 150° = 0.5$. So $\sin^{-1}$ always gives the one between −90° and 90°, $\cos^{-1}$ the one between 0° and 180°, and $\tan^{-1}$ the one between −90° and 90°. In a right triangle the angle is always between 0° and 90°, so the calculator's answer is the one you want.

### n_area_integral
- title: Area and integrals
- def: Adding up thinner and thinner strips is exactly what an integral does. In calculus terms, displacement is the integral of velocity over time. The Calculus web explains this on its definite integral page.

### n_suvat
- title: "suvat"
- def: Many textbooks call these the "suvat" equations, after the five letters s, u, v, a and t. Some books write $v_0$ or $v_i$ for the start velocity and $\Delta x$ or d for the displacement.

### n_g_varies
- title: g is not quite the same everywhere
- def: g is about 9.78 m/s² at the equator and 9.83 m/s² at the poles, and a little smaller on high mountains. 9.8 m/s² is a good average. On the Moon it is about 1.6 m/s².

### n_galilean
- title: When adding velocities fails
- def: Adding velocities works for everything in daily life. Near the speed of light it breaks down: light moves at the same speed for every observer. The page on special relativity explains what replaces it.

### n_air_resistance
- title: Real balls and air
- def: Air pushes back on a moving ball, so real throws fall short of these numbers, and the best angle is usually a bit below 45°. For a heavy, slow object over a short distance, the effect is small.

### n_galileo_newton
- title: Who worked it out
- def: For nearly 2000 years people followed Aristotle, who taught that moving things need a constant push. Galileo, in the early 1600s, rolled balls on ramps and argued that motion continues unless something stops it. Isaac Newton made it his first law in 1687.

### n_scales
- title: What bathroom scales measure
- def: A bathroom scale measures how hard you press on it, a force, then divides by 9.8 to show kilograms. Take it to the Moon and it would show about a sixth of your mass, which is wrong. A balance that compares you against known masses would still be right.

### n_centripetal_proof
- title: Where v²/r comes from
- def: In a short time t, the object goes a distance $vt$ around the circle, so its direction turns through an angle $\frac{vt}{r}$ in radians. The velocity arrow, length v, turns by that angle, so it changes by about $v \times \frac{vt}{r}$. Divide by t to get the acceleration: $\frac{v^2}{r}$.

### n_hooke_sign
- title: The minus sign in Hooke's law
- def: $F = kx$ gives the size of the force. Writing $F = -kx$ also gives its direction: if x is a stretch to the right (positive), the spring pulls to the left (negative).

### n_conservative
- title: Forces where the path does not matter
- def: Gravity and springs are called conservative forces: the work they do depends only on where you start and finish, not the route. Friction is not like this. A longer route means more friction and more work against it.

### n_joule_heat
- title: Heat is energy
- def: In the 1840s James Joule let a falling weight turn a paddle in water and measured the water warming. The same amount of work always gave the same warming. That showed heat is a form of energy, and the unit of energy is named after him.

### n_newton_momentum
- title: Newton's own version
- def: Newton wrote his second law as "the change of motion is proportional to the force", where by "motion" he meant mass times velocity. $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ also works when the mass changes, as for a rocket burning fuel; $F = ma$ assumes a fixed mass.

### n_elastic_solve
- title: Solving the elastic collision
- def: Momentum: $m_1u = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$. Energy: $\tfrac{1}{2}m_1u^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2v_2^2$. Rearranging and dividing one by the other gives $u + v_1 = v_2$, a simple straight-line equation. Putting that into the momentum equation gives the two formulas.

### n_torque_unit
- title: N·m is not joules here
- def: Torque and work are both a force times a distance, so both come out in N·m. For work, the force is along the distance; for torque, it is at right angles. To avoid mixing them up, torque is always written N·m, never J.

### n_moon_test
- title: Newton's Moon test
- def: Newton worked out the Moon's acceleration from its distance and its 27.3-day orbit, and found about 0.0027 m/s². That is 9.8 divided by about 3600, just as an inverse-square law predicts at 60 Earth radii. The match convinced him that the same gravity reaches the Moon.

### n_cavendish
- title: Measuring G
- def: Henry Cavendish first measured the pull between lead balls in a lab in 1798, using a very fine twisting wire. Once G was known, $g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$ gave the Earth's mass. He was said to have "weighed the Earth".

### n_disc_inertia
- title: Where ½MR² comes from
- def: Cut a disc into thin rings. Each ring has all its mass at its own radius, so contributes mass × radius². Adding up all the rings, with an integral, gives $\tfrac{1}{2}MR^2$. The inner rings count for little, which is why it is less than a ring's $MR^2$.

### n_kepler_history
- title: Kepler and Brahe
- def: Tycho Brahe made the most accurate naked-eye measurements of the planets ever taken, before telescopes. Kepler spent about eight years fitting circles to Mars's path before trying an ellipse, which fitted at once. He published the first two laws in 1609 and the third in 1619.

### n_equal_areas
- title: Why equal areas
- def: The Sun's pull always points straight at the Sun, so it gives no torque about the Sun. The planet's angular momentum is therefore constant, and equal areas in equal times is exactly what constant angular momentum looks like.

### n_pe_far
- title: Where −GMm/r comes from
- def: The work done lifting a mass from r out to infinity is the integral of the force, $\int_r^\infty \frac{GMm}{x^2}\,dx = \frac{GMm}{r}$. Taking zero at infinity, the PE at r is minus that amount. Close to the ground, this gives back $mgh$ for small heights.

### n_pendulum_wide
- title: Wide swings
- def: The formula uses the fact that $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ for small angles in radians. At 15° the true period is only about 0.4% longer than the formula gives. At 60° it is about 7% longer.

### n_sound_temp
- title: Sound and temperature
- def: Sound is faster in warm air, because the molecules move faster and pass the push along sooner. A handy rule: about 331 m/s at 0°C, plus 0.6 m/s for every degree warmer.

### n_archimedes_crown
- title: Archimedes and the crown
- def: The story goes that a king asked Archimedes whether a crown was pure gold, without damaging it. Stepping into a bath, he saw the water rise and realised he could measure the crown's volume that way, then compare its density with gold's. He is said to have run home shouting "Eureka!" ("I have found it!").

### n_temp_ke
- title: Temperature and energy exactly
- def: For a simple gas, the average kinetic energy of each molecule is $\tfrac{3}{2}kT$, where T is in kelvin and $k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}$ J/K is Boltzmann's constant. So doubling the kelvin temperature doubles the average kinetic energy.

### n_conduction_rate
- title: The conduction formula
- def: The rate heat flows through a slab is $\frac{kA\Delta T}{d}$, where A is the area, ΔT the temperature difference across it, d the thickness, and k the material's thermal conductivity. Copper's k is about 400; still air's is about 0.025, 16,000 times smaller.

### n_entropy
- title: Why entropy goes up
- def: There are vastly more ways for energy to be spread out among many particles than bunched up in a few. Random jostling therefore almost always spreads it out. Going the other way is not forbidden, just so unlikely that it never happens for anything big enough to see.

### n_neutral
- title: How neutral is matter?
- def: Extremely. If your body had just 1% more electrons than protons, the push between you and a person standing next to you would be strong enough to lift the whole Earth.

### n_drift
- title: How slow is the drift?
- def: In a copper wire 1 mm across carrying 1 A, the electrons drift at only about 0.1 mm per second. At that rate, an electron takes about a quarter of an hour to move 10 cm. In AC mains, they just jiggle back and forth and get nowhere at all.

### n_resistivity
- title: Resistivity values
- def: Resistivity is in ohm-metres (Ω·m). Copper is about $1.7 \times 10^{-8}$, nichrome about $1.1 \times 10^{-6}$ (about 65 times copper's), and glass about $10^{12}$, an insulator.

### n_earth_field
- title: The Earth's moving poles
- def: The Earth's field comes from currents of molten iron in its outer core. The magnetic poles drift slowly, by tens of kilometres a year, and every few hundred thousand years the whole field flips. Rocks record these flips, which helped prove that the sea floor spreads.

### n_flemings
- title: Fleming's left-hand rule
- def: Many school courses give the direction with Fleming's left-hand rule instead: hold your thumb, first finger and second finger at right angles. First finger points along the Field, second finger along the Current, and the thuMb gives the Motion (force). It gives the same answer.

### n_lenz
- title: Lenz's law as energy conservation
- def: If induced currents helped the change instead of fighting it, a magnet nudged toward a coil would be pulled in faster and faster, making ever more current from nothing. Lenz's law is what stops that: it is conservation of energy applied to induction.

### n_maxwell
- title: Maxwell's equations
- def: In 1865, Maxwell summed up all of electricity and magnetism in a few equations, now called Maxwell's equations. The prediction of electromagnetic waves came straight out of them. Heinrich Hertz made and detected radio waves in a lab in 1887, proving him right.

### n_fermat
- title: The quickest path
- def: Light crossing from air to water bends so that it takes the quickest route between two points, like a lifeguard who runs further along the beach before swimming, because running is faster. This is Fermat's principle, and Snell's law follows from it.

### n_bohr
- title: Beyond Bohr's model
- def: Bohr pictured electrons circling the nucleus like tiny planets. The modern picture, quantum mechanics, replaces orbits with clouds of probability, but it keeps Bohr's key idea of fixed energy levels and gives the same hydrogen energies.

### n_planck
- title: Planck's reluctant idea
- def: Planck saw his energy packets as a mathematical trick, and did not believe light was really made of particles. It was Einstein, five years later, who took the packets seriously to explain the photoelectric effect.

### n_light_clock
- title: Where γ comes from
- def: In the light clock, let the mirrors be a distance L apart. At rest, a tick takes $t_0 = \frac{L}{c}$. Moving sideways at v, the light travels a slanted path: in time t, it goes $ct$ while the mirror moves $vt$, so by Pythagoras $(ct)^2 = L^2 + (vt)^2$. Solving gives $t = \frac{t_0}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}} = \gamma t_0$.

### n_einstein_field
- title: Einstein's equations
- def: General relativity is summed up in Einstein's field equations, which say how mass and energy curve spacetime. They are ten linked equations, hard to solve except in special cases, such as a single round star or black hole.

### n_decay_rate
- title: Why decay is exponential
- def: Each nucleus has the same small chance λ of decaying each second, so the number decaying per second is λN. A rate of change proportional to the amount present, $\frac{dN}{dt} = -\lambda N$, always gives an exponential curve.
