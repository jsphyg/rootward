## The family of the Sun

| Planet | Distance (AU) | Year | Diameter (Earth = 1) | Mass (Earth = 1) | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 0.39 | 88 days | 0.38 | 0.06 | rocky |
| Venus | 0.72 | 225 days | 0.95 | 0.82 | rocky |
| Earth | 1.00 | 1 year | 1 | 1 | rocky |
| Mars | 1.52 | 1.9 years | 0.53 | 0.11 | rocky |
| Jupiter | 5.20 | 11.9 years | 11.2 | 318 | gas giant |
| Saturn | 9.54 | 29.5 years | 9.4 | 95 | gas giant |
| Uranus | 19.2 | 84 years | 4.0 | 15 | ice giant |
| Neptune | 30.1 | 165 years | 3.9 | 17 | ice giant |

*Notice: there is a big jump after Mars. The inner four are small and rocky; the outer four are giants, much further apart.*

All of them obey [[keplers-laws|Kepler's laws]]: each moves on a nearly circular ellipse, faster when closer, and the years follow $T^2 = a^3$.

## Two kinds of planet

**Rocky (terrestrial) planets** are made mostly of rock and metal, with solid surfaces. Venus has a thick, crushing atmosphere of carbon dioxide that traps heat, making it the hottest planet. Mars has a thin atmosphere, ice caps and signs of ancient rivers.

**Giant planets** have no solid surface you could stand on. Jupiter and Saturn are mostly hydrogen and helium; Saturn's average density is less than water's. Uranus and Neptune have more water, ammonia and methane "ices". All four have ring systems and many moons; some moons, like Jupiter's Europa and Saturn's Enceladus, hide oceans beneath icy crusts.

The next page explains why the two kinds formed where they did.

## Smaller bodies

- **Dwarf planets**, like Pluto and Ceres, are round but have not cleared their orbits of other objects.
- **Asteroids**: rocky leftovers, mostly in the belt between Mars and Jupiter.
- **Kuiper belt**: icy bodies beyond Neptune, including Pluto.
- **Comets**: icy bodies on long, stretched orbits. Near the Sun, their ice turns to gas and makes a glowing tail.

## A sense of scale

Sizes are tiny compared with distances. Shrink the Sun to a 1 m ball: the Earth is a 9 mm marble 107 m away, Jupiter a 10 cm ball over half a kilometre away, and Neptune a 3.5 cm ball over 3 km away. Almost all of the solar system is empty space.

:::example
title: Using Kepler's third law, find Neptune's year. How long does sunlight take to reach it? (Light takes 499 s to cross 1 AU.)

1. $T^2 = a^3 = 30.1^3 \approx 27{,}300$.
   why: Kepler's third law in years and AU.
   uses: [keplers-laws]

2. $T = \sqrt{27{,}300} \approx 165$ years.
   why: Take the square root.
   uses: []

3. Light time: $30.1 \times 499 \approx 15{,}000$ s.
   why: 30.1 AU, at 499 s per AU.
   uses: []

4. **About 165 years; sunlight takes about 4.2 hours to get there.** Neptune has gone round the Sun only once since it was discovered in 1846.
   why: $15{,}000 \div 3600 \approx 4.2$ hours.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: Saturn's average density is 690 kg/m³. Would it float in a big enough bath of water?
answer: **Yes**: it is less dense than water (1000 kg/m³).
:::

:::problems
q: Name the four rocky planets and the four giants.
answer: **Rocky: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Giants: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.**

q: Which is the largest planet, and how many Earths of mass is it?
answer: **Jupiter, about 318 Earth masses.**

q: Find Neptune's year from Kepler's third law.
answer: **About 165 years.**

q: Uranus is 19.2 AU from the Sun. Find its year.
answer: $\sqrt{19.2^3} \approx$ **84 years**.

q: How long does sunlight take to reach Jupiter, at 5.2 AU?
answer: $5.2 \times 499 \approx 2600$ s, about **43 minutes**.

q: Where is the asteroid belt?
answer: **Between Mars and Jupiter.**

q: Why is Venus hotter than Mercury, though it is further from the Sun?
answer: **Its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat** (a runaway greenhouse effect).

q: Why is Pluto called a dwarf planet?
answer: **It has not cleared its orbit of other bodies**; it shares the Kuiper belt with many other icy objects.
:::
