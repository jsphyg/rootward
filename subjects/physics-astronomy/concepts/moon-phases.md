## Half lit, always

Shine a lamp on a ball in a dark room. The half facing the lamp is lit; the other half is dark. Now walk around the ball, and you see different amounts of the lit half. That is all the Moon's phases are.

The Moon gives off no light of its own. The Sun always lights the half of the Moon facing it. As the Moon orbits the Earth over about a month, we look at that lit half from different angles.

| Where the Moon is | We see | Phase | Up in the sky |
|---|---|---|---|
| between the Earth and Sun | the dark side | new moon | in daytime, invisible |
| a quarter of the way round | half the disc lit | first quarter | afternoon and evening |
| opposite the Sun | the whole lit side | full moon | all night, rising at sunset |
| three-quarters round | the other half lit | last quarter | late night and morning |

*Notice: between new and full, the lit part grows (waxing); between full and new, it shrinks (waning). A "quarter" moon looks half lit: the name means a quarter of the way through the cycle.*

Between these, there are thin **crescents** (less than half lit) and fat **gibbous** moons (more than half).

## Not the Earth's shadow

A common mistake is that the dark part of the Moon is the Earth's shadow. At full moon, the Moon is on the far side of the Earth from the Sun, but slightly above or below the Earth's shadow, so it is fully lit. Only on the rare occasions it passes through the shadow is there an eclipse.

## 29.5 days, not 27.3

The Moon orbits the Earth once, measured against the stars, in **27.3 days**. But in that time the Earth has moved about a month's worth along its orbit, so the Sun is in a different direction. The Moon needs about 2 more days to line up with the Sun again. So the cycle of phases, new moon to new moon, takes **29.5 days**. This is the origin of the "month".

## The same face

The Moon turns on its own axis exactly once per orbit. So it always keeps the same face toward the Earth, and we never see its far side from here. This is called **tidal locking**, explained on the tides page.

:::practice
q: About how many days pass from new moon to full moon?
answer: Half the cycle: **about 15 days** (14.8).
:::

:::problems
q: What causes the Moon's phases?
answer: **How much of its sunlit half we see**, as it orbits the Earth.

q: Why can't we see a new moon?
answer: **Its lit side faces away from us**, and it is in the sky with the Sun.

q: When does a full moon rise?
answer: **Around sunset.**

q: A "first quarter" moon looks half lit. Why is it called a quarter?
answer: **It is a quarter of the way through the cycle**, a quarter of the way round its orbit.

q: What is the difference between waxing and waning?
answer: **Waxing: the lit part is growing** (new to full). **Waning: it is shrinking** (full to new).

q: Why is the cycle of phases 29.5 days, not 27.3?
answer: **The Earth moves along its orbit during the month**, so the Moon needs about 2 extra days to line up with the Sun again.

q: Why do we always see the same face of the Moon?
answer: **It turns once on its axis in exactly the time it takes to orbit once.**

q: Is the dark part of a crescent moon in the Earth's shadow?
answer: **No.** It is just the Moon's own night side, facing away from the Sun.
:::
