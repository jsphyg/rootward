## One family of waves

Light is an [[em-waves|electromagnetic wave]]. So are the radio waves carrying music, the microwaves heating your food, and the X-rays showing your bones. They are all the same thing, travelling at the same speed $c$. The only difference is the wavelength, and so the frequency.

| Type | Wavelength, about | Made by, or used for |
|---|---|---|
| radio | 1 m to many km | broadcasting, phones, Wi-Fi |
| microwave | 1 mm to 1 m | microwave ovens, radar, satellite links |
| infrared | 700 nm to 1 mm | warm objects; remote controls, thermal cameras |
| visible light | 400 nm to 700 nm | the Sun, lamps; our eyes |
| ultraviolet | 10 nm to 400 nm | the Sun; sunburn, sterilising |
| X-rays | 0.01 nm to 10 nm | X-ray tubes; seeing bones |
| gamma rays | under 0.01 nm | radioactive atoms, exploding stars; cancer treatment |

*Notice: visible light is a tiny slice in the middle. Our eyes are blind to almost all of the spectrum.*

## Wavelength and frequency

All of these travel at $c = 3 \times 10^8$ m/s in a vacuum, and $c = f\lambda$. So shorter wavelength always means higher frequency:

$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda}$$

Within visible light, the colours run from red (longest, about 700 nm) through orange, yellow, green and blue to violet (shortest, about 400 nm). White light is all the colours mixed.

## Energy and danger

Light comes in tiny packets of energy called photons, and each photon carries an energy that grows with its frequency (the photon page shows how). So:

- Radio and microwave photons carry very little energy each.
- Ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma photons carry enough to break molecules apart, including the DNA in cells. This is called **ionising radiation**. It causes sunburn and can cause cancer, which is why X-ray staff stand behind shields.

Microwaves can still heat food, because a strong oven sends a huge number of photons that make water molecules jiggle.

## Seeing the whole sky

Different objects shine in different parts of the spectrum. Cool dust clouds glow in infrared. Very hot gas around black holes gives out X-rays. The leftover glow of the Big Bang is in microwaves. So astronomers build telescopes for every part. The atmosphere blocks most infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays, so many of those telescopes have to be put in space.

:::example
title: Put these in order from longest to shortest wavelength, and name each: a wave of $1 \times 10^9$ Hz, one of $5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz, and one of $3 \times 10^{18}$ Hz.

1. $\lambda = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{1 \times 10^9} = 0.3$ m: **microwave**.
   why: $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$; 0.3 m falls between 1 mm and 1 m.
   uses: [em-waves]

2. $\lambda = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{5 \times 10^{14}} = 6 \times 10^{-7}$ m $= 600$ nm: **visible light** (orange).
   why: 600 nm is between 400 and 700 nm.
   uses: []

3. $\lambda = \dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^{18}} = 1 \times 10^{-10}$ m $= 0.1$ nm: **X-ray**.
   why: 0.1 nm is between 0.01 and 10 nm.
   uses: []

4. **Microwave (0.3 m), then visible (600 nm), then X-ray (0.1 nm).** Higher frequency, shorter wavelength.
   why: The order of frequencies is the reverse of the order of wavelengths.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: A microwave oven uses 2.45 GHz. Find the wavelength.
answer: $\dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{2.45 \times 10^9} \approx$ **0.12 m** (12 cm).
:::

:::problems
q: Which has the longest wavelength: X-rays, radio or ultraviolet?
answer: **Radio.**

q: Which has the highest frequency: infrared, gamma rays or microwaves?
answer: **Gamma rays.**

q: Find the frequency of red light, 700 nm.
answer: $\dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{7 \times 10^{-7}} \approx$ **$4.3 \times 10^{14}$ Hz**.

q: Find the frequency of violet light, 400 nm.
answer: **$7.5 \times 10^{14}$ Hz.**

q: A wave has a wavelength of 1 mm. Is it infrared or microwave?
answer: **It is right at the border** between the two.

q: A wave has a frequency of $3 \times 10^{16}$ Hz. Find its wavelength and name it.
answer: $\dfrac{3 \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^{16}} = 10^{-8}$ m $=$ **10 nm**, at the border of **ultraviolet and X-rays**.

q: Why does ultraviolet cause sunburn but visible light does not?
answer: **Each ultraviolet photon carries more energy**, enough to damage skin cells.

q: Which three types of radiation are ionising?
answer: **Ultraviolet (the shorter part), X-rays and gamma rays.**

q: How does a thermal camera see people in the dark?
answer: **Warm bodies give out infrared**, which the camera detects.

q: Why are X-ray and gamma-ray telescopes put in space?
answer: **The atmosphere absorbs those wavelengths**, so they never reach the ground.

q: Do X-rays travel faster than radio waves in space?
answer: **No.** All electromagnetic waves travel at c.

q: Name the parts of the spectrum in order, from longest wavelength to shortest.
answer: **Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.**
:::
