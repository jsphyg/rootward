## Two skaters

Two people stand face to face on ice skates and one pushes the other. **Both** roll backward, away from each other. It does not matter who did the pushing.

| Who pushes | What happens |
|---|---|
| A pushes B | both move apart |
| B pushes A | both move apart |
| both push | both move apart |

*Notice: you cannot push someone without being pushed back. The push goes both ways at once.*

## The law

**Newton's third law:** if object A exerts a force on object B, then B exerts a force on A that is

- the same size,
- in the opposite direction,
- and the same kind of force (a contact push with a contact push, gravity with gravity).

The two forces are called a {{force-pair|third-law pair}}. They happen at the same instant. Neither one comes first.

## Pairs act on different objects

This is the part that trips people up. The two forces in a pair act on **different** objects. One acts on A, the other on B. So they never cancel each other, because cancelling only happens between forces on the **same** object.

That answers an old puzzle. If a horse pulls a cart and the cart pulls back on the horse equally, how does anything move? Look at the horse alone: the cart pulls it back, but the ground pushes its hooves forward harder. The net force on the horse is forward, so it accelerates. The cart's pull on the horse does not cancel the horse's pull on the cart, because they act on different things.

## Pairs in action

| Action | Pair force that moves you |
|---|---|
| walking: your foot pushes the ground backward | the ground pushes your foot forward |
| swimming: your hands push water backward | the water pushes you forward |
| a rocket pushes hot gas downward | the gas pushes the rocket upward |
| a gun pushes a bullet forward | the bullet pushes the gun backward (the kick) |

A rocket does not need air to push against. It pushes on its own exhaust gas, and the gas pushes back.

## Not every equal and opposite pair is a third-law pair

A book rests on a table. Its weight (down) and the table's push (up) are equal and opposite, but they are **not** a third-law pair. Both act on the **book**, and they are different kinds of force. They are equal because the book is not accelerating, which is the first law.

The real pairs are:

- Earth pulls the book down; the book pulls the Earth up, just as hard.
- The table pushes the book up; the book pushes the table down, just as hard.

**Why the Earth does not move toward the book.** It does feel the same force, but its mass is so enormous that the acceleration is far too small to notice.

:::example
title: A 60 kg skater pushes a 30 kg skater with a force of 120 N. What force acts on the 60 kg skater, and which skater speeds up more?

1. The 30 kg skater feels 120 N, away from the pusher.
   why: That is the push that was given.
   uses: [force]

2. By the third law, the 60 kg skater feels **120 N** in the opposite direction.
   why: Every force has an equal and opposite partner acting on the other object.
   uses: []

3. Both feel the same size of force, but the 30 kg skater has half the mass.
   why: The same push changes the motion of a lighter object more, because it has less inertia.
   uses: [newtons-first-law]

4. So **the 30 kg skater speeds up more** (twice as much), even though the forces are equal.
   why: Equal forces do not mean equal effects when the masses differ.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: You push on a wall with a force of 50 N. What force does the wall exert on you?
answer: **50 N, pushing back toward you.** It is the third-law partner of your push.
:::

:::problems
q: You push a wall with 50 N. What force does the wall push back on you with?
answer: **50 N, in the opposite direction.**

q: A truck hits a small car. Which feels the bigger force?
answer: **Neither. The forces are equal in size.** The car is damaged more because it has less mass.

q: When you walk, what pushes you forward?
answer: **The ground.** Your foot pushes the ground backward, and the ground pushes your foot forward.

q: How does a rocket accelerate in empty space with no air?
answer: **It pushes exhaust gas backward, and the gas pushes the rocket forward.**

q: Why does a gun kick back when it fires?
answer: **The gun pushes the bullet forward, so the bullet pushes the gun backward with an equal force.**

q: A book rests on a table. Are its weight and the table's upward push a third-law pair?
answer: **No.** Both act on the book, and they are different kinds of force. They are equal because the book is not accelerating.

q: What is the third-law partner of the Earth's gravity pulling on an apple?
answer: **The apple's gravity pulling the Earth upward**, with the same size of force.

q: A swimmer pushes water backward with 80 N. What force moves the swimmer forward?
answer: **80 N, from the water.**

q: If a horse and cart pull on each other equally, how can they move forward?
answer: The two pulls act on different objects, so they do not cancel. **The ground pushes the horse forward harder than the cart pulls it back.**

q: A 60 kg skater pushes a 30 kg skater with 120 N. Which one speeds up more?
answer: Both feel 120 N, but **the 30 kg skater speeds up more**, because it has less mass.
:::
