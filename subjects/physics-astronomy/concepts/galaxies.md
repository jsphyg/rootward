## Island universes

On a dark autumn night, a faint smudge can be seen with the naked eye in the constellation Andromeda. For centuries, such fuzzy "nebulae" were a puzzle. Were they clouds of gas inside our galaxy, or whole other {{galaxy|galaxies}}, "island universes", far away?

In 1920, astronomers held a public "Great Debate" on the question, and nobody could settle it. The answer needed a distance.

In 1924, Edwin Hubble found pulsing stars called **Cepheids** in Andromeda. Henrietta Leavitt had shown in 1912 that a Cepheid's pulse rate gives its luminosity. That makes them [[brightness-distance|standard candles]]: compare true luminosity with how bright the star looks, and you have its distance. Andromeda came out at about 2.5 million light-years, far outside the Milky Way, which is only 100,000 across. The universe had suddenly grown enormously.

## Galaxy shapes

| Type | Shape | Stars and gas | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| spiral | a flat, spinning disc with arms around a bulge; some have a bar across the middle | young blue stars in the arms, plenty of gas, still forming stars | the Milky Way, Andromeda |
| elliptical | a smooth round or oval ball, no disc | mostly old red stars, little gas, few new stars | M87, a giant in the Virgo cluster |
| irregular | no clear shape, often disturbed by a neighbour | lots of gas, often bursting with new stars | the Large Magellanic Cloud |

Sizes range from dwarf galaxies of a few million stars to giant ellipticals of trillions. Every large galaxy studied so far seems to have a supermassive [[black-holes|black hole]] at its centre. When gas pours onto one, it can blaze brighter than all the galaxy's stars together: a **quasar**.

## Groups, clusters and collisions

Galaxies are not scattered evenly. They gather together.

| Grouping | Size | Example |
|---|---|---|
| group | a few to dozens of galaxies, a few million light-years across | the Local Group: the Milky Way, Andromeda, Triangulum and dozens of dwarfs |
| cluster | hundreds to thousands of galaxies | the Virgo cluster, about 54 million light-years away |
| supercluster | many clusters and groups linked in long filaments | the one containing the Local Group and the Virgo cluster |

Between the filaments lie vast, nearly empty voids. On the largest scale, the universe looks like a foam or a web.

Galaxies in a group pull on each other and sometimes collide. The stars are so far apart that they almost never hit each other, but the gas clouds crash, setting off bursts of star formation, and the galaxies end up merged. Andromeda is heading toward us and will merge with the Milky Way in about 4 to 5 billion years, probably forming a giant elliptical.

## Looking back in time

Light from Andromeda left it 2.5 million years ago. We see the galaxy as it was then. The further away we look, the further back in time we see. The most distant galaxies seen, by the James Webb Space Telescope, are seen as they were only a few hundred million years after the universe began.

:::practice
q: A galaxy 50 million light-years away is 100,000 light-years across. What angle does it span, in degrees?
answer: $\dfrac{100{,}000}{50{,}000{,}000} = 0.002$ rad, $\times \dfrac{180}{\pi} \approx$ **0.11°**, about 7 arcminutes.
:::

:::problems
q: Why did finding Andromeda's distance settle the Great Debate?
answer: **2.5 million light-years is far outside the Milky Way**, so it had to be a separate galaxy.

q: What makes Cepheids useful for distances?
answer: **Their pulse rate gives their luminosity**, so they are standard candles.

q: A galaxy has a smooth oval shape, mostly old red stars and little gas. What type is it?
answer: **Elliptical.**

q: Why do spiral galaxies look bluer than ellipticals?
answer: **They are still forming stars**, including short-lived hot blue ones; ellipticals have mostly old red stars.

q: A galaxy 10 million light-years away spans 10 arcminutes. How wide is it?
answer: $10' = 0.0029$ rad, so **about 29,000 light-years**.

q: Andromeda's lines are blueshifted by 300 km/s. Where does the 656.3 nm hydrogen line appear?
answer: $656.3 \times \left(1 - \dfrac{300}{300{,}000}\right) \approx$ **655.6 nm**.

q: Andromeda approaches the Milky Way's centre at about 110 km/s and is 2.5 million light-years away. At that speed, how long until they meet?
answer: $\dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{22} \text{ m}}{1.1 \times 10^5 \text{ m/s}} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{17}$ s, **about 7 billion years**. Gravity will speed it up, so the real answer is nearer 4 to 5 billion.

q: When two galaxies collide, why do their stars almost never hit each other?
answer: **The stars are tiny compared with the distances between them.**

q: We see a galaxy 54 million light-years away. When did its light leave?
answer: **54 million years ago.**

q: What is the Local Group?
answer: **Our group of galaxies**: the Milky Way, Andromeda, Triangulum and dozens of dwarfs.
:::
