## Same work, different speed

Two people carry the same box up the same stairs. One walks; the other runs. They do the same [[work|work]], because the force and height are the same. But the runner does it faster. **Power** measures how fast.

| Person | Work | Time | Power |
|---|---|---|---|
| walks up | 2400 J | 12 s | 200 W |
| runs up | 2400 J | 4 s | 600 W |

*Notice: the same work in a third of the time means three times the power.*

## The formula

$$P = \frac{W}{t}$$

The unit is the {{watt|watt}}, W. One watt is one joule every second: $1\text{ W} = 1\text{ J/s}$.

Power applies to any energy transfer, not just mechanical work. A 60 W lamp turns 60 J of electrical energy into light and heat every second. A 2000 W kettle uses 2000 J every second.

| Thing | Power, about |
|---|---|
| phone charger | 10 W |
| person climbing stairs | 300 to 500 W |
| kettle | 2000 W (2 kW) |
| family car | 100,000 W (100 kW) |

Car engines are often rated in horsepower: 1 horsepower is about 746 W.

## Power and speed

If a force F pushes something steadily along at speed v, then in a time t it moves a distance $vt$. The work is $F \times vt$, so the power is

$$P = Fv$$

A car at a steady speed on a flat road pushes against air resistance and friction. The faster it goes, the more power it needs, and air resistance itself also grows with speed.

## The kilowatt-hour

Electricity bills count energy in {{kwh|kilowatt-hours}}. One kWh is the energy used by 1000 W running for one hour:

$$1\text{ kWh} = 1000 \text{ W} \times 3600 \text{ s} = 3{,}600{,}000 \text{ J}$$

:::example
title: A 60 kg person runs up stairs 4 m high in 5 s. What power do they develop?

1. Force needed: their weight, $60 \times 9.8 = 588$ N.
   why: Climbing at a steady pace means lifting your weight.
   uses: [work]

2. Work done: $588 \times 4 = 2352$ J.
   why: Force times distance along the force (upward).
   uses: []

3. Power: $\dfrac{2352}{5} \approx 470$ W.
   why: Work divided by time.
   uses: []

4. **About 470 W**, a bit over half a horsepower, but only for a few seconds.
   why: Comparing to 746 W gives a sense of scale.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: A 100 W lamp is left on for one minute. How much energy does it use?
answer: $W = Pt = 100 \times 60 =$ **6000 J**.
:::

:::problems
q: A machine does 500 J of work in 10 s. Find its power.
answer: $\dfrac{500}{10} =$ **50 W**.

q: A 1500 W kettle runs for 2 minutes. How much energy does it use?
answer: $1500 \times 120 =$ **180,000 J**.

q: A 70 kg climber gains 10 m of height in 20 s. Find the power.
answer: $70 \times 9.8 \times 10 = 6860$ J. $\dfrac{6860}{20} =$ **343 W**.

q: A crane lifts 500 kg by 20 m in 10 s. Find its power.
answer: $500 \times 9.8 \times 20 = 98{,}000$ J. $\dfrac{98{,}000}{10} =$ **9800 W** (9.8 kW).

q: A rope pulls a sled with 200 N at a steady 3 m/s. Find the power.
answer: $P = Fv = 200 \times 3 =$ **600 W**.

q: A car's engine gives 30 kW to keep it at a steady speed against 1500 N of resistance. How fast is it going?
answer: $v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{30{,}000}{1500} =$ **20 m/s**.

q: A cyclist puts out 250 W at a steady 10 m/s. How big is the resistance force?
answer: $F = \dfrac{250}{10} =$ **25 N**.

q: A 50 W motor lifts a 10 kg load. How fast can it raise it at a steady speed?
answer: $v = \dfrac{P}{F} = \dfrac{50}{98} \approx$ **0.51 m/s**.

q: How many joules are in 1 kWh?
answer: $1000 \times 3600 =$ **3,600,000 J**.

q: A 2 kW heater runs for 3 hours. How much energy does it use in kWh?
answer: $2 \times 3 =$ **6 kWh**.

q: A car engine is rated at 100 horsepower. What is that in kilowatts?
answer: $100 \times 746 = 74{,}600$ W, about **75 kW**.

q: Two cranes lift identical loads to the same height. One takes twice as long. Compare their work and power.
answer: **Same work; the slower crane has half the power.**
:::
