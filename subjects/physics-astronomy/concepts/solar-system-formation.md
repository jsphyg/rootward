## Clues in today's solar system

Any story of how the solar system formed has to explain what we see:

| Clue | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| all planets orbit in nearly one plane, the same way | they formed from one flat, spinning disc |
| the Sun also spins that way | the Sun formed from the same spinning cloud |
| rocky planets close in, giants further out | temperature in the disc mattered |
| asteroids, comets and meteorites | leftover building blocks |
| the oldest meteorites are 4.6 billion years old | the solar system's age |

## From cloud to disc

Start with a huge, cold cloud of gas (mostly hydrogen and helium) and dust, slowly turning. Something, perhaps a nearby exploding star, squeezes it, and gravity takes over. The cloud collapses.

As it shrinks, it spins faster, like a skater pulling in their arms, because [[angular-momentum|angular momentum]] is conserved. Material can fall in easily along the spin axis, but spinning resists falling inward around the middle. So the cloud flattens into a spinning disc, with a dense, hot bulge at the centre.

## The Sun and the planets

The centre grew hot and dense enough to start nuclear fusion: the Sun was born, about 4.6 billion years ago.

In the disc, dust grains collided and stuck, building pebbles, then boulders, then bodies kilometres across called **planetesimals**. Their gravity pulled in more material, and the biggest grew into planets.

## The frost line

Near the young Sun, the disc was hot. Only rock and metal could stay solid; water and other ices turned to gas. Beyond about 3 AU, the {{frost-line|frost line}}, it was cold enough for ice to freeze too.

| Inside the frost line | Beyond the frost line |
|---|---|
| only rock and metal, which were scarce | rock, metal and plentiful ice |
| small rocky planets: Mercury to Mars | big cores, up to 10 Earth masses |
| too small to hold onto hydrogen gas | heavy enough to sweep up hydrogen and helium: the [[planets|giant planets]] |

Leftover planetesimals remain as asteroids (rocky, inside) and comets (icy, outside).

## The age

Some meteorites contain radioactive atoms that have been decaying since they formed. Measuring how much has decayed, using the half-life idea, gives about 4.6 billion years, the age of the solar system.

:::example
title: A gas cloud collapses, shrinking its radius 1000 times. If its angular momentum is conserved, by how much does its spin speed up? (Take its moment of inertia to scale as $r^2$.)

1. Angular momentum: $I_1\omega_1 = I_2\omega_2$.
   why: Nothing outside twists the cloud.
   uses: [angular-momentum]

2. I scales as $r^2$, so it falls by $1000^2 = 10^6$ times.
   why: For a given mass, I goes as radius squared.
   uses: []

3. So ω rises by $10^6$ times.
   why: Smaller I means faster spin, in exact proportion.
   uses: []

4. **A million times faster.** A cloud turning once in a million years would end up turning about once a year. That spin is why the cloud flattens into a disc.
   why: Rapid spin resists collapse around the middle, but not along the axis.
   uses: [planets]
:::

:::practice
q: Why did the collapsing cloud flatten into a disc instead of staying a ball?
answer: **It spun faster as it shrank. Spin resists collapse around the middle but not along the axis**, so it flattened.
:::

:::problems
q: How old is the solar system, and how do we know?
answer: **About 4.6 billion years, from radioactive dating of meteorites.**

q: Why do all the planets orbit in the same direction?
answer: **They formed from one spinning disc.**

q: What is the frost line, and where was it?
answer: **The distance beyond which ice could freeze in the disc**, about 3 AU from the Sun.

q: Why are the inner planets small and rocky?
answer: **Close to the hot Sun, only rock and metal could stay solid**, and there was too little to build big planets.

q: Why did the giant planets get so big?
answer: **Beyond the frost line, ice added lots of solid material**, so their cores grew large enough to pull in hydrogen and helium gas.

q: What are asteroids and comets, in this story?
answer: **Leftover building blocks**: rocky asteroids from inside the frost line, icy comets from outside.

q: A cloud shrinks to a tenth of its radius. By how much does its spin speed up?
answer: **100 times** ($10^2$).

q: What started the Sun shining?
answer: **Its core became hot and dense enough for nuclear fusion.**
:::
