## Two ripples meeting

Drop two stones into a still pond, a little apart. Each makes rings of ripples. Where the rings cross, you see something odd: some lines on the water where the waves are extra tall, and between them, lines where the water is almost flat.

## Waves add up

When two waves pass through the same place, the water (or air, or string) moves by the **sum** of what each wave would do on its own. This is the {{superposition|principle of superposition}}. Afterwards, each wave carries on as if the other had never been there.

| Where they meet | Wave 1 | Wave 2 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| crest on crest | +3 cm | +2 cm | +5 cm |
| trough on trough | −3 cm | −2 cm | −5 cm |
| crest on trough | +3 cm | −2 cm | +1 cm |
| equal crest on trough | +2 cm | −2 cm | 0 |

*Notice: waves can make each other bigger, or cancel each other out.*

Adding up is called {{interference|interference}}:

- **Constructive interference**: the waves are in step, and add to a bigger wave.
- **Destructive interference**: they are half a wave out of step, and cancel.

## Path difference

Two sources send out identical [[waves|waves]], in step. At any spot, the waves have each travelled a certain distance. The **path difference** is how much further one has come.

| Path difference | Waves arrive | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 0, λ, 2λ, 3λ, ... | in step | constructive: loud, bright |
| ½λ, 1½λ, 2½λ, ... | crest on trough | destructive: quiet, dark |

That is the pattern on the pond: lines of tall waves where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, flat lines in between.

Energy is not destroyed in the quiet spots. It is moved to the loud ones.

## Where you meet it

- **Noise-cancelling headphones** listen to outside noise and play the same wave upside down, so it cancels in your ear.
- **Soap bubbles and oil films** shine in colours. Light reflecting off the top and bottom of the thin film interferes, and different colours cancel at different thicknesses.
- **Light is a wave.** In 1801, Thomas Young shone light through two narrow slits and saw bright and dark stripes, the same pattern as the pond. Only waves do that.
- **Beats.** Two notes with slightly different frequencies drift in and out of step, so the sound swells and fades. The number of beats per second is the difference in frequency. Musicians tune by listening for the beats to slow and stop.

:::example
title: Two speakers play a 343 Hz note in step. Sound travels at 343 m/s. Is it loud or quiet at a spot 4 m from one speaker and 6.5 m from the other? What about 4 m and 7 m?

1. Wavelength: $\lambda = \dfrac{343}{343} = 1$ m.
   why: $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$.
   uses: [waves]

2. First spot: path difference $6.5 - 4 = 2.5$ m, which is $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ wavelengths.
   why: Divide the path difference by the wavelength.
   uses: []

3. A whole number plus a half means crest meets trough: **quiet**.
   why: Destructive interference.
   uses: []

4. Second spot: $7 - 4 = 3$ m, exactly 3 wavelengths, so crests meet crests: **loud**.
   why: Constructive interference.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: Two waves of amplitude 4 cm meet. What is the biggest and smallest amplitude they can make together?
answer: In step: **8 cm**. Exactly out of step: **0**.
:::

:::problems
q: A 3 cm crest meets a 2 cm crest. Find the height where they overlap.
answer: **5 cm.**

q: A 3 cm crest meets a 2 cm trough. Find the height.
answer: **1 cm.**

q: Two speakers in step give waves of wavelength 0.5 m. At a spot, the path difference is 0.25 m. Loud or quiet?
answer: Half a wavelength: **quiet**.

q: The path difference is 1 m, with λ = 0.5 m. Loud or quiet?
answer: Two whole wavelengths: **loud**.

q: A 686 Hz note (sound at 343 m/s) comes from two speakers in step. At a spot the path difference is 0.75 m. Loud or quiet?
answer: $\lambda = 0.5$ m, so 0.75 m is $1\tfrac{1}{2}$ wavelengths: **quiet**.

q: With λ = 2 m, what is the smallest path difference that gives a quiet spot?
answer: Half a wavelength: **1 m**.

q: How do noise-cancelling headphones work?
answer: **They play a copy of the noise upside down**, so crests meet troughs and the noise cancels.

q: At a quiet spot between two speakers, has the sound energy been destroyed?
answer: **No.** It has been moved to the loud spots.

q: Two tuning forks, 440 Hz and 444 Hz, sound together. How many beats per second do you hear?
answer: **4 beats per second.**

q: A guitar string and a 440 Hz tuning fork give 2 beats per second. What could the string's frequency be?
answer: **438 Hz or 442 Hz.** Tighten or loosen it slightly and listen to whether the beats speed up or slow down.

q: Why do soap bubbles show colours?
answer: **Light reflected from the two surfaces of the thin film interferes**, cancelling some colours and strengthening others, depending on the thickness.

q: Why did Young's two-slit experiment show that light is a wave?
answer: **It made bright and dark stripes**, an interference pattern, which only waves can produce.
:::
