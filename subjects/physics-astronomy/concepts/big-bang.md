## Running the film backwards

[[hubble-law|Hubble's law]] says the galaxies are moving apart. Run the film backwards and they crowd together. Further back still, there were no galaxies: everything was packed into a hotter and hotter, denser and denser state.

The **Big Bang theory** says that about 13.8 billion years ago, the universe was in an extremely hot, dense state, and it has been expanding and cooling ever since. The name was coined in 1949 by Fred Hoyle, who did not believe it and meant it as a joke.

## Not an explosion in space

The name suggests a bomb going off at one spot and debris flying out into empty space. That picture is wrong.

- There was no "outside" for things to fly into. **Space itself** was expanding.
- It happened **everywhere** at once. The place where you are now was part of it.
- So there is **no centre** and no edge to point to.

We can see only part of the universe, the {{observable-universe|observable universe}}: the region from which light has had time to reach us. Because space has stretched while that light travelled, its edge is now about 46 billion light-years away. What lies beyond, perhaps without end, we cannot see.

## Stretching light

As space stretches, light travelling through it is stretched too. If the universe doubles in size while the light travels, its wavelength doubles. So

$$1 + z = \frac{\text{size of the universe now}}{\text{size when the light left}}$$

A galaxy at z = 1 is seen as it was when the universe was half its present size. The most distant galaxies, at z above 10, are seen as they were when it was under a tenth of its present size.

## A short history

| Time after the start | What happened | Temperature |
|---|---|---|
| first fraction of a second | a hot soup of particles and light; matter's basic pieces form | trillions of K |
| 1 second to 3 minutes | protons and neutrons fuse into helium and a little lithium; most protons stay as hydrogen | about a billion K |
| 380,000 years | electrons join nuclei to make atoms; light travels freely for the first time | about 3000 K |
| a few hundred million years | the first stars and galaxies form | |
| 9.2 billion years | the Sun and Earth form | |
| 13.8 billion years | today | 2.7 K |

## The evidence

| Evidence | Why it supports the Big Bang |
|---|---|
| the expansion | galaxies moving apart means they were once together |
| the cosmic microwave background | the leftover glow of the hot early universe, seen in every direction (next page) |
| the light elements | the first minutes should make about 75% hydrogen and 25% helium by mass, just what [[stellar-spectra|the oldest stars]] show |
| galaxies changing | distant galaxies, seen long ago, look younger and more chaotic than nearby ones |

The theory does not say what came "before", or why there was a Big Bang. Those are still open questions.

:::practice
q: On that cosmic calendar, how long before midnight on December 31 did modern humans appear, 300,000 years ago?
answer: $\dfrac{300{,}000}{13.8 \text{ billion}} \times 525{,}600$ minutes $\approx$ **11 minutes**.
:::

:::problems
q: Light from a galaxy at z = 1 left it when the universe was what size, compared with now?
answer: **Half.** $1 + z = 2$.

q: A galaxy is seen at z = 3. How much bigger is the universe now than when the light left?
answer: **4 times.**

q: Light leaves a galaxy at 500 nm and the universe triples in size before it arrives. What wavelength is seen?
answer: **1500 nm**, in the infrared.

q: Was the Big Bang an explosion at one point in space? Explain.
answer: **No. All of space expanded at once**, everywhere, with no centre.

q: What fraction of ordinary matter, by mass, came out of the Big Bang as helium?
answer: **About a quarter.**

q: Why did light only travel freely after 380,000 years?
answer: **Before then, free electrons scattered it**; once atoms formed, the universe became clear.

q: On the cosmic calendar, when did the dinosaurs die out, 66 million years ago?
answer: About 42 hours before the end: **early on December 30**.

q: On the cosmic calendar, how long before midnight did writing begin, 5000 years ago?
answer: **About 11 seconds.**

q: Give three pieces of evidence for the Big Bang.
answer: **The expansion of the universe, the cosmic microwave background, and the amounts of hydrogen and helium.**

q: Why can't we see beyond the observable universe?
answer: **Light from further away has not had time to reach us.**
:::
