## The band across the sky

On a dark night far from city lights, a pale, glowing band arches across the sky. Ancient Greeks called it the "milky circle". In 1610, Galileo turned a telescope on it and found it was made of countless faint stars.

The band is our own galaxy seen from inside. The Milky Way is a flat disc of stars, and the Sun lies within it. Look along the disc and you see through vast numbers of stars, which merge into a glow. Look out of the disc and you see far fewer.

## The shape of our galaxy

| Part | What it is | Its stars |
|---|---|---|
| disc | a thin, flat layer about 100,000 light-years across and about 1000 thick, with spiral arms | young and old; new stars still forming in gas clouds |
| bulge | a crowded, slightly stretched ball of stars around the centre | mostly old |
| halo | a faint round cloud surrounding everything, with about 150 globular clusters | the oldest stars, over 12 billion years |
| centre | Sagittarius A*, a black hole of 4 million solar masses | a dense swarm of stars orbiting it |

The **spiral arms** are bright because they are where gas is squeezed and new stars form. Hot, blue, massive stars live only a few million years, as [[stellar-evolution|the life of a star]] showed, so they never get far from where they were born. They light up the arms like strings of lamps.

The galaxy holds roughly **100 to 400 billion stars**. The number is hard to pin down, because most are faint red dwarfs.

*Notice: the different parts hold stars of different ages. That is a record of the galaxy's history: the halo formed first, the disc later.*

## The Sun's place and orbit

In the 1910s, Harlow Shapley mapped the globular clusters and found they are centred on a point far away in the direction of Sagittarius. The Sun is not at the centre, but about **26,000 light-years** out, in the disc, between two spiral arms.

Like a planet round the Sun, the Sun [[orbits|orbits]] the centre of the galaxy:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| distance from the centre | about 26,000 light-years |
| orbital speed | about 230 km/s |
| time for one orbit (a {{galactic-year|galactic year}}) | about 220 million years |

The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old, so it has made about 20 laps.

## Weighing the galaxy

For a circular orbit, gravity supplies the centripetal force: $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$. Solving for M:

$$M = \frac{v^2 r}{G}$$

This gives the mass **inside** the orbit. If the mass is spread roughly evenly around the centre, the pulls of the mass further out mostly cancel, so they barely change the orbit. So measuring how fast stars orbit at different distances maps out where the galaxy's mass is. The next page shows the surprise that this reveals.

:::example
title: The Sun orbits at 230 km/s, 26,000 light-years ($2.46 \times 10^{20}$ m) from the centre. Find the galaxy's mass inside the Sun's orbit.

1. $M = \dfrac{v^2 r}{G} = \dfrac{(2.3 \times 10^5)^2 \times 2.46 \times 10^{20}}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}}$.
   why: Gravity provides the centripetal force for the Sun's orbit.
   uses: [orbits]

2. Top: $5.29 \times 10^{10} \times 2.46 \times 10^{20} \approx 1.30 \times 10^{31}$.
   why: Square the speed, then multiply.
   uses: []

3. $M \approx \dfrac{1.30 \times 10^{31}}{6.67 \times 10^{-11}} \approx 1.95 \times 10^{41}$ kg.
   why: Divide.
   uses: []

4. **About $2 \times 10^{41}$ kg, roughly 100 billion solar masses.**
   why: Divide by the Sun's mass, $2.0 \times 10^{30}$ kg.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: The Sun travels 2πr per orbit, with r = $2.46 \times 10^{20}$ m, at 230 km/s. Find the galactic year.
answer: $\dfrac{2\pi \times 2.46 \times 10^{20}}{2.3 \times 10^5} \approx 6.7 \times 10^{15}$ s, **about 210 million years**.
:::

:::problems
q: Why does the Milky Way look like a band in the sky?
answer: **We are inside a flat disc**, and see many more stars looking along it.

q: About how far is the Sun from the galaxy's centre?
answer: **About 26,000 light-years.**

q: How many orbits has the Sun made in its 4.6 billion years, if each takes 220 million years?
answer: $\dfrac{4600}{220} \approx$ **21 orbits**.

q: How far does the Sun travel in one year at 230 km/s? Give it in AU.
answer: $2.3 \times 10^5 \times 3.16 \times 10^7 \approx 7.3 \times 10^{12}$ m, **about 49 AU**.

q: A star orbits at 230 km/s, twice as far out as the Sun. How much mass is inside its orbit?
answer: $M \propto v^2 r$, so **twice as much**, about 200 billion solar masses.

q: A star orbits at 230 km/s at half the Sun's distance. How long is its orbit?
answer: **Half as long, about 110 million years**: half the distance at the same speed.

q: Why are spiral arms brighter than the gaps between them?
answer: **New stars form there**, including hot, bright, short-lived blue stars.

q: Why are the halo's stars all old?
answer: **The halo formed first and has almost no gas left** to make new stars.

q: How did Shapley find that the Sun is not at the galaxy's centre?
answer: **The globular clusters are centred on a point far away** toward Sagittarius.

q: What is at the very centre of the Milky Way?
answer: **Sagittarius A*, a black hole of about 4 million solar masses.**

q: Why does the mass outside a star's orbit barely affect its orbital speed?
answer: **Spread roughly evenly around, it pulls from all sides**, so its pulls mostly cancel.
:::
