# Chapter 20: Notes on Sources

*Kept out of the chapter file to match Chapters 13 to 19.*

- **Gilbert White** (1720 to 1793; curate of Selborne, Hampshire; nearly 40 years of observations; the 1783 to 1784 diary that records the Laki haze; *The Natural History and Antiquities of Selborne*, 1789; phenology records with William Markwick, 1768 to 1793): Wikipedia, "Gilbert White." The "blank as a clouded moon" and rust-colored light come from White's own description of the 1783 haze in *Selborne* (letter to Daines Barrington). Laki began erupting in June 1783 (standard).
- **Increase Lapham** (first official US storm warning, Great Lakes, issued from Chicago, November 8, 1870; "Father of the U.S. Weather Service"): Wikipedia, "Increase A. Lapham," and its cited source "First Official Weather Warning in the United States." The warning's exact words were not confirmed, so the chapter doesn't quote them.
- **The "tornado" ban** (1887, Signal Service, "severe local storms"; kept by the Weather Bureau from 1890; lifted for emergency managers in 1938; ended for the public by Francis Reichelderfer, circular letter of July 12, 1950): Wikipedia, "Tornado warning."
- **Severe-storm outlook levels** (marginal, slight, enhanced, moderate, high) and **watch, warning, advisory** definitions: Storm Prediction Center and NWS standard definitions.
- **First tornado emergency** (May 3, 1999, south Oklahoma City and Moore; David Andra, NWS Norman): Wikipedia, "Tornado emergency."
- **Impact tags** ("radar indicated," "observed," "considerable," "catastrophic"; "destructive" severe thunderstorm warnings that trigger phone alerts) and the **2024 rename** of wind chill products to extreme cold warnings and cold weather advisories: NWS impact-based warning program and the NWS 2024 hazard-simplification notice. Ch 10 already noted the 2024 change.
- **Joplin** (May 22, 2011; EF5; 158 deaths by the NWS count, 161 by the city's; sirens about 20 minutes before; "many Joplin residents did not heed the warning or the sirens"): Wikipedia, "2011 Joplin tornado," citing Reuters. The NWS service assessment's own findings were not read, so the chapter doesn't say why people waited. "Deadliest single US tornado in more than sixty years" refers to the 1947 Woodward, Oklahoma, tornado (standard).
- **1938 New England hurricane** (September 21, 1938; Charles Pierce, 28, overruled at the noon meeting by chief forecaster Charles Mitchell and senior staff; landfalls on Long Island near Bellport and near New Haven; 682 deaths; Long Island residents not warned): Wikipedia, "1938 New England hurricane."
- **Wilson Bentley** (first snowflake photograph January 15, 1885): Wikipedia, "Wilson Bentley." His age (19) follows from his birth in February 1865, standard.
- **Franklin's kite** (1752) is told as "is said to," because whether and how he flew it is debated. **Georg Wilhelm Richmann** (killed in St. Petersburg in 1753 while repeating the experiment; often called the first person killed in electrical research): standard history.
- **Wireless Emergency Alerts** and **NOAA Weather Radio**: FEMA and NWS public descriptions. Outdoor sirens are meant for people outdoors (NWS guidance).
- **Quick-reference card**: each line is drawn from that hazard chapter's own safety box (Chapters 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 17) and should be updated if those boxes change.
