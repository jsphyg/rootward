# Chapter 14: Notes on Sources

*Kept out of the chapter file to match Chapters 1 to 13.*

- El Niño's name: Camilo Carrillo's 1892 report to the geographical society congress in Lima (summarized in NOAA and Wikipedia ENSO histories).
- Tambora and 1816: UCAR Center for Science Education, "Mount Tambora and the Year Without a Summer."
- Eratosthenes: standard histories of his measurement (Syene, Alexandria, about 7.2 degrees).
- Utqiaġvik polar night: TIME, November 18, 2024 (last sunset November 18, next sunrise January 22, about 64 days).
- "Coldest winter" quote: Quote Investigator, November 30, 2011 (no evidence Twain said it).
- Franklin and Folger: Newberry Library, "Benjamin Franklin's Chart of the Gulf Stream, 1768–1770" (mail ships up to two weeks slower).
- Europe's mild winters and the Gulf Stream: Seager and others, "Is the Gulf Stream responsible for Europe's mild winters?" Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society, 2002.
- George Hadley: Philosophical Transactions 39 (1735), "Concerning the Cause of the General Trade-Winds."
- Brouwer Route: route of 1611, about half the voyage time, compulsory for VOC ships by 1616.
- Mumbai floods: India Meteorological Department figure of 944 mm at Santacruz, July 26, 2005; about 1,094 deaths across the event.
- Gilbert Walker: Walker's 1924 memoir naming the Southern Oscillation; Bjerknes 1969 linking it to El Niño.
- 1982–83 El Niño and the TAO array: NOAA PMEL (McPhaden) histories (not detected until months after onset; El Chichón cold bias in satellite data).
- 2015–16 El Niño: Scripps Institution of Oceanography, "Why was the 2015-16 El Niño event so dry?" (2017).
- Eunice Foote: American Physical Society, "This Month in Physics History," July 2023.
- 2021 heat dome attribution: World Weather Attribution, "Western North American extreme heat virtually impossible without human-caused climate change" (2021).
- ENSO thresholds, outlook categories, and normals: NOAA Climate Prediction Center and NCEI.
