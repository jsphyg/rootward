# Chapter 16: Notes on Sources

*Kept out of the chapter file to match Chapters 13 to 15.*

- **World records in general** (rain at every time span, Vostok, Agata and Tosontsengel pressure, Typhoon Tip, Barrow Island gust, Gopalganj hailstone, lightning distance, duration, and deaths): WMO, "Records of Weather and Climate Extremes" table, version of July 25, 2025 (public.wmo.int), and the WMO Archive of Weather and Climate Extremes map pages.
- **El Azizia** (58.0°C, September 13, 1922; review 2010 to 2012; rejected September 13, 2012; new observer from September 11, 1922; replacement thermometer; reading probably about 7°C too high; dark tarmac-like site; Khalid El Fadli out of contact for about six months in 2011, first email August 13, 2011): El Fadli and others, Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society (2013); Smithsonian, "How the Record for Hottest Temperature Ever Was Refuted"; Wikipedia, "ʽAziziya"; Christopher Burt's Weather Underground account.
- **Death Valley** (56.7°C, July 10, 1913, Greenland Ranch, thermometer graduated to 135°F, possible sandstorm): WMO Archive of Weather and Climate Extremes. "Some climatologists doubt it" is kept general.
- **Rain records** (Unionville, Holt, Foc-Foc, Cherrapunji, Cratère Commerson): WMO table. Holt is given as "less than an hour" because the WMO lists it as the 60-minute record. The tropical cyclone at Foc-Foc is not named because the name was not checked.
- **Galveston, Chicago 1995, Buffalo 2014, Patricia, Lytton**: numbers reused from Chapters 9, 11, 10, 9, and 5 and 14, as the book map allows. Lytton's margin over Canada's old record (45.0°C, 1937) is standard.
- **Coldest surface** (about −98°C, East Antarctic Plateau, winters 2004 to 2016, satellite thermal data, snow surface not air): AGU press release, "Coldest place on Earth is colder than scientists thought" (June 25, 2018); Scambos and others, Geophysical Research Letters (2018).
- **Browning, Montana** (7°C to −49°C, January 23 to 24, 1916): figures from Chapter 6. The claim that neighbors "plunged too" was left out because it was not checked.
- **Montague, New York** (77 inches, January 11 to 12, 1997; five board measurements instead of four; not accepted) and **Silver Lake, Colorado** (75.8 inches, April 14 to 15, 1921): Christopher Burt, Weather Underground, "US 24-hour snowfall records" (2016) and "Extreme snowfall reports: how reliable are they?" (2018); NOAA press release of May 1, 1997. Alaska's competing Mile 47 Camp claim (78 inches, 1963) was left out to keep the story simple.
- **Wind** (Mount Washington 231 mph, April 12, 1934, record for 62 years; Barrow Island 408 km/h three-second gust at 10 m, April 10, 1996, private instrument, WMO announcement January 2010): MPR News (January 27, 2010); Wikipedia, "Cyclone Olivia"; Courtney and others, Australian Meteorological and Oceanographic Journal (2012).
- **Vivian, South Dakota hailstone** (July 23, 2010; 8.0 in diameter, 18.625 in circumference, 1 lb 15 oz; Les Scott; freezer and power cut; weighed at the post office) and **Aurora, Nebraska** (June 22, 2003; circumference 18.75 in) and **Coffeyville, Kansas** (1.67 lb, 1970, earlier weight record): NWS Aberdeen, "Vivian hailstone"; NCDC National Climate Extremes Committee report (November 2010).
- **Megaflashes** (768 km, April 29, 2020, southern US; 17.1 s, June 18, 2020, Uruguay and northern Argentina; certified 2022; 829 km certified July 31, 2025, eastern Texas to near Kansas City, found by re-examining GOES-16 data): WMO press releases (February 2022 and July 31, 2025). The 829 km flash's year is left out on purpose: the WMO press release says October 2017, but the WMO records table lists October 22, 2020.
- **Dronka, Egypt** (November 2, 1994, 469 deaths): WMO table. That burning fuel was carried into town by floodwater is from standard accounts.
- **Antarctic records** (Esperanza 18.3°C, February 6, 2020, accepted; Seymour Island 20.75°C, February 9, 2020, rejected for an improvised radiation shield): WMO press release, July 1, 2021.
- **Coningsby** (40.3°C, July 19, 2022; confirmed July 28, 2022; site inspections, equipment tests, cross-checks; Floors Castle provisional Scottish value rejected for site setup): Met Office, "Record high temperatures verified" (July 28, 2022).
- **Old Weather** (launched October 2010; WWI Royal Navy logs; 16,400 volunteers and more than a million pages by July 2012; later US Arctic ships; feeds reanalyses including the 20th Century Reanalysis): Wikipedia, "Old Weather"; NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory news (December 4, 2015).
- **Time-of-observation bias** (shift from afternoon to morning readings in the US cooperative network gives a cool bias): Karl and others, Journal of Climate and Applied Meteorology (1986), kept general.
- **Ellicott City** (late July 2016, about 6 inches in a couple of hours; May 27, 2018, about 8 inches in a few hours; Patapsco River rose more than 16.5 ft in under three hours; both called 1,000-year events): NOAA Climate.gov event tracker (2018); WUSA9 weather blog. Deaths were left out because they were not checked.
- **Return-period arithmetic**: worked out in this thread (1 − 0.99^30 ≈ 0.26; 1 − 0.998^30 ≈ 0.06; 1 − 0.999^30 ≈ 0.03; 1 − 0.98^20 ≈ 0.33).
- **Record highs vs. lows about two to one in the 2000s**: Meehl and others, Geophysical Research Letters (2009).
