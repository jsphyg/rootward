# Weather Book Map: Which Chapter Owns Each Idea

*Working version 1, made after Chapter 1 was revised. Each chapter thread should read this before starting, along with [style-guide.md](style-guide.md).*

---

## The rule

**Every idea has one home.** The owner chapter explains it fully. Any other chapter that needs it uses one or two sentences and points back ("as we saw in Chapter 3").

The draft breaks this rule often, and that is the biggest reason it is about 127,000 words long. For example, tornadoes are explained in Chapters 7, 8, 19, and 20, and radar in Chapters 7, 8, 12, and 13.

## 2. Structural changes (approved by Joe, 2026-11-22)

These change the shape of the book, not just the wording. **All five are approved.** Chapter threads should follow them.

**A. Merge tornado content into Chapter 8.**
Chapter 7 keeps one short paragraph on tornadoes and hands off. Saves several thousand words.

**B. Put safety in one place per hazard.**
Each hazard chapter (7, 8, 9, 10, 11) keeps its own short safety box, and Chapter 20 becomes a one-page quick-reference card that points back. The draft repeats full safety advice in both places.

**C. Make Chapter 19 a pure visual field guide.**
It becomes picture keys and side-by-side comparisons ("wall cloud vs. shelf cloud"), with almost no explanation, since Chapters 4, 6, and 7 already explain everything. This fits the illustrated-guide style well.

**D. Combine the forecasting routines.**
Chapters 13, 18, and 20 each have their own morning, afternoon, and evening routine, and Chapters 13 and 20 both have a "three-sentence forecast." Keep one routine, in Chapter 13.

**E. Fold Chapter 16's instrument sections into Chapter 18.**
Chapter 16 keeps records and how they are verified. Chapter 18 keeps how instruments work.
