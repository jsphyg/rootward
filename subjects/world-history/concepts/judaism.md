## One God

Most ancient peoples believed in many gods, each with power over one thing: the sun, a river, a city. The ancient Israelites, also called the Hebrews, came to believe something different: that there is only one God, who created everything. This belief is called {{monotheism|monotheism}}.

According to Jewish tradition, the story begins with Abraham, who came from the city of Ur in Mesopotamia. God made a {{covenant|covenant}}, a sacred agreement, with Abraham and his descendants. Later, the prophet Moses led the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt and received God's law, including the Ten Commandments.^[n_exodus]

## Kingdom, conquest and exile

| About when | What happened |
|---|---|
| About 1000 BCE | Kings David and Solomon rule a kingdom of Israel, with a temple in Jerusalem |
| 722 BCE | The Assyrians conquer the northern kingdom, Israel |
| 586 BCE | The Babylonians destroy Jerusalem and its Temple, and take many Jews into {{exile|exile}} |
| 538 BCE | The Persian king Cyrus lets the exiles return and rebuild the Temple |
| 70 CE | The Romans destroy the rebuilt Temple |

*Notice: the Jewish people were conquered by almost every great empire of the region, one after another.*

## A portable faith

The exile in Babylon changed Judaism deeply. With the Temple gone and the people far from home, Jewish faith came to center on scripture, law, prayer and the Sabbath, the weekly day of rest. These could be practiced anywhere.

The holiest scripture is the {{torah|Torah}}, the first five books of the Hebrew Bible. Its laws cover worship, food, family and justice, including care for the poor and the stranger.

After the Romans destroyed the Temple in 70 CE, most Jews lived scattered across many lands. This scattering is called the {{diaspora|diaspora}}. Their faith held them together as a people for nearly 2,000 years without a homeland.

**Why it matters.** Judaism's belief in one God, and its idea that God demands justice and right behavior, passed into Christianity and Islam. Together, these three faiths are followed by more than half of the people in the world today.
