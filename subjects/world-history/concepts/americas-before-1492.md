## A separate world

After the Ice Age, rising seas cut the Americas off from Asia. For thousands of years, the peoples of the Americas had no contact with the rest of the world. They domesticated their own crops, maize, beans, squash and potatoes, and built their own civilizations.

Two regions saw the biggest states: {{mesoamerica|Mesoamerica}} (southern Mexico and Central America) and the Andes mountains of South America.

| People | Where | About when |
|---|---|---|
| Olmec | Gulf coast of Mexico | 1200 to 400 BCE |
| Maya | Southern Mexico, Guatemala, Belize | City-states at their height about 250 to 900 CE |
| Cahokia | Mississippi valley, North America | About 1050 to 1350 CE |
| Aztec | Central Mexico | About 1325 to 1521 CE |
| Inca | Andes of South America | About 1400 to 1533 CE |

*Notice: these peoples did not all live at the same time. The Olmec flourished more than 2,000 years before the Aztecs.*

## The Maya

The Maya built dozens of city-states in the rainforests of Central America, with stone temples and pyramids. They developed the most complete writing system in the Americas. Their astronomers tracked the sun, moon and Venus very precisely, and their mathematics used a symbol for zero.

Many southern Maya cities were abandoned in the 800s and 900s CE, probably because of drought, war and overused land. But the Maya people did not vanish. Millions of Maya live in the region today.

## The Aztecs

The Aztecs, who called themselves the Mexica, founded their capital, Tenochtitlan, around 1325 on an island in a lake. They grew food on floating garden beds and built causeways to the shore. By 1500 the city may have had 200,000 people, more than any city in Spain at the time.

The Aztecs ruled by conquest and {{tribute|tribute}}. Conquered peoples paid goods, food and captives to the capital. That made many of them resent Aztec rule.

## The Inca

The Inca built the largest empire in the Americas along the Andes mountains, with perhaps 10 million people. Its capital was Cusco.

The Inca had no writing, no iron and no wheeled vehicles. Yet they ran a huge empire. A road network of tens of thousands of kilometers carried relay runners and armies. Storehouses held food for hard times. Officials recorded numbers on {{quipu|quipu}}, sets of knotted strings.

**Why it matters.** The Americas show that civilization was not invented once and spread. People on a separate continent built cities, states, calendars and writing on their own.

:::example
title: Why did the Americas develop on a separate path from Europe, Africa and Asia?

1. Rising seas after the Ice Age cut the Americas off from Asia.
   why: The land bridge was flooded, and the oceans were too wide to cross regularly.
   uses: [human-origins]

2. The Americas had different plants and very few large animals that could be domesticated.
   why: Horses had died out there thousands of years earlier. The llama was the only large pack animal.
   uses: [agricultural-revolution]

3. So the peoples of the Americas built cities, states and writing with their own crops and tools.
   why: They developed independently, with no ideas borrowed from other continents.
   uses: []

4. They also had no exposure to the diseases of Europe, Africa and Asia.
   why: This would prove catastrophic after 1492, when contact brought new diseases they had no resistance to.
   uses: [causation]
:::

:::practice
q: The Inca had no writing. How did they run an empire of millions of people?
answer: Officials kept records of people, goods and taxes on knotted-string quipu. A huge road system carried relay runners with messages and moved armies quickly. Storehouses along the roads held food and supplies. People paid taxes partly in labor for the state.
:::
