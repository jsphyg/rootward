## A system in trouble

By the 1980s the Soviet Union had serious problems.

| Problem | Effect |
|---|---|
| A stagnant economy | Central planning produced shortages, poor-quality goods and long lines for basics |
| The arms race | Huge military spending, which rose further as the United States built up its forces in the 1980s |
| Afghanistan | A long, costly war from 1979 to 1989, which the Soviets could not win |
| Unhappy satellites | Many Eastern Europeans resented Soviet control; in Poland, the free trade union Solidarity, founded in 1980, gained millions of members |

## Gorbachev's reforms

Mikhail Gorbachev became Soviet leader in 1985. He wanted to save communism by reforming it.

| Reform | Meaning |
|---|---|
| {{glasnost|Glasnost}} | "Openness": more freedom of speech and press, and honesty about problems |
| {{perestroika|Perestroika}} | "Restructuring": some market reforms in the economy |
| New thinking abroad | Arms reduction deals with the United States, and an end to forcing communism on Eastern Europe |

*Notice: Gorbachev meant to strengthen the Soviet system. By allowing people to speak freely, he let loose criticism that the system could not survive.*

## 1989: the year of revolutions

| Country | What happened |
|---|---|
| Poland | Solidarity wins free elections in June; a non-communist government takes office |
| Hungary | Opens its border with Austria; East Germans flee west through it |
| East Germany | Mass protests; on 9 November the Berlin Wall is opened, and crowds tear it down |
| Czechoslovakia | The peaceful "Velvet Revolution"; the dissident writer Václav Havel becomes president |
| Romania | The only violent revolution; the dictator Ceaușescu is executed |

Germany was reunited on 3 October 1990.

## The Soviet Union breaks apart

The republics that made up the Soviet Union, from the Baltic states to Ukraine, demanded independence. In August 1991 hard-line communists tried to overthrow Gorbachev, but crowds in Moscow, led by Boris Yeltsin, stopped them. On 26 December 1991 the Soviet Union dissolved into 15 independent countries, the largest of them Russia.

*Notice: one of history's great empires ended with very little bloodshed, compared with the wars that ended most empires.*

:::practice
q: Why did Gorbachev's reforms help bring down the Soviet Union, which he wanted to save?
answer: Glasnost let people speak openly about shortages, corruption and past crimes, which destroyed faith in the system. Perestroika's half-reforms disrupted the old planned economy without creating a working market one, so shortages grew. And his refusal to use force let Eastern Europe and then the Soviet republics break away.
:::
