## A Church under criticism

By 1500 the Catholic Church was rich and powerful, and many people felt it had lost its way. Some popes lived like princes. Some priests could barely read.

What angered many most was the sale of {{indulgence|indulgences}}. Buying one was said to shorten the punishment for sins after death. In 1517 a preacher in Germany was selling them to raise money to rebuild St. Peter's Basilica in Rome.

## Luther's protest

Martin Luther was a German monk and professor at Wittenberg. In 1517 he wrote 95 Theses, arguments against indulgences, and sent them to church leaders.^[n_luther_door]

| Luther's idea | What it meant |
|---|---|
| Faith alone | People are saved by trusting God, not by buying indulgences or earning it |
| The Bible alone | The Bible, not the pope or church tradition, is the final authority |
| Every believer | Ordinary people can read the Bible and pray to God directly, without a priest in between |

The pope {{excommunicate|excommunicated}} him in 1521. That same year he refused to take back his views before the emperor. A German prince hid him in a castle, where he translated the New Testament into German so ordinary people could read it.

*Notice: earlier critics of the Church had been silenced. Luther survived because printing spread his ideas faster than they could be stopped, and because powerful princes protected him.*

## Many churches

Luther's followers came to be called {{protestant|Protestants}}, because they protested. Soon there were several kinds.

| Church | Founder or leader | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Lutheran | Martin Luther | Much of Germany and Scandinavia |
| Reformed (Calvinist) | John Calvin, in Geneva | Switzerland, the Netherlands, Scotland, parts of France |
| Church of England | King Henry VIII, 1534 | England |

Henry VIII's reasons were partly personal. The pope would not let him end his marriage, which had produced no son, so Henry made himself head of the Church in England.

## The Catholic response

The Catholic Church reformed itself. The Council of Trent, meeting from 1545 to 1563, ended the worst abuses but kept Catholic teachings. A new order of priests, the Jesuits, founded in 1540, built schools and sent missionaries around the world.

## A century of religious war

Rulers fought over religion and power for more than a hundred years. The worst was the Thirty Years' War from 1618 to 1648, which devastated Germany. The Peace of Westphalia in 1648 let each ruler choose the religion of their own state. Western Christianity has stayed divided ever since.

:::practice
q: Why did some German princes support Luther?
answer: Some honestly agreed with his religious ideas. But many also saw a chance to gain: by breaking with Rome they could take over Church lands and wealth, stop money flowing to the pope, and gain more independence from the Catholic emperor. Faith and self-interest worked together.
:::
