## The call

In the 1000s the Seljuk Turks, Muslim newcomers from Central Asia, took over much of the Middle East. In 1071 they defeated the Byzantines and moved into Anatolia. They also controlled Jerusalem, the city where Jesus died, which Christians saw as holy.

The Byzantine emperor asked the pope for soldiers. In 1095 Pope Urban II gave a famous speech in France. He called on knights to free Jerusalem, promising that their sins would be forgiven. Crowds answered "God wills it!"

A {{crusade|crusade}} was a war that the pope declared holy. Crusaders sewed a cloth cross on their clothes. Some went out of faith, some for land, wealth or adventure, and many for all of these.

## The main crusades

| Dates | Crusade | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1096 to 1099 | First | Crusaders take Jerusalem and kill many of its Muslim and Jewish people. They set up four crusader states |
| 1147 to 1149 | Second | Fails |
| 1187 | — | Saladin, sultan of Egypt and Syria, crushes the crusaders and retakes Jerusalem |
| 1189 to 1192 | Third | Led by kings of England and France and the German emperor. Wins back some coast but not Jerusalem |
| 1202 to 1204 | Fourth | Never reaches the Holy Land. Sacks Christian Constantinople instead |
| 1291 | — | Acre, the last crusader city, falls to the Mamluks of Egypt |

*Notice: after the first, almost every crusade failed. The numbering was added by later historians, and many smaller crusades are left out.*^[n_crusade_numbers]

## Who suffered

The Crusades were violent for many people, not only soldiers.

- Muslims and Jews in Jerusalem were massacred in 1099.
- On their way east in 1096, crusader bands attacked Jewish communities in the Rhineland, in Germany, killing thousands.
- The sack of Constantinople in 1204 left a lasting bitterness between Catholic and Orthodox Christians.

## Effects

| Area | Effect |
|---|---|
| Trade | Italian cities such as Venice and Genoa grew rich carrying crusaders and trading for eastern spices and silk |
| Kings | Many troublesome nobles left or died abroad, which helped some kings increase their power |
| Ideas | Contact with the Muslim world added to the flow of learning into Europe, though more came through Spain and Sicily |
| Relations | Distrust between Christians and Muslims, and between western and eastern Christians, grew deeper |

:::practice
q: Why did the Fourth Crusade attack Constantinople, a Christian city?
answer: The crusaders could not pay Venice for the ships it had built them, so they agreed to help Venice and to back a claimant to the Byzantine throne in return for money. When that deal fell apart, they attacked and looted the city in 1204. Greed, debt and old distrust between western and eastern Christians turned the crusade against fellow Christians.
:::
