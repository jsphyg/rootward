## Hatred made law

Nazi ideology held that Germans belonged to a superior "Aryan" race and that Jews were a dangerous enemy race. This was {{antisemitism|antisemitism}}, hatred of Jews, an old prejudice that the Nazis made the core of state policy.

| Year | Step |
|---|---|
| 1933 | Boycott of Jewish shops; Jews pushed out of government jobs, universities and professions |
| 1935 | The Nuremberg Laws: Jews lose German citizenship and may not marry non-Jews |
| 1938 | Kristallnacht, the "Night of Broken Glass": synagogues burned, shops wrecked, about 30,000 Jewish men sent to concentration camps |
| 1939 to 1941 | After invading Poland, the Nazis force Jews into crowded, walled {{ghetto|ghettos}}, where many die of hunger and disease |

*Notice: the Holocaust did not begin with gas chambers. It began with words, laws and exclusion, each step making the next easier.*

## Mass murder

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| Mass shootings, from June 1941 | As Germany invaded the Soviet Union, killing squads shot Jews village by village. At Babi Yar, near Kyiv, about 33,000 Jews were shot in two days in September 1941 |
| Planning, January 1942 | At the Wannsee Conference, Nazi officials coordinated the "Final Solution": the murder of all Europe's Jews |
| Death camps, 1942 to 1944 | Jews from across Europe were sent by train to camps in occupied Poland, such as Treblinka, Sobibor, Belzec and Auschwitz-Birkenau, and most were killed in gas chambers on arrival |

At Auschwitz-Birkenau alone, about 1.1 million people were murdered, about a million of them Jews.

## Other victims

The Nazis also murdered:

- Roma and Sinti people, perhaps 200,000 or more.
- About 250,000 disabled people, killed in hospitals and institutions.
- About 3 million Soviet prisoners of war, through starvation and shooting.
- Many Poles and other Slavs, political opponents, gay men and Jehovah's Witnesses.

## Resistance and rescue

Some Jews fought back. In the Warsaw Ghetto in 1943, a few hundred poorly armed fighters held off German troops for nearly a month. Others escaped to join partisan groups, or rose up inside the camps at Treblinka and Sobibor.

Some non-Jews risked their lives to hide or rescue Jews. Israel honors them as Righteous Among the Nations. Denmark smuggled almost all its Jews to safety in Sweden in 1943. But many more people looked away, and many helped the killers.

## After

Allied soldiers who reached the camps in 1944 and 1945 found starving survivors and mass graves. Nazi leaders were tried at Nuremberg in 1945 and 1946.

In 1944 a lawyer named Raphael Lemkin created the word {{genocide|genocide}}, the deliberate destruction of a people. In 1948 the United Nations made genocide a crime under international law.

The Holocaust is one of the best documented events in history, through Nazi records, photographs, trials and the testimony of survivors, perpetrators and liberators.^[n_holocaust_evidence]

:::practice
q: Why do historians stress that the Holocaust happened step by step?
answer: The genocide did not begin all at once. It started with propaganda, laws and exclusion, then violence, then ghettos, and only then mass murder. Each step was accepted or ignored by many people, which made the next step possible. Understanding this shows how prejudice can grow into mass killing, and why it matters to speak up early.
:::
