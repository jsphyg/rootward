## The First Emperor

For two centuries, the Warring States fought for control of China. In 221 BCE the state of Qin conquered the last of its rivals. Its king took a new title: Qin Shi Huang, "First Emperor of Qin". The name China probably comes from Qin.

He ruled with {{legalism|Legalism}}: strict laws, harsh punishments and total obedience to the state. To make China one country, he made many things the same everywhere.

| What he standardized | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Writing | People with different spoken dialects could read the same documents |
| Coins | One currency made trade easier |
| Weights and measures | Fair taxes and trade across the empire |
| Cart axle widths | Carts could use the same road ruts everywhere |

*Notice: a shared written script held China together for the next 2,000 years, even when its spoken languages differed.*

He built roads and canals, and joined earlier walls into a long defensive wall in the north. He burned books of rival schools of thought.^[n_burning]

He was buried with an army of life-size clay soldiers, the Terracotta Army, rediscovered by farmers digging a well in 1974.

The Qin did not last. Forced labor and harsh laws made people furious. Soon after the First Emperor died in 210 BCE, revolts broke out, and the dynasty fell in 206 BCE.

## The Han dynasty

The Han dynasty, founded by Liu Bang, a man of humble birth, ruled from 206 BCE to 220 CE. It kept the Qin's united empire and many of its systems, but softened its harshness.

- **Confucian government.** The Han made Confucianism the official philosophy. Officials were increasingly chosen for their learning in the Confucian classics.
- **Expansion and trade.** Han armies pushed west into Central Asia, and Han envoys opened the routes later called the Silk Roads.
- **Inventions.** Paper was invented in the Han era. So were better plows and the earliest seismograph to detect earthquakes.

A census in 2 CE counted about 57 million people, roughly as many as the Roman Empire at the same time. To this day, China's largest ethnic group calls itself the Han.

**Why it matters.** The Qin created a single Chinese empire; the Han made it last. The pattern they set, one emperor ruling through educated officials over a unified China, lasted until 1912.

:::practice
q: How did standardizing writing help hold China together?
answer: People in different regions spoke very different dialects. But with one written script, officials, merchants and scholars everywhere could read the same laws, letters and books. That let one government rule all of China, and gave Chinese people a shared culture.
:::
