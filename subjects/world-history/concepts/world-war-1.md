## Two sides

The July Crisis turned into a war between two alliances.

| The Allies | The Central Powers |
|---|---|
| France, Britain and its empire, Russia, Serbia, Belgium | Germany, Austria-Hungary |
| Later: Japan (1914), Italy (1915), the United States (1917) | Later: the Ottoman Empire (1914), Bulgaria (1915) |

Almost everyone expected a short war, "over by Christmas."

## The Western Front

Germany planned to defeat France quickly by going through Belgium, then turn on Russia. In September 1914 the French and British stopped the Germans at the Battle of the Marne, near Paris.

Both sides then dug in. By the end of 1914 a line of {{trench|trenches}} ran about 700 km from the English Channel to Switzerland. Between them lay "no man's land," a wasteland of mud, craters and barbed wire.

| Battle | Year | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Verdun | 1916 | France and Germany fought for 10 months; hundreds of thousands died on each side |
| The Somme | 1916 | On the first day, 1 July, the British army suffered about 57,000 casualties, about 19,000 of them killed |

*Notice: defense had become far stronger than attack. Generals kept trying to break through, and kept failing, for almost four years.*

## New weapons

| Weapon | Effect |
|---|---|
| Machine guns | Could fire hundreds of bullets a minute across no man's land |
| Artillery | Huge guns caused most of the war's casualties |
| Poison gas | First used on a large scale by Germany in 1915; later by both sides |
| Tanks | Introduced by Britain in 1916 to cross trenches |
| Aircraft and submarines | Planes scouted and bombed; German submarines sank ships at sea |

## A world war

The war spread far beyond Europe. Russia fought Germany and Austria-Hungary in the east. The Ottoman Empire fought the Allies at Gallipoli and in the Middle East. Soldiers from India, Africa, Canada, Australia and New Zealand fought for the British and French empires. In 1915 the Ottoman government began the mass killing of its Armenian subjects, in which about a million people died, now widely recognized as genocide.

## Total war

This was a {{total-war|total war}}. Governments took control of factories, rationed food and used propaganda. Millions of women took over men's jobs in factories and offices. Civilians, not just soldiers, became targets of blockades and bombing.

## The end

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1917 | Revolution in Russia; the new Bolshevik government makes peace with Germany in March 1918 |
| April 1917 | The United States joins the Allies, angered by German submarine attacks on its ships |
| 1918 | Germany's last great attack fails; its allies collapse and Germany faces revolt at home |
| 11 November 1918 | The armistice: fighting stops at 11 a.m. |

About 10 million soldiers died, and millions of civilians.^[n_ww1_deaths] The empires of Germany, Austria-Hungary, Russia and the Ottomans all fell.

:::practice
q: What made World War I a "total war"?
answer: Governments used their whole society and economy for the war, not just their armies. They ran factories to make weapons, rationed food, drafted millions of men, brought women into the workforce and used propaganda to keep people supporting the war. Civilians became targets too, through blockades meant to starve whole countries.
:::
