## The richest colony

Saint-Domingue, on the western part of the Caribbean island of Hispaniola, produced more sugar and coffee than any other colony in the world. Its wealth rested on brutal slavery.

| Group | About how many, in 1789 |
|---|---|
| Enslaved Africans | 500,000 |
| White colonists | 40,000 |
| Free people of color | 30,000 |

Conditions were so harsh that many enslaved people died within a few years of arriving, and planters bought more.

## Revolt

News of the French Revolution and its Declaration of the Rights of Man reached the colony. Free people of color demanded equal rights. Then, in August 1791, enslaved people in the north rose up, burning plantations.

The revolt's greatest leader was Toussaint Louverture, a formerly enslaved man who had learned to read and became a brilliant general.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1791 | Enslaved people revolt |
| 1793 to 1794 | French officials free the enslaved in the colony; in 1794 France abolishes slavery in all its colonies |
| 1790s | Toussaint defeats Spanish and British invasions and takes control of the colony |
| 1802 | Napoleon sends a large army to regain control. Toussaint is captured and dies in a French prison in 1803 |
| 1802 to 1803 | Fearing slavery's return, Haitians fight on under Jean-Jacques Dessalines. Yellow fever and battle destroy the French army |
| 1 January 1804 | Dessalines declares independence. The country takes the name Haiti, from a native Taíno word |

*Notice: the revolutionaries took the French Revolution's ideas more seriously than France did. When France tried to take freedom back, they fought for independence.*

## A frightening example

Haiti was the second independent country in the Americas, after the United States. Slaveholders across the Americas were terrified that its example would spread. The United States refused to recognize Haiti until 1862. France demanded a huge payment in 1825 for its lost property, including the people it had enslaved, which burdened Haiti for more than a century.^[n_haiti_debt]

Haiti also helped others. In 1816 it gave Simón Bolívar ships, weapons and soldiers for his fight to free South America, asking in return that he free the enslaved.

:::practice
q: Why did Napoleon's army fail to retake Saint-Domingue?
answer: Haitians fought fiercely because they knew defeat meant a return to slavery. Napoleon's capture of Toussaint did not end the resistance, since Dessalines and others kept fighting. And yellow fever killed tens of thousands of French soldiers, who had no immunity to it, so the French army collapsed.
:::
