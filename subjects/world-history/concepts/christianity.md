## Jesus of Nazareth

Jesus was a Jewish teacher from Galilee, in the Roman province of Judea. He was born around 4 BCE and began preaching around 28 CE.^[n_jesus_evidence] He taught love of God and of one's neighbor, forgiveness, and care for the poor and outcast. He spoke of the coming kingdom of God.

Around 30 CE, the Roman governor, Pontius Pilate, had him executed by {{crucifixion|crucifixion}}, a Roman punishment for rebels and slaves.

His followers believed that he rose from the dead, and that he was the {{messiah|Messiah}} promised in the Jewish scriptures. "Christ" is the Greek word for Messiah, so his followers came to be called Christians.

## From Jewish sect to world religion

At first, Jesus' followers were Jews. The apostle Paul, a Jew who became a follower, preached to non-Jews across the eastern Mediterranean and taught that they could join without following Jewish law. His letters became part of the New Testament.

The Roman Empire helped Christianity spread:

| Roman feature | How it helped |
|---|---|
| Roads and sea routes | Missionaries could travel safely and quickly |
| Greek as a shared language | The message and the New Testament could reach many peoples |
| Big cities | Ideas spread fast among crowds |
| Peace across the empire | Travel and letters moved freely |

*Notice: Christianity spread along the same networks that held the empire together.*

## Persecution, then power

Christians refused to worship the Roman gods or the emperor, which many Romans saw as disloyal. Some emperors persecuted them, from Nero in 64 CE to Diocletian around 303 CE. Christians killed for their faith were honored as martyrs.

Then everything changed. In 313 CE, Emperor Constantine legalized Christianity. In 325 he called the Council of Nicaea, where bishops agreed on core beliefs. In 380, Emperor Theodosius made Christianity the empire's official religion.

The Church organized itself like the empire, with bishops in major cities. The bishop of Rome, later called the pope, became the leader of Christians in the west.

**Why it matters.** Christianity became the religion of the Roman world and, through it, of Europe and later the Americas. It is the largest religion in the world today, with over two billion followers.
