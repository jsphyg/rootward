## The Ming: China for the Chinese

Mongol rule in China ended in rebellion. In 1368 Zhu Yuanzhang, a former peasant and monk, drove out the Mongols and founded the Ming dynasty. Ming means "bright."

| Ming achievement | What it was |
|---|---|
| The Forbidden City | A vast palace in Beijing, built from 1406 to 1420, where emperors lived cut off from ordinary people |
| The Great Wall | Mostly rebuilt in brick and stone, in the form tourists see today |
| Zheng He's voyages | Huge fleets sent across the Indian Ocean from 1405 to 1433 |
| Porcelain | Blue-and-white porcelain, prized and copied around the world |

After Zheng He, the Ming turned inward. They limited foreign trade and focused on defending the northern frontier.

## Silver and trade

China sold silk, porcelain and tea, and wanted silver in return. Much of it came from the Americas, through Spanish Manila and European traders. By the late 1500s China collected most taxes in silver, so its economy depended on a metal mined on the other side of the world.

## The Qing: a Manchu dynasty

The Manchus were a people from northeast of the Great Wall. In 1644, as rebels captured Beijing and the last Ming emperor killed himself, the Manchus swept in and founded the Qing dynasty.

The Manchus were a small minority ruling millions of Chinese. They kept the Confucian exam system and Chinese officials, but made Chinese men wear the Manchu hairstyle, a {{queue|queue}}, as a sign of loyalty.

| Qing emperor | Ruled | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Kangxi | 1661 to 1722 | Expanding the empire, supporting learning |
| Qianlong | 1735 to 1796 | The largest Qing empire, including Tibet, Xinjiang and Mongolia |

China's population grew from about 150 million around 1700 to more than 300 million by 1800, fed partly by American crops.

## "We possess all things"

Europeans wanted Chinese tea, silk and porcelain, but China wanted little that Europe made. From 1757 the Qing limited European trade to one port, Canton (Guangzhou).

In 1793 a British mission led by Lord Macartney asked for more open trade. The Qianlong emperor wrote back to King George III that China possessed all things and had no need of Britain's goods.

*Notice: in 1793 China was confident and strong. Within 50 years, Britain would use opium and warships to force it open.*

:::practice
q: How did the Manchus, a small minority, keep control of China?
answer: They kept the Chinese system of government, including the Confucian exams and Chinese officials, so educated Chinese had a stake in Qing rule. They also kept Manchu control of the army and top posts, and demanded signs of loyalty, such as the queue hairstyle. Strong early emperors and peace at home made their rule accepted.
:::
