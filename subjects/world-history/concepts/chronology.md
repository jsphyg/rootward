## One line of years

To compare events, historians need one shared way to count years. Most of the world now uses the same count. It starts from a year 1 that was meant to be the year Jesus was born.^[n_jesus_birth]

Years after that start are labeled CE. Years before it are labeled BCE.^[n_bc_ad] These two labels, {{bce-ce|BCE and CE}}, are how this site writes every date.^[n_other_calendars]

| 300 BCE | 200 BCE | 100 BCE | 1 BCE | 1 CE | 100 CE | 200 CE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ← further back | | | | | | later → |

*Notice: BCE numbers get smaller as time moves forward. There is no year 0: 1 BCE is followed directly by 1 CE.*

## Counting across the line

Working out how long ago something happened is easy when both dates are CE. From 1900 CE to 2000 CE is 100 years.

When one date is BCE and the other is CE, add the two numbers, then take away 1. You take away 1 because there is no year 0. From 200 BCE to 300 CE is 200 + 300 − 1 = 499 years.

**Why it works.** From 200 BCE to 1 BCE is 199 years. One more year gets you to 1 CE. Then 299 more get you to 300 CE. That is 199 + 1 + 299 = 499.

## Centuries

A {{century|century}} is 100 years. The 1st century CE is the years 1 to 100. The 2nd century is 101 to 200. So the century number is always one more than the "hundreds" in the year.

| Years | Century |
|---|---|
| 1 to 100 CE | 1st century CE |
| 1801 to 1900 | 19th century |
| 1901 to 2000 | 20th century |
| 500 to 401 BCE | 5th century BCE |

*Notice: the 1800s are the 19th century, not the 18th. This is the most common slip.*

## Periods and eras

Long stretches of time get names, such as "the classical world" or "the Middle Ages". Each name marks a {{period|period}} that historians think hangs together.

Three things to remember about periods:

- **People at the time did not use the names.** Nobody in 1200 thought they lived in the "Middle Ages".
- **The edges are blurry.** Change happens gradually, so historians disagree about where one period ends.
- **A period can fit one region and not another.** "The Middle Ages" is a European label. China in the same centuries had a very different story.

When an exact year is unknown, historians write "c." before it, short for {{circa|circa}}: c. 3200 BCE means "about 3200 BCE".

:::example
title: Rome was founded, by tradition, in 753 BCE. The western Roman Empire ended in 476 CE. How many years is that?

1. Notice that one date is BCE and the other is CE.
   why: The two dates are on opposite sides of year 1, so the counting-across rule applies.
   uses: []

2. Add the two numbers: 753 + 476 = 1229.
   why: 753 years lie on the BCE side and 476 on the CE side.
   uses: []

3. Take away 1: 1229 − 1 = 1228 years.
   why: There is no year 0, so simply adding counts one year too many.
   uses: []

4. Check the answer by counting in steps: 752 years from 753 BCE to 1 BCE, 1 year to 1 CE, then 475 years to 476 CE. 752 + 1 + 475 = 1228.
   why: Counting a second way catches mistakes with the missing year 0.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: The Battle of Hastings, in England, was fought in 1066. Which century is that?
answer: The **11th century**. The 11th century is the years 1001 to 1100. The hundreds in 1066 are 10, and the century number is one more: 11.
:::
