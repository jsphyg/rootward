## The Yellow River

Chinese civilization grew up along the Huang He, the Yellow River, named for the yellow soil it carries. That fine soil, called loess, is easy to farm. But the river floods violently and has often changed its course, killing huge numbers of people. It has been called "China's sorrow".

Farmers here grew millet in the north, while rice was farmed along the Yangtze River to the south.

## The Shang

The first Chinese {{dynasty|dynasty}} we know from its own writing is the Shang, which ruled from about 1600 to 1046 BCE.^[n_xia]

The Shang kings asked their ancestors for advice. Priests carved questions on {{oracle-bones|oracle bones}}, turtle shells and the shoulder blades of oxen, then heated them until they cracked. The cracks were read as answers: Will the harvest be good? Should the king go to war?

These carvings are the earliest Chinese writing, and they are the direct ancestor of the characters used in China today. That is a continuity of over 3,000 years.

The Shang were also master workers in {{bronze|bronze}}. They cast huge, richly decorated vessels for offerings to their ancestors.

## The Zhou and the Mandate of Heaven

In 1046 BCE, the Zhou people overthrew the last Shang king. They needed to explain why rebelling against a king was right. Their answer was the {{mandate-of-heaven|Mandate of Heaven}}.

Heaven, they said, gives the right to rule to a just and good ruler. If a ruler becomes cruel or careless, Heaven takes the mandate away and gives it to someone else. Floods, famines and rebellions were signs that a ruler had lost it.

The Zhou ruled longer than any other Chinese dynasty, until 256 BCE. But in its later centuries, the king's power faded and rival states fought for control. In this troubled time lived the thinker Confucius (about 551 to 479 BCE), who taught how to restore order through good government and proper behavior.

**Why it matters.** The Mandate of Heaven shaped Chinese politics for 3,000 years. Every new dynasty claimed it. It explained the rise and fall of dynasties as a cycle: a strong start, slow decline, disaster, rebellion, and a new dynasty with Heaven's blessing.

:::practice
q: Why are oracle bones such valuable sources for historians?
answer: They are primary sources written by the Shang themselves, at the time. They name kings and ancestors and show what worried the Shang: harvests, weather, war, illness. They also prove that Chinese writing is over 3,000 years old.
:::
