## The peacemakers

In January 1919 the Allies met at Paris. Germany was not invited to negotiate. The leaders had different aims.

| Leader | Country | Main aim |
|---|---|---|
| Woodrow Wilson | United States | A fair peace based on his Fourteen Points, {{self-determination|self-determination}} for nations, and a League of Nations |
| Georges Clemenceau | France | Make Germany pay and keep it too weak to attack France again |
| David Lloyd George | Britain | Punish Germany, but not so harshly that it would seek revenge |

*Notice: Wilson wanted a peace of fairness, Clemenceau a peace of security. The treaty ended up as a compromise that satisfied no one fully.*

## What Germany lost

The treaty was signed on 28 June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors at the palace of Versailles, five years to the day after the assassination in Sarajevo.

| Term | Detail |
|---|---|
| War guilt | Article 231: Germany accepts responsibility for the war's loss and damage |
| {{reparations|Reparations}} | Payments for war damage, set in 1921 at 132 billion gold marks |
| Land | Alsace-Lorraine to France; land to the new Poland, splitting Germany in two; all colonies taken |
| Military | Army limited to 100,000; no air force, no tanks, no submarines; the Rhineland, next to France, demilitarized |
| Austria | Germany and Austria forbidden to unite |

## A new map

Other treaties broke up the defeated empires.

- In Europe, new states appeared: Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Austria, Hungary, and the Baltic states.
- The Ottoman Empire's Arab lands became {{mandate|mandates}}, ruled by Britain (Iraq, Palestine, Transjordan) and France (Syria, Lebanon). Many Arabs, who had been promised independence, felt betrayed.

## The League of Nations

The League of Nations was meant to settle disputes before they became wars. But the US Senate refused to approve the treaty, so the United States never joined. Germany and the Soviet Union were at first left out. Without an army of its own, the League could do little to stop aggressive powers.

## A peace that did not last

Many Germans believed their army had not really been defeated and that they had been betrayed. They called the treaty a "Diktat," a dictated peace. The British economist John Maynard Keynes warned in 1919 that the reparations would wreck Germany's economy. Twenty years later, Europe was at war again.^[n_versailles_debate]

:::practice
q: Why was the League of Nations weak from the start?
answer: The United States, whose president had proposed it, never joined, because the US Senate rejected the treaty. Germany and the Soviet Union were at first excluded. The League had no army, so it could only ask members to apply pressure, and the strongest members often would not act. Aggressive countries could simply ignore it.
:::
