## Five weeks

:::widget timeline
28 June | Franz Ferdinand is killed in Sarajevo.
5–6 July | Germany promises Austria-Hungary its full support: the "blank cheque".
23 July | Austria-Hungary sends Serbia a list of demands with a 48-hour deadline.
25 July | Serbia accepts most of the demands, but not all. Austria-Hungary breaks off relations.
28 July | Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia.
30 July | Russia orders its whole army to prepare for war.
1 August | Germany declares war on Russia.
3 August | Germany declares war on France.
4 August | Germany invades Belgium. Britain declares war on Germany.
caption: Notice: almost four weeks of slow pressure, then one week in which every great power was drawn in.
:::

## Germany's promise

After the [[sarajevo-assassination|assassination]], Austria-Hungary wanted to punish Serbia. But attacking Serbia might bring in Russia. So Austria-Hungary first asked its ally Germany. On 5 and 6 July, Germany promised to back it whatever it did. This promise is called the {{blank-cheque|blank cheque}}.

## The ultimatum

On 23 July, Austria-Hungary sent Serbia an {{ultimatum|ultimatum}}: a list of harsh demands, with 48 hours to accept. One demand was that Austro-Hungarian officials take part in the investigation inside Serbia. The demands were written to be hard to accept.

Serbia's reply accepted most of them, but not all. Austria-Hungary called that a rejection. On 28 July it declared war.

## How two countries became many

Now the [[alliance-system|alliance system]] took over.

- **Russia** backed Serbia. On 30 July it ordered its whole army to {{mobilize|mobilize}}.
- **Germany** saw Russian mobilization as a threat. Its war plan, the Schlieffen Plan, assumed a war on two fronts. It aimed to knock out France quickly, by marching through Belgium, before Russia's huge army was ready. So Germany declared war on Russia, then on France.
- **Britain** was not bound to fight. But when Germany invaded neutral Belgium on 4 August, Britain declared war.

Who was most to blame is still argued about.^[n_blame]

**Why it moved so fast at the end.** Armies of the time took weeks to gather, and plans were built around railway timetables. Each country feared that if it waited while its enemy mobilized, it would lose the war before it began. So once one great power started to mobilize, the others felt they had to as well.

title: How the war spread from 28 July to 4 August 1914.

1. Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia.
   why: It wanted to crush Serbia, and Germany's blank cheque made it confident.
   uses: [sarajevo-assassination]

2. Russia mobilizes to support Serbia.
   why: Russia saw itself as Serbia's protector and would not let Austria-Hungary control the Balkans.
   uses: [alliance-system]

3. Germany declares war on Russia, then on France.
   why: Germany was allied to Austria-Hungary, and its war plan required striking France before Russia was ready.
   uses: [alliance-system]

4. Germany invades Belgium, and Britain declares war on Germany.
   why: The Schlieffen Plan went through Belgium. Britain had promised to respect Belgium's neutrality.

:::

:::practice
q: Russia had no alliance with Serbia. Why did it still mobilize in July 1914?
answer: Russia saw itself as the protector of the Slavic peoples, and it did not want Austria-Hungary to dominate the Balkans. It had also backed down once before, in the Bosnian Crisis of 1908 to 1909, and its leaders did not want to be humiliated again.
:::
