## From Macedonia

Macedonia was a kingdom on the northern edge of the Greek world. Its king, Philip II, built a powerful army and took control of the Greek city-states in 338 BCE.

When Philip was murdered in 336 BCE, his son Alexander became king at 20. Aristotle had been his teacher.

## Ten years of conquest

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 334 BCE | Alexander invades the Persian Empire |
| 332 to 331 BCE | He takes Egypt and founds the city of Alexandria |
| 330 BCE | The Persian Empire falls |
| 326 BCE | He reaches India; his exhausted army refuses to go further |
| 323 BCE | He dies in Babylon, aged 32 |

*Notice: in about ten years, Alexander conquered the largest empire in the world, without losing a major battle.*

He kept much of the Persian system of government. He married Roxana, a noblewoman from Central Asia, and later a daughter of the defeated Persian king. He wanted Greeks and Persians to rule together.

## The Hellenistic world

Alexander left no adult heir. His generals fought over his empire and split it into kingdoms. The biggest were the Ptolemies in Egypt and the Seleucids in Asia.

The period that followed, from 323 to 31 BCE, is called the {{hellenistic|Hellenistic}} age, from Hellas, the Greeks' name for Greece. Greek settlers lived in new cities across the conquered lands. Greek became the common language of government, trade and learning, from Egypt to Afghanistan.

Alexandria in Egypt became the greatest center of learning in the world, with a famous library. Greek ideas mixed with Egyptian, Persian and Indian ones in art, science and religion.^[n_greek_language]

**Why it matters.** Alexander's conquests spread Greek culture far beyond Greece. When Rome later conquered these lands, it absorbed a Greek-speaking world, and that is how Greek ideas reached the whole Roman Empire and, later, Europe.

:::practice
q: Alexander's empire fell apart as soon as he died. Did his conquests still matter? Explain.
answer: Yes. The empire split, but the Greek kingdoms that replaced it lasted for centuries. Greek language, cities and culture spread from Egypt to Central Asia and stayed long after. Rome inherited that Greek-speaking world, which carried Greek ideas into later history.
:::
