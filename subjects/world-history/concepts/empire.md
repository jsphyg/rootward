## One center, many peoples

The first cities ruled only themselves and their nearby fields. An {{empire|empire}} is different. It rules many peoples, with different languages, religions and customs, from one capital.

Most empires were built by conquest. The first known one was built by Sargon of Akkad in Mesopotamia, around 2300 BCE. Many followed.

| Empire | Center | At its height |
|---|---|---|
| Persian | Persia (Iran) | About 500 BCE |
| Roman | Rome | About 117 CE |
| Han | China | About 100 BCE |
| Inca | Cusco, in the Andes | About 1500 CE |

*Notice: empires grew up on every continent with cities, in very different times.*

## How empires hold together

Conquering land is one thing. Holding it is harder. Empires that lasted usually used the same set of tools:

- **An army** to defend the borders and crush revolts.
- **Officials and governors** to rule distant regions in the ruler's name.
- **Taxes or {{tribute|tribute}}** to pay for the army and the officials.
- **Roads and messengers** to move soldiers and orders quickly.
- **A shared idea**, such as a religion, a law or a belief that the ruler was chosen by the gods.
- **Some tolerance.** Letting conquered peoples keep their gods, languages and local leaders made them less likely to revolt.

## Why empires fall

Empires rarely fall for one reason. Common causes include growing too large to defend, running out of money, fights over who should rule next, revolts by conquered peoples, and attacks from outside. Usually several of these build up together.

**Why it matters.** For most of recorded history, most people lived inside empires. Empires spread languages, religions, laws and trade over huge areas. They also brought conquest, heavy taxes and rule by outsiders.

:::example
title: How a new empire turns conquered land into a working state.

1. The conquerors put governors in charge of each region.
   why: One ruler cannot run every place personally from the capital.
   uses: []

2. The governors collect taxes or tribute from the local people.
   why: The army and officials must be paid, and the empire must profit from its conquests.
   uses: []

3. The empire builds roads and a messenger system.
   why: Orders, news and soldiers must reach distant regions quickly, before a revolt can spread.
   uses: []

4. The empire leaves local customs and religions mostly alone, as long as people pay and obey.
   why: People are less likely to revolt if their daily life is not turned upside down.
   uses: [causation]
:::

:::practice
q: In 146 BCE, Rome was a republic ruled by elected officials, but it governed Italy, Spain, Greece and North Africa. Was it already an empire?
answer: Yes. An empire is a state that rules many peoples and lands from one center. Rome did that long before it had an emperor. The word "empire" is about what is ruled, not about the title of the ruler.
:::
