## The land between the rivers

"Mesopotamia" is Greek for "between the rivers". It is the flat land between the Tigris and Euphrates, mostly in modern Iraq. It is part of the Fertile Crescent, where farming began.

The rivers made the land rich, but they were hard to live with. Their floods came at unpredictable times and could be violent. Rain was scarce. Farmers needed canals and dikes to control the water, and that took organization.

## Sumer: the first cities

By about 3500 BCE, the people of Sumer, in the far south, had built the first cities: Uruk, Ur, Lagash and others. Each was a {{city-state|city-state}}, an independent city ruling its nearby farmland.

At the center of each city stood a {{ziggurat|ziggurat}}, a stepped temple tower for the city's own god. The Sumerians believed in many gods, who controlled the weather, the rivers and the harvest. Keeping them happy was the city's most important job.

The Sumerians gave the world a great deal:

| Invention | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Cuneiform writing | Records, laws, letters and stories could be kept |
| Some of the earliest wheels | First for pottery, then for carts |
| Counting in 60s | It is why an hour has 60 minutes and a circle 360 degrees |
| Written stories | The Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the oldest stories we have^[n_gilgamesh] |

## The first empire

The city-states often fought each other over water and land. Around 2300 BCE, Sargon of Akkad conquered them all. He ruled the first known {{empire|empire}}: one ruler governing many cities and peoples.

## Hammurabi's laws

Around 1754 BCE, Hammurabi, king of Babylon, had his laws carved on a stone pillar and set up in public. The code covered theft, trade, marriage, wages and injury.

Two things stand out. First, the laws were written down, so everyone could, in principle, know them. Second, punishments depended on who you were. Hurting a noble cost far more than hurting a commoner or a slave. "An eye for an eye" applied between equals.

## A crossroads, often conquered

Mesopotamia had no mountains or deserts to protect it, and its wealth attracted invaders. After the Sumerians and Akkadians came the Babylonians, the Assyrians, and later the Persians, who all ruled it in turn.

**Why it matters.** Cities, writing, written law and empire all appear here first. Later peoples, from the Hebrews to the Persians and Greeks, built on what Mesopotamia started.

:::practice
q: Why is an hour divided into 60 minutes?
answer: The Mesopotamians counted in groups of 60. Their astronomers' system was passed on to the Greeks and from them to us. So our hours, minutes and the 360 degrees of a circle all come from Mesopotamian counting.
:::
