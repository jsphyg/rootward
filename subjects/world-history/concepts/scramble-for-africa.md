## From coasts to the whole continent

For centuries Europeans had traded on Africa's coasts, including in enslaved people, but rarely ruled far inland. Disease and strong African states kept them out.

| Year | Share of Africa under European rule |
|---|---|
| 1880 | About 10 percent |
| 1914 | About 90 percent |

*Notice: the change took barely 30 years. Quinine, steamships and machine guns made it possible.*

## The Berlin Conference

As rivalry grew, the German leader Otto von Bismarck hosted European powers in Berlin from 1884 to 1885. They agreed that a country could claim African land if it actually occupied it. No Africans were invited.

Europeans drew borders with rulers on maps, often along rivers or straight lines of latitude. Some borders split peoples apart, while others joined rival peoples in one colony.

## Who took what

| Power | Main colonies |
|---|---|
| Britain | Egypt, Sudan, Nigeria, Kenya, South Africa, Rhodesia |
| France | Most of West and North Africa, including Algeria and Senegal, plus Madagascar |
| Belgium | The Congo |
| Germany | Tanganyika, Cameroon, Togo, South West Africa (Namibia) |
| Portugal | Angola, Mozambique |
| Italy | Libya, Eritrea, Somalia |

## Resistance

Africans fought back hard.

- In 1879 a Zulu army destroyed a British force at Isandlwana, though Britain went on to conquer the Zulu kingdom.
- In 1896 Ethiopia's emperor, Menelik II, crushed an Italian army at Adwa. Ethiopia stayed independent.
- Revolts such as the Maji Maji rising in German East Africa, from 1905 to 1907, were put down at terrible cost. In South West Africa, German forces killed most of the Herero and many of the Nama peoples from 1904 to 1908, an act now recognized as genocide.

## The Congo horror

King Leopold II of Belgium ruled the Congo Free State from 1885 to 1908 as his personal property. His agents forced Congolese people to collect wild rubber, taking hostages and cutting off hands to enforce quotas. Huge numbers died from violence, hunger and disease.^[n_congo] International outrage forced Belgium's government to take over in 1908.

## Lasting effects

Colonial economies were built to send raw materials to Europe, with railways running from mines to ports rather than linking African regions. When most African countries won independence in the 1950s and 1960s, they kept the colonial borders, along with the problems some of those borders caused.

:::practice
q: Why do many African countries today have borders that cut across ethnic groups?
answer: The borders were drawn by European powers in the 1880s and 1890s to suit their own claims, often using rivers or straight lines on maps, with no Africans consulted. Some peoples were split between colonies while rival groups were joined in one. When African countries became independent, they mostly kept these colonial borders to avoid wars over redrawing them.
