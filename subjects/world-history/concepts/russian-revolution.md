## A country at breaking point

In 1914 Russia was ruled by a tsar, Nicholas II, with almost unlimited power. Most people were poor peasants. A small but growing number worked in factories in cities such as Petrograd, the capital, and Moscow.

World War I was a disaster for Russia. By 1917 millions of soldiers had been killed, wounded or captured. Trains carried soldiers instead of food, and the cities went hungry.

## Two revolutions in one year^[n_russian_calendar]

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| February 1917 | Strikes and bread riots in Petrograd. Soldiers refuse to fire on the crowds and join them. The tsar gives up his throne |
| 1917 | A Provisional Government takes over but keeps fighting the war. Workers' and soldiers' councils, called {{soviet|soviets}}, also claim power |
| April 1917 | Lenin, a Marxist exile, returns with Germany's help and demands "Peace, Land, Bread" |
| October 1917 | Lenin's {{bolshevik|Bolsheviks}} seize power in Petrograd |

*Notice: the first revolution was a popular uprising that no party controlled. The second was a planned takeover by a small, disciplined party.*

## Civil war and a new state

The Bolsheviks made peace with Germany in March 1918, giving up huge areas of land. Opponents, called the Whites, fought them in a civil war from 1918 to 1921. The Bolsheviks, or Reds, won, but millions died from fighting, hunger and disease. The tsar and his family were shot in 1918.

In 1922 the Bolsheviks, now called Communists, founded the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics, the USSR or Soviet Union. It was a one-party state, with no free press or elections.

## Stalin

After Lenin died in 1924, Joseph Stalin took control.

| Policy | What it did | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Five-Year Plans, from 1928 | Built steel mills, dams and factories at great speed | Brutal working conditions; forced labor |
| Collectivization | Forced peasants onto huge state-run farms | Famine in 1932 to 1933 killed millions, especially in Ukraine^[n_soviet_famine] |
| The Great Terror, 1936 to 1938 | Purged suspected enemies, including many loyal Communists | About 700,000 shot; millions sent to labor camps called the {{gulag|Gulag}} |

*Notice: Stalin turned the Soviet Union into an industrial power that would help defeat Nazi Germany, but at the cost of millions of lives.*

:::practice
q: Why was Russia an unexpected place for the first communist revolution?
answer: Marx predicted that communist revolution would come in the most industrial countries, where factory workers were the majority. Russia was mostly a country of peasant farmers, with only a small industrial working class. It took the strain of World War I and Lenin's tightly organized party to make revolution happen there first.
:::
