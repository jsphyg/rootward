## Two camps

Before 1914, Europe's {{great-powers|great powers}} signed a series of agreements. Step by step, these sorted them into two camps. Many of the quarrels they settled, or sharpened, were about colonies: Britain and France had nearly fought over Africa in 1898, and Britain and Russia had long competed in Central Asia.

:::widget timeline
1879 | Germany and Austria-Hungary form the Dual Alliance.
1882 | Italy joins them: the Triple Alliance.
1894 | France and Russia form an alliance.
1904 | Britain and France settle old quarrels: the Entente Cordiale.
1907 | Britain and Russia settle their quarrels in Asia.
caption: Notice: Germany's camp was complete by 1882. The other camp took shape between 1894 and 1907.
:::

## A promise, or just an understanding?

The two camps were not built the same way.

An {{alliance|alliance}} is a treaty promise to help each other in war. Germany and Austria-Hungary had one. So did France and Russia.

An {{entente|entente}} is a friendly understanding. It settles quarrels, but it does not promise to fight. Britain's ties to France and Russia were ententes. So in 1914, Britain was not forced to join a war. It had to decide.

What decided it was Belgium. Britain had signed a treaty promising to respect Belgium's {{neutrality|neutrality}}.^[n_belgium_1839] When Germany invaded Belgium, Britain declared war.

Italy shows the opposite case. It was in the Triple Alliance, but it did not fight with its partners in 1914.^[n_italy]

## Why alliances made war bigger

Each alliance was meant to keep the peace. The idea was simple: no one would attack a country with strong friends.

But the alliances also meant that a war between two countries would not stay between two countries. If one member was at war, its partners could be pulled in, and then their enemies' partners too. This is how the [[july-crisis|July Crisis]] of 1914 spread.

:::example
title: Suppose Austria-Hungary attacks Serbia. Who might be pulled in?

1. Russia might step in to protect Serbia.
   why: Russia had no treaty with Serbia, but it saw itself as the protector of Slavic peoples and did not want Austria-Hungary to control the Balkans.
   uses: []

2. If Russia fights Austria-Hungary, Germany backs its ally Austria-Hungary.
   why: The Dual Alliance of 1879 tied Germany to Austria-Hungary.
   uses: []

3. If Germany fights Russia, France is likely to fight Germany.
   why: France and Russia were allies. France also wanted back the land it had lost to Germany in 1871.
   uses: []

4. Britain is not bound to join. It must choose.
   why: Britain's ties to France and Russia were ententes, not alliances. Only something like an attack on Belgium gave it a clear reason.
   uses: []
:::

:::practice
q: The alliances were meant to prevent war. Why did they make it harder to keep a war small?
answer: Each alliance promised help in war, so a war between two countries could pull in their partners, and then their enemies' partners. A local quarrel could quickly become a war among all the great powers.
:::
