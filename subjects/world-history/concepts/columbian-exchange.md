## Two worlds meet

For thousands of years, the Americas and the rest of the world had developed separately, with different plants, animals and diseases. After 1492, ships began carrying all of these back and forth. Historians call this the Columbian Exchange.^[n_crosby]

| From the Americas to the world | From the world to the Americas |
|---|---|
| Maize (corn), potatoes, sweet potatoes, cassava | Wheat, rice, barley |
| Tomatoes, chili peppers, beans, squash | Sugar cane, bananas, coffee |
| Cacao (chocolate), tobacco, peanuts | Horses, cattle, pigs, sheep, chickens |
| Turkeys | Smallpox, measles, flu, malaria |

*Notice: many foods we think of as traditional, such as Italian tomato sauce, Irish potatoes or Indian chili, only became possible after 1492.*

## Feeding the world

American crops changed diets everywhere.

- Potatoes grow well in cool, poor soil and give a lot of food per field. They helped Europe's population grow from the 1700s. Ireland came to depend on them so much that when a disease ruined the crop in the 1840s, about a million people died in the famine.
- Maize and sweet potatoes could grow on hillsides in China where rice could not. They helped China's population more than double between 1700 and 1850.
- Cassava and maize became basic foods in much of Africa.

## Changing the Americas

Old World animals transformed the Americas. Horses, which had died out there thousands of years before, changed life for peoples of the Great Plains, who became mounted buffalo hunters. Cattle and sheep spread over vast grasslands.

But the exchange was most deadly in the Americas. Diseases from Europe and Africa killed far more native people than any war.

## Sugar and slavery

Sugar cane grew well in the hot Caribbean and Brazil. Europeans built huge sugar plantations there. With native populations collapsing, planters turned to enslaved Africans for labor. The exchange of crops was tied directly to the exchange of people by force.

## Silver goes global

American silver, especially from Potosí, flowed to Europe and, from 1565, across the Pacific on Spanish ships to Manila in the Philippines. There it was traded for Chinese silk and porcelain. China needed huge amounts of silver for its money. For the first time, trade linked every inhabited continent.

:::practice
q: How was the Columbian Exchange connected to the Atlantic slave trade?
answer: Sugar cane, brought from the Old World, grew well in the Caribbean and Brazil, and Europeans built vast sugar plantations there. Diseases from the Old World had killed most of the native people who might have been forced to work. So planters bought enslaved Africans in huge numbers to grow sugar, linking the exchange of crops to the forced movement of people.
:::
