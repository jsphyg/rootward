## A closed country

From the early 1600s, Japan was ruled by the {{shogun|shoguns}} of the Tokugawa family, military rulers who governed in the name of a powerless emperor. In the 1630s they banned Christianity and almost all foreign contact. Only a few Dutch and Chinese traders could visit, at the port of Nagasaki.

## The black ships

In 1853 the American Commodore Matthew Perry sailed into Tokyo Bay with steam-powered warships, which the Japanese called "black ships." He demanded that Japan open its ports. Japan, with no navy to match them, agreed in 1854 and soon signed unequal treaties like China's.

Many Japanese were furious that the shogun had given in. They had seen what happened to China.

## The Meiji Restoration

In 1868 rebels, mostly young samurai, overthrew the shogun and announced that the emperor would rule again. The emperor took the name Meiji, meaning "enlightened rule." This is called the Meiji {{restoration|Restoration}}, though in practice a small group of leaders ruled in his name.

Their slogan was "Rich country, strong army."

| Reform | What happened |
|---|---|
| End of the samurai | The old warrior class lost its privileges; a modern army was drafted from all classes from 1873 |
| Learning from the West | Officials toured America and Europe; experts were hired from abroad |
| Industry | The government built railways, from 1872, shipyards, mines and factories, then sold many to private companies |
| Education | Schooling for all children; by 1900 most children attended school |
| Constitution | A constitution in 1889, modeled on Germany's, with an elected parliament but a powerful emperor |

*Notice: Japan did not simply copy the West. It chose what it needed from different countries and kept its own traditions and loyalty to the emperor.*

## An empire of its own

Japan used its new strength to become an imperial power.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1894 to 1895 | Japan defeats China and takes Taiwan |
| 1902 | Alliance with Britain |
| 1904 to 1905 | Japan defeats Russia, the first time in modern times an Asian country beat a European great power in a major war |
| 1910 | Japan takes over Korea as a colony |

By 1911 Japan had won the end of the unequal treaties. It was now treated as a great power, but its growing empire would bring it into conflict with China and, later, the United States.

:::practice
q: Why was Japan's victory over Russia in 1905 so important?
answer: It was the first time in modern history that an Asian country defeated a European great power in a major war. It showed that industrial and military strength was not only for Western nations. It made Japan a recognized great power and inspired nationalists across Asia who hoped to resist European rule.
