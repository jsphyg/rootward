## An empire in retreat

For centuries the Ottoman Empire ruled the Balkans, the mountainous peninsula of southeastern Europe. In the 1800s its hold weakened. One by one, Balkan peoples won independence: Greece, then Serbia, Romania and Bulgaria.

As the Ottomans pulled back, two great empires moved in to fill the gap.

## Two empires watching

**Russia** saw itself as the protector of the Slavic and Orthodox Christian peoples of the Balkans. This idea was called {{pan-slavism|Pan-Slavism}}. Russia also wanted influence in the region.

**Austria-Hungary** feared the opposite. Every gain for Serbia made South Slav [[nationalism|nationalism]] inside its own borders stronger.

So the two empires watched each other in the Balkans. Each saw the other's gain as its own loss.

## Three crises

:::widget timeline
1878 | Serbia becomes fully independent. Austria-Hungary is allowed to occupy Bosnia, which stays officially Ottoman.
1908 | Austria-Hungary formally takes over Bosnia. Serbia and Russia protest, but Russia backs down in 1909.
1912 | First Balkan War: Serbia, Bulgaria, Greece and Montenegro drive the Ottomans almost entirely out of Europe.
1913 | Second Balkan War: Bulgaria fights its former allies and loses. Serbia gains more land.
caption: Notice: in five years Serbia went from humiliated to victorious. Across the two Balkan Wars it nearly doubled in size.
:::

In 1908 Austria-Hungary decided to {{annex|annex}} Bosnia (in full, Bosnia and Herzegovina; this page says Bosnia for short). It had run Bosnia for thirty years, but now made it officially its own. Serbs were the largest group in Bosnia, and Serbia had hoped to gain it one day. Russia protested, then backed down when Germany firmly supported Austria-Hungary.^[n_russia_1905]

The Balkan Wars of 1912 and 1913 left Serbia bigger, more confident and more popular with South Slavs inside Austria-Hungary. To many in Vienna, Serbia now looked like a real danger.

**Why the region was so explosive.** Several things met in one place. A collapsing empire left land to fight over. Young nations wanted to grow. Two great powers each feared the other would win. Any spark there could involve the great powers.

:::practice
q: Serbia did not fight Austria-Hungary in 1912 or 1913. Why did its victories in those years still worry Austria-Hungary?
answer: The victories made Serbia bigger and more attractive to the South Slavs living inside Austria-Hungary. The stronger Serbia grew, the more likely those South Slavs were to want to leave the empire and join it.
