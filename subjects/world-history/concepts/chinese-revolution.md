## A broken country

After the century of humiliation that began with the Opium Wars, revolutionaries overthrew the Qing dynasty in 1911 to 1912. Sun Yat-sen, a revolutionary leader, hoped to build a modern republic. Instead, China split among regional military rulers called warlords.

In 1919 students protested against foreign control of China, in the May Fourth Movement. Many young Chinese turned to new ideas, including Marxism, after the Russian Revolution. The Chinese Communist Party was founded in 1921.

## Nationalists and Communists

| | Nationalists (Guomindang) | Communists |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | Chiang Kai-shek | Mao Zedong |
| Support | Cities, business, landlords, the army | Peasants in the countryside |
| Aim | A unified, modern China under one party | A communist revolution led by peasants |

The two parties first worked together, then in 1927 Chiang turned on the Communists and killed thousands of them.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1934 to 1935 | The Long March: Communist forces escape the Nationalists on a 10,000 km retreat; Mao becomes leader |
| 1937 to 1945 | Japan invades China. Both parties fight Japan, and the Communists win peasant support |
| 1946 to 1949 | Civil war. The Communists win; Chiang's Nationalists flee to Taiwan |
| 1 October 1949 | Mao proclaims the People's Republic of China |

*Notice: unlike Marx and Lenin, Mao built his revolution on peasants, not factory workers, because most Chinese were farmers.*

## Mao's China

| Campaign | Dates | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Land reform | Early 1950s | Land taken from landlords and given to peasants; hundreds of thousands of landlords, or more, were killed |
| The Great Leap Forward | 1958 to 1962 | A drive to industrialize fast, with giant communes and backyard furnaces. Grain was taken for the state even as harvests failed. The famine killed tens of millions^[n_great_leap] |
| The {{cultural-revolution|Cultural Revolution}} | 1966 to 1976 | Mao urged young Red Guards to attack "old ideas" and his rivals. Teachers, officials and artists were beaten, imprisoned or killed; schools closed |

## Reform

After Mao's death in 1976, Deng Xiaoping became China's leader. From 1978 he allowed farmers to sell their crops, opened China to foreign trade and investment, and let private businesses grow, while the Communist Party kept total political control. When students demanded democracy in Tiananmen Square in Beijing in 1989, the army crushed the protest.

China's economy then grew faster than any large economy in history. Hundreds of millions of people rose out of poverty.

:::practice
q: How did Deng Xiaoping change China after Mao?
answer: Deng kept the Communist Party in full political control but changed the economy. He let farmers sell their crops, allowed private businesses, and opened China to foreign trade and investment. The economy grew extremely fast and lifted hundreds of millions out of poverty, though China stayed a one-party state, as the crushing of the 1989 protests showed.
