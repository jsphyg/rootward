## The Mauryan Empire

Around 321 BCE, soon after Alexander the Great had reached India's edge, a ruler named Chandragupta Maurya built an empire across northern India. His grandson, Ashoka, extended it over almost the whole subcontinent.

## Ashoka's change of heart

Around 260 BCE, Ashoka conquered the kingdom of Kalinga, on India's east coast. The war was terribly bloody: by his own account, over 100,000 people were killed. Ashoka was filled with remorse.

He turned to Buddhism and changed how he ruled. He had his messages carved on pillars and rocks across the empire. They urged nonviolence, kindness to animals, respect for parents and tolerance of all religions. Many of these {{edicts|edicts}} survive and can still be read.

He also sent Buddhist missionaries abroad, including to Sri Lanka, which helped Buddhism become a world religion.

The empire broke apart about 50 years after his death.

## The Gupta golden age

Around 320 CE, the Gupta dynasty reunited much of northern India. The Gupta period is often called a golden age.

| Field | Achievement |
|---|---|
| Mathematics | The decimal place-value system with a zero, the basis of the numbers used worldwide today^[n_numerals] |
| Astronomy | The astronomer Aryabhata argued that the earth spins on its axis |
| Literature | The poet Kalidasa; classic Sanskrit writing |
| Religion | Hinduism flourished, and Hindu temples were built in stone |

*Notice: numbers we call "Arabic numerals" actually began in India; Arab scholars passed them to Europe.*

The Gupta Empire weakened under attacks by the Huns from Central Asia and broke up by about 550 CE.

**Why it matters.** Ashoka is remembered as a ruler who tried to govern by moral principles, not only force. The Gupta age gave the world the number system it still uses.

:::practice
q: Why are Ashoka's edicts such valuable sources for historians?
answer: They are primary sources in the emperor's own words, carved at the time and still in place across India. They show what he wanted his subjects to believe, how he saw his own rule, and how far his empire reached.
