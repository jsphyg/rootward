## Rome lives on in the east

When the western Roman Empire fell in 476, the eastern half carried on. Its people still called themselves Romans. Historians call it the Byzantine Empire, after Byzantium, the old Greek town on the site of its capital.^[n_byzantine_name]

The emperor Constantine had rebuilt Byzantium as a new capital and named it Constantinople in 330 CE. Today it is Istanbul, in Turkey. The city sat on a narrow strait between Europe and Asia. Water protected it on three sides, and huge walls on the fourth. Trade between Europe, Asia and the Black Sea passed its harbor, which made it rich.

## Justinian's empire

The most famous Byzantine emperor was Justinian, who ruled from 527 to 565.

| Achievement | What it was |
|---|---|
| Law | He had centuries of Roman laws collected into one code. It later shaped the law of much of Europe |
| Building | He built the Hagia Sophia, a huge domed church finished in 537 |
| War | His generals won back Italy, North Africa and part of Spain from Germanic kingdoms |

*Notice: Justinian tried to rebuild the old Roman Empire. The reconquests were costly, and a terrible plague in 541 killed a large share of the population. Much of Italy was lost to new invaders, the Lombards, within a few years of his death.*

## Greek and Orthodox

Over time the empire became less Roman and more Greek. Greek replaced Latin as the language of government. The emperor was seen as God's representative on earth, and ran the Church as well as the state.

Christians in the east and west slowly drifted apart. They disagreed about who led the Church and about religious practices, such as the use of {{icon|icons}}. In 1054 church leaders in Rome and Constantinople excommunicated each other. This split, the {{schism|Great Schism}}, divided Christianity into the Roman Catholic Church in the west and the {{orthodox|Eastern Orthodox Church}} in the east. It has lasted to this day.

## A long decline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 630s to 640s | Arab Muslim armies take Syria, Palestine and Egypt |
| 1071 | The Seljuk Turks defeat a Byzantine army at Manzikert and move into Anatolia |
| 1204 | Western crusaders capture and loot Constantinople |
| 1261 | The Byzantines win back their capital, but the empire is now small and weak |
| 1453 | The Ottoman Turks capture Constantinople. The empire ends |

*Notice: the empire was worn down over 800 years, from many directions. Its final blow came from the Ottomans, but some of the worst damage was done by fellow Christians in 1204.*

## Why it matters

The Byzantines kept ancient Greek books safe while much of the west lost them. Greek scholars who fled to Italy before and after 1453 brought those books with them.

They also spread Orthodox Christianity north. Two Byzantine missionaries, Cyril and Methodius, created an alphabet for Slavic languages in the 800s. Their followers developed it into the Cyrillic alphabet, still used in Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian and Bulgarian.

:::practice
q: Why do some historians say the Roman Empire did not really end until 1453?
answer: The Byzantine Empire was the eastern half of the Roman Empire, not a new state. Its rulers were Roman emperors, its people called themselves Romans, and it kept Roman law. Only the western half fell in 476, so the Roman Empire, in this view, ended when the Ottomans captured Constantinople in 1453.
:::
