## The prince who left home

Siddhartha Gautama was born, according to tradition, a prince in what is now Nepal, probably in the 400s or 500s BCE.^[n_buddha_dates] Raised in luxury, he was shocked when he finally saw old age, sickness and death. He left his palace to search for the answer to suffering.

After years of trying extreme self-denial, he chose a "middle way" between luxury and harsh denial. Meditating under a tree, he reached enlightenment, a full understanding of the truth. From then on he was called the Buddha, "the awakened one". He spent the rest of his life teaching.

## The Four Noble Truths

| Truth | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1. Suffering | Life involves suffering and dissatisfaction |
| 2. Its cause | Suffering comes from craving and attachment |
| 3. Its end | Suffering can end, if craving ends |
| 4. The path | The way to end it is the Eightfold Path |

*Notice: the truths are set out like a doctor's diagnosis: the illness, its cause, the cure, and the treatment.*

The Eightfold Path is a guide to right understanding, right speech, right action and right concentration, among others. Following it leads to {{nirvana|nirvana}}: the end of craving, of suffering and of the cycle of rebirth.

## From Hinduism, and against it

The Buddha kept Hindu ideas of [[hinduism|karma and rebirth]]. But he rejected the authority of the Vedas, the power of priests and the caste system. Anyone, of any caste, man or woman, could follow his path. Communities of monks and nuns, the {{sangha|sangha}}, spread his teachings.

## Spreading across Asia

Buddhism spread widely, helped by the Indian emperor Ashoka in the 200s BCE and by traders on the Silk Roads. It reached Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, China, Korea, Japan and Tibet.

Strangely, it later faded in India, its birthplace, where Hinduism remained strong.

**Why it matters.** Buddhism became one of the great world religions, shaping the art, thought and daily life of much of Asia. Today it has around half a billion followers.
