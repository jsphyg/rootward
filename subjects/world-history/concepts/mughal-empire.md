## Founding the empire

The Mughals came from Central Asia. Their founder, Babur, was descended from both Genghis Khan and Timur, a conqueror of the late 1300s. "Mughal" comes from the Persian word for Mongol.

In 1526 Babur invaded northern India. At the Battle of Panipat, his smaller army used cannons and muskets to defeat the much larger army of the Sultan of Delhi.

## The great emperors

| Emperor | Ruled | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | 1526 to 1530 | Founding the empire |
| Akbar | 1556 to 1605 | Expansion, strong government, religious tolerance |
| Jahangir | 1605 to 1627 | Art and painting |
| Shah Jahan | 1628 to 1658 | Building the Taj Mahal |
| Aurangzeb | 1658 to 1707 | The largest empire, but endless wars and religious strictness |

## Akbar the Great

Akbar became emperor at 13. A Muslim ruling a land where most people were Hindu, he chose to win them over.

- He ended the {{jizya|jizya}}, the tax on non-Muslims.
- He married a Hindu Rajput princess and gave Hindu nobles high posts in the army and government.
- He invited Muslim, Hindu, Christian, Jain and Zoroastrian scholars to debate religion at his court.
- He built an efficient government, with officials ranked and paid by the state.

*Notice: like the Ottomans, the Mughals ruled a mix of faiths. Akbar's tolerance made his rule stronger, not weaker.*

## Wealth and beauty

Mughal India was one of the richest places on earth, with perhaps 100 to 150 million people.^[n_mughal_wealth] Its cotton cloth was sold across Asia, Africa and Europe.

Mughal art blended Persian, Indian and Islamic styles. Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal in Agra as a tomb for his wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631. It took more than 20 years and about 20,000 workers.

In the same era, a new religion grew in the Punjab. {{sikhism|Sikhism}}, founded by Guru Nanak, who lived from 1469 to 1539, teaches devotion to one God and the equality of all people.

## Decline

Aurangzeb conquered almost all of India but spent decades at war in the south. He brought back the jizya and destroyed some Hindu temples, turning many subjects against him. After he died in 1707, governors and rival powers broke away. The British East India Company took Bengal after the Battle of Plassey in 1757, and the British removed the last Mughal emperor in 1858.

:::practice
q: Why were the Mughals able to defeat the much larger army of the Sultan of Delhi at Panipat in 1526?
answer: Babur's army used gunpowder weapons, cannons and muskets, which the Sultan's army, relying on soldiers and war elephants, could not match. Babur also used skilled Central Asian cavalry tactics, attacking from the sides. New weapons and better tactics beat greater numbers.
:::
