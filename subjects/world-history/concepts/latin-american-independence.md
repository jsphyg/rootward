## A rigid social order

Spain's American colonies were organized by birth and ancestry.

| Group | Who | Place |
|---|---|---|
| Peninsulares | Born in Spain | Top government and church jobs |
| {{creole|Creoles}} | European descent, born in the Americas | Often rich landowners, but shut out of top posts |
| Mestizos | Mixed European and native ancestry | Small farmers, workers, artisans |
| Native peoples | Descendants of the original inhabitants | Paid tribute; many did forced labor |
| Enslaved Africans and their descendants | Brought by force | At the bottom |

Creoles had read Enlightenment writers and watched the American and French revolutions. Many resented being ruled from Spain.

## The spark: Napoleon

In 1808 Napoleon invaded Spain and put his brother on the throne. With no legitimate Spanish king, colonial cities set up their own governments. What began as loyalty to the captured king turned into a fight for independence.

## Independence won

| Region | Leader | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico | Father Miguel Hidalgo, then others | Hidalgo, a priest, called native and mestizo peasants to revolt in 1810. He was captured and executed in 1811. Mexico won independence in 1821 |
| Northern South America | Simón Bolívar | Freed Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, then helped free Peru and Bolivia, which is named after him |
| Southern South America | José de San Martín | Freed Argentina, crossed the Andes to free Chile in 1818, then entered Peru |
| Brazil | Prince Pedro | When the Portuguese court went home, Pedro stayed and declared Brazil independent in 1822, becoming its emperor |

The last big Spanish army in South America was defeated at Ayacucho, in Peru, in 1824. By 1826, of Spain's American empire, only Cuba and Puerto Rico remained.

*Notice: Brazil's path was different. Its royal family had fled there from Napoleon in 1808, so independence came with little fighting, under a monarchy.*

## Freedom for whom?

Bolívar dreamed of one great South American nation, but it split apart. Gran Colombia broke into Venezuela, Colombia and Ecuador by 1831. Many new countries were ruled by military strongmen called caudillos.

Independence mostly moved power from Spanish-born officials to creole landowners. Native peoples and the poor saw little change. Slavery was abolished in most Spanish-speaking countries by the 1850s, but Brazil kept it until 1888.

:::practice
q: How was independence in Brazil different from independence in Spanish America?
answer: In 1808 the Portuguese royal family fled Napoleon and moved to Brazil, making Rio de Janeiro the capital of the empire. When the king returned to Portugal, his son Pedro stayed and declared Brazil independent in 1822, with little fighting. Brazil became a single empire with its own emperor, while Spanish America broke into many republics after long wars.
:::
