## A disease on the move

The Black Death was {{plague|plague}}, a disease caused by bacteria. Fleas carried it, living on rats and other animals, and bit humans. Victims got fever and painful black swellings, and most died within days.

The plague started in Central Asia in the 1330s. It moved west along the busy trade routes of the Mongol world.

| Year | Where the plague struck |
|---|---|
| 1346 | The Black Sea port of Caffa, under siege by a Mongol army^[n_caffa] |
| 1347 | Italian ships carry it to Sicily, then Constantinople and Egypt |
| 1348 | Italy, France, Spain and England |
| 1349 to 1351 | Northern Europe and Russia |

*Notice: the same trade routes that the Mongol peace made safe carried the disease fast and far.*

## How many died

No one counted, so all numbers are estimates. Historians think that between 1347 and 1351 the plague killed between a third and a half of Europe's people, perhaps 25 to 50 million. Some towns lost more than half their people. It killed huge numbers in the Middle East and North Africa too. Plague returned again and again for centuries after.

## How people reacted

No one knew what caused the plague. People blamed bad air, the position of the stars, or God's anger at sin.

- Some groups, the flagellants, marched from town to town whipping themselves to beg for God's mercy.
- In many towns, Jews were falsely accused of poisoning wells. Thousands were murdered, for example in Strasbourg in 1349.
- Priests died at the same rate as everyone else, and prayers did not stop the dying. Some people began to doubt the Church.

## A changed society

The plague hit the feudal system hard.

| Before the plague | After the plague |
|---|---|
| Many peasants, not enough land | Plenty of land, not enough peasants |
| Lords could demand work from serfs | Workers could move to whoever paid most |
| Low wages | Wages rose, despite laws trying to stop them |

Lords tried to force workers back. In England a law of 1351 fixed wages at pre-plague levels, and anger over this and new taxes led to the Peasants' Revolt of 1381. The revolt was crushed, but over the next century serfdom faded away in western Europe.

*Notice: by killing so many, the plague gave the survivors more bargaining power.*

:::practice
q: How did the Black Death help weaken serfdom in western Europe?
answer: It killed so many peasants that lords had too few workers for their land. Survivors could demand higher wages or leave for lords who offered better terms. Laws trying to freeze wages and force people back to work failed and led to revolts. Over the following century, lords had to free serfs or turn them into paid workers and tenants.
:::
