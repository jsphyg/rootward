## A religion without a founder

Most major religions trace back to one founder. Hinduism does not. It grew slowly in India over more than 3,000 years, mixing many traditions.

Its oldest scriptures are the {{vedas|Vedas}}, hymns and rituals composed in the ancient language Sanskrit, beginning around 1500 BCE. They were passed down by memory for centuries before being written down. Later texts include the Upanishads, which explore the nature of reality, and great epic poems such as the Ramayana and the Mahabharata.^[n_hindu_word]

Whether any Hindu beliefs go back to the Indus Valley civilization is uncertain, because its writing cannot be read.

## Many gods, one reality

Hindus worship many gods and goddesses, such as Vishnu, Shiva and Lakshmi. Many Hindus see them all as forms of one ultimate reality, called Brahman.

## Karma, rebirth and release

Three ideas are at the heart of Hindu belief:

| Idea | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reincarnation | When a person dies, their soul is reborn in a new body |
| {{karma|Karma}} | Good and bad actions shape what happens to you, in this life and the next |
| {{dharma|Dharma}} | Each person's duty: the right way to live for someone of their place in life |

*Notice: the three ideas fit together. Doing your dharma creates good karma, which leads to a better rebirth.*

The final goal is moksha: release from the cycle of rebirth, when the soul unites with Brahman.

## Caste

Hinduism became tied to a social order of {{caste|castes}}: groups that people were born into, which decided their job, whom they could marry and how others treated them. The Vedas describe four broad classes: priests, warriors and rulers, merchants and farmers, and laborers. Below them, some groups were treated as "untouchable". Discrimination by caste was outlawed in India's constitution in 1950, though its effects remain.

**Why it matters.** Hinduism is followed today by over a billion people, most of them in India. Its ideas of karma and rebirth also shaped Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, which grew up in India too.

:::practice
q: How is Hinduism different from Judaism in how it began?
answer: Judaism traces itself back to particular founders, Abraham and Moses, and a covenant with one God. Hinduism has no single founder or moment of beginning. It grew gradually over thousands of years from many traditions, and many Hindus worship many gods as forms of one reality.
