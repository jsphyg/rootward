## A rebirth

"Renaissance" is French for rebirth.^[n_renaissance_word] Italian scholars of the 1300s felt that the great culture of ancient Rome had been lost for a thousand years, and that their own age was bringing it back to life.

## Why Italy?

| Reason | What it meant |
|---|---|
| Rich cities | Florence, Venice and Milan grew wealthy from trade and banking |
| Roman ruins | Reminders of ancient greatness stood all around |
| Patrons | Rich families such as the Medici of Florence paid artists and scholars, to show off their wealth and power |
| Greek scholars | Byzantine scholars came to Italy, especially as the Ottomans closed in on Constantinople, bringing Greek books and teaching Greek |

The Black Death may have played a part too. It shook people's faith in old ways of thinking, and some survivors inherited large fortunes to spend.

*Notice: the Renaissance needed money. Without wealthy patrons, few artists could have worked full time.*

## Humanism

The scholars at the heart of the Renaissance were {{humanism|humanists}}. They hunted for old Latin and Greek manuscripts in monastery libraries and studied them closely. They believed education in history, poetry and philosophy made people better citizens. Most were still Christians, but they put new value on human talent and life in this world.

## A new kind of art

| Artist | Famous work |
|---|---|
| Leonardo da Vinci (1452 to 1519) | The Mona Lisa and The Last Supper; notebooks full of inventions and anatomy |
| Michelangelo (1475 to 1564) | The statue of David; the painted ceiling of the Sistine Chapel in Rome |
| Raphael (1483 to 1520) | The School of Athens, showing ancient Greek thinkers |

Artists used {{perspective|perspective}} to make flat paintings look deep and real. They studied the human body, even dissecting corpses, so their figures looked alive.

## Printing spreads it all

Around 1450, Johannes Gutenberg in Germany built a printing press using metal {{movable-type|movable type}}. A book that had taken a scribe months to copy could now be printed in hundreds of copies. By 1500, presses across Europe had printed millions of books. Ideas could now spread faster than ever, and more people learned to read.

The Renaissance moved north in the 1500s, to thinkers such as Erasmus in the Netherlands and later writers such as Shakespeare in England.

:::example

   why: A scholar's new idea in Italy could be read in England or Germany within months.

   why: Each step fed the next, so Renaissance learning reached far beyond Italy.

:::

:::practice
q: How did the fall of the Byzantine Empire help the Italian Renaissance?
answer: As the Ottomans closed in on Constantinople, and after it fell in 1453, Byzantine scholars fled to Italy, bringing ancient Greek books that western Europe had largely lost. They taught Greek to Italian humanists, who could then read Plato and other Greek writers in the original. This gave Renaissance learning much of its material.
:::
