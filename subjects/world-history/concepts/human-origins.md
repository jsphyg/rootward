## A very long story

Human history before farming is enormous. For this stretch, historians usually count in "years ago" instead of BCE, because the numbers are so large and so rough. 300,000 years ago is about 298,000 BCE, but no one knows the date that closely.

| How long ago | What happened |
|---|---|
| About 2 million years | Earlier human species, such as Homo erectus, spread out of Africa |
| About 300,000 years | Modern humans appear in Africa |
| About 70,000 to 50,000 years | Modern humans spread out of Africa |
| At least 50,000 years | People reach Australia, by boat |
| About 45,000 years | Modern humans reach Europe |
| At least 15,000 years | People reach the Americas |
| About 12,000 years | Farming begins |

*Notice: farming, cities and writing fill only the last few percent of the human story.*

## Who the first humans were

We belong to one species, {{homo-sapiens|Homo sapiens}}. For most of our past we shared the world with other kinds of humans, such as the Neanderthals of Europe and western Asia. They died out about 40,000 years ago, but not completely: many people today carry a little Neanderthal DNA.^[n_neanderthal]

## How they lived

For more than 95 percent of human history, everyone was a {{hunter-gatherer|hunter-gatherer}}. People lived in small bands, moved with the seasons and got food by hunting, fishing and gathering plants.

This was the {{stone-age|Old Stone Age}}. People made stone tools, controlled fire, made clothing and painted. The paintings in Chauvet Cave in France are about 36,000 years old.

## Spreading across the world

Small groups moved, generation by generation, following animals, coasts and better land. During the {{ice-age|Ice Age}}, so much water was frozen in ice sheets that the seas were lower. Land appeared where there is sea today, which opened routes between continents.

**Why it matters.** This slow spread put people on every continent except Antarctica. When the ice melted and the seas rose, some groups were cut off from others for thousands of years. That is one reason the Americas and the rest of the world developed separately until 1492.

:::example
title: How did people reach the Americas?

1. During the Ice Age, vast ice sheets locked up water, and sea level fell by over 100 meters.
   why: Water frozen on land is water missing from the oceans.
   uses: []

2. A wide land bridge, now called Beringia, joined Siberia to Alaska.
   why: The sea between them is shallow, so a lower sea left it dry.
   uses: []

3. People crossed into the Americas, on foot and possibly by boat along the coast.
   why: They followed animals and coastlines, not a plan to find a new continent.
   uses: []

4. As the ice melted, the sea rose and covered the bridge. The Americas were cut off from Asia.
   why: From then until 1492, the peoples of the Americas developed without contact with Africa, Asia and Europe.^[n_americas]
   uses: []

:::practice
q: Why do historians usually write "years ago" instead of BCE for the time of the first humans?
answer: The numbers are huge and only roughly known. "About 300,000 years ago" is honest about how rough the date is. Writing "298,000 BCE" would suggest a precision nobody has.
:::
