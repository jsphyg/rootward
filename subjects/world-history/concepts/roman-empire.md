## The emperors

Augustus ruled from 27 BCE to 14 CE. He held all real power but called himself only "first citizen". After him came a long line of {{emperor|emperors}}. Some were able, and some were cruel or mad.

## The Roman peace

Augustus' reign began the {{pax-romana|Pax Romana}}, about 200 years of relative peace inside the empire. At its largest, around 117 CE, the empire stretched from Britain to Egypt and Mesopotamia, with perhaps 50 to 70 million people.

## What held it together

| What | How it helped |
|---|---|
| The army | Professional soldiers guarded the frontiers |
| Roads | About 80,000 km of paved roads moved troops, trade and news |
| {{latin|Latin}} and Greek | Latin in the west and Greek in the east were shared languages of government and trade |
| Roman law | The same laws and courts across the empire |
| Citizenship | Gradually extended; in 212 CE nearly all free men in the empire became citizens |
| Cities | Built on a Roman pattern, with baths, temples, forums and theaters |

*Notice: many of these tools also made life better for ordinary people, which gave them reasons to stay loyal.*

Roman engineers built {{aqueduct|aqueducts}} to carry water into cities, and great buildings using concrete, such as the Colosseum (finished in 80 CE).

## Christianity and a new capital

Christianity began in the Roman province of Judea in the first century CE and spread along Roman roads. At first Christians were sometimes persecuted. Then, from 312 CE, Emperor Constantine favored Christianity, and he was baptized before he died in 337. By the end of the century, Christianity was the empire's official religion.

In 330 CE, Constantine founded a new capital in the east: Constantinople, today Istanbul.

## East and west

The empire was too big for one ruler. After 395 CE it was permanently divided into an eastern and a western half. The western half fell in 476 CE. The eastern half, centered on Constantinople, lasted almost another thousand years.

**Why it matters.** Rome's laws, languages, roads, cities and religion shaped Europe and the Mediterranean for over a thousand years. French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Romanian all grew from Latin.

:::practice
q: Several modern European languages are called "Romance languages". Why?
answer: They grew from Latin, the language of the Romans. As the empire spread across Europe, Latin spread with it. After the empire fell, local forms of Latin slowly turned into Italian, French, Spanish, Portuguese and Romanian.
:::
