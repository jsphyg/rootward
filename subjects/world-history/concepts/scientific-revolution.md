## The old picture

For centuries, educated Europeans explained nature using the ancient Greek writers Aristotle and Ptolemy, together with Church teaching. In this picture, the Earth stood still at the center of the universe, and the sun, moon and planets circled it. This is the {{geocentric|geocentric}} model.

## A new picture of the heavens

| Year | Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| 1543 | Nicolaus Copernicus, Poland | The Earth and planets move around the sun: the {{heliocentric|heliocentric}} model |
| 1609 | Johannes Kepler, Germany | Planets move in ellipses, not perfect circles |
| 1609 to 1610 | Galileo Galilei, Italy | His telescope shows moons circling Jupiter, craters on the moon and phases of Venus |
| 1687 | Isaac Newton, England | The same laws of motion and gravity explain both planets and falling apples |

*Notice: each thinker built on the one before. Newton later said, "If I have seen further, it is by standing on the shoulders of giants."*

## Galileo and the Church

Galileo argued openly that Copernicus was right. The Catholic Church said this went against the Bible. In 1633 it put Galileo on trial, forced him to take back his views and kept him under house arrest for the rest of his life. His books still spread across Europe.

## A new way to know

The deeper change was in how people sought knowledge.

- Francis Bacon, in England, said knowledge should come from careful observation and experiment, not from old books.
- René Descartes, in France, said to doubt everything that could not be proved and to reason step by step, using mathematics.

Together these ideas grew into the {{scientific-method|scientific method}}: observe, form a {{hypothesis|hypothesis}}, test it with experiments, and change the idea if the evidence disagrees.

## Beyond astronomy

| Field | Discovery |
|---|---|
| Anatomy | Andreas Vesalius, in 1543, published detailed drawings of the human body based on his own dissections |
| Medicine | William Harvey showed in 1628 that the heart pumps blood around the body in a circuit |
| Instruments | The microscope, thermometer and barometer let people measure what they could not see |

Scientists formed societies to share results, such as the Royal Society in London, founded in 1660.

Older learning still mattered. Muslim scholars such as Ibn al-Haytham, who studied light and vision around 1000 CE, had already stressed testing ideas by experiment, and European scientists read their work in Latin translation.

:::practice
q: Why was Galileo's telescope so important to the case for the heliocentric model?
answer: It gave new evidence. Galileo saw moons circling Jupiter, proving that not everything orbits the Earth. He saw that Venus goes through a full set of phases like the moon, which fits a Venus that circles the sun. Observation, not just argument, now supported Copernicus.
:::
