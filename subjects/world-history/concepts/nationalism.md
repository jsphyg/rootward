## One people, one country

A {{nation|nation}} is a group of people who feel they belong together. Usually they share a language, a culture and a history. A {{state|state}} is a country with its own government and borders.

Nationalism says the two should match. Each nation should have its own state, and should rule itself instead of being ruled by outsiders.

The idea grew strong after the French Revolution, which taught that power belongs to the nation, the people, not to a king. Napoleon's armies spread that idea across Europe, and the peoples he conquered turned it against French rule.

## Joining together

In the 1800s, nationalism redrew the map of Europe.

| Year | What happened | How nationalism shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1861 | The Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed | Italian speakers in many small states join into one country |
| 1871 | The German Empire is proclaimed | German states join under the lead of Prussia |
| 1878 | Serbia becomes fully independent of the Ottoman Empire | Serbs gain a state of their own |

*Notice: in Italy and Germany, nationalism built a bigger country by joining small ones.*

## Breaking apart

Nationalism can also pull a country apart. An {{empire|empire}} rules many different peoples from one center. Austria-Hungary ruled more than ten peoples. The Ottoman Empire, which once held most of southeastern Europe, had lost nearly all of it by 1914.

For an empire, nationalism is a threat. Each people that wants its own state is a piece that might break off.

## Why it mattered in 1914

Many {{south-slavs|South Slavs}} lived inside Austria-Hungary. Next door was Serbia, an independent South Slav state. Some South Slavs dreamed of leaving Austria-Hungary and joining Serbia in one big South Slav country.^[n_yugoslavia]

Austria-Hungary's leaders feared that if the South Slavs left, other peoples would follow and the empire would collapse. That fear shaped everything that happened in [[balkan-tensions|the Balkans]] before 1914.

**Why nationalism is so strong.** It gives ordinary people a reason to feel loyal to "their" country, to pay its taxes and to fight for it. That loyalty can hold a nation-state together. In an empire of many nations, the same loyalty points people away from the empire.

:::practice
q: In 1871 nationalism helped create a new, stronger Germany. Why did the same idea weaken Austria-Hungary?
answer: Germany's people mostly shared one language and culture, so nationalism joined them into one state. Austria-Hungary held many different nations. Nationalism told each of them it should have its own state, which meant leaving the empire.
:::
