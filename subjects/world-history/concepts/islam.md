## Arabia before Islam

Arabia is a large desert peninsula. Most Arabs were nomadic herders, living in tribes that often fought one another. Some lived in trading towns along the caravan routes.

One such town was Mecca. It was a trading center and a place of pilgrimage. At its heart stood the Kaaba, a cube-shaped shrine then filled with statues of many gods.

## Muhammad

Muhammad was born in Mecca around 570 CE and became a merchant.^[n_muhammad_sources] Muslims believe that around 610, when he was about 40, the angel Gabriel brought him the first of many messages from God. These messages were later collected as the {{quran|Quran}}, the holy book of Islam.

Muhammad preached that there is only one God, Allah in Arabic, and that all people are equal before God. He told the rich to care for the poor. Many of Mecca's leaders saw this as a threat to their gods, their pilgrim trade and their power.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| about 610 | Muhammad begins preaching in Mecca |
| 622 | Facing danger, he and his followers move to the town of Medina. This journey is the {{hijra|Hijra}} |
| 630 | Muhammad returns with an army and takes Mecca peacefully. The statues are removed from the Kaaba |
| 632 | Muhammad dies. Most of Arabia has accepted Islam |

*Notice: the Hijra is so important that the Islamic calendar counts its years from 622.*

## What Muslims believe

"Islam" means submission to God. A follower is a Muslim, "one who submits." Muslims see Muhammad as a human {{prophet|prophet}}, the last in a line that includes Abraham, Moses and Jesus. That is why the Quran calls Jews and Christians "People of the Book."

The core duties of a Muslim are the {{five-pillars|Five Pillars}}:

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Declaration of faith | "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the messenger of God" |
| Prayer | Praying five times a day, facing Mecca |
| Charity | Giving a share of one's wealth to the poor |
| Fasting | Not eating or drinking from dawn to sunset in the month of Ramadan |
| Pilgrimage | Traveling to Mecca at least once in life, if able |

## The split over leadership

Muhammad left no agreed successor. Most Muslims accepted leaders chosen by the community. A smaller group believed leadership belonged to Muhammad's family, through his cousin and son-in-law, Ali. This disagreement led, over time, to the two main branches of Islam, {{sunni-shia|Sunni and Shia}}. Today most Muslims are Sunni.

:::practice
q: Why do Muslims call Jews and Christians "People of the Book"?
answer: Islam teaches that the same one God sent earlier messages to Jews and Christians through prophets such as Abraham, Moses and Jesus, and that these messages were written down in scriptures. So Muslims see Jews and Christians as fellow believers in God who received earlier revelations, while believing the Quran is God's final message.
:::
