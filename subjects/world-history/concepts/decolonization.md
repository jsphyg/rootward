## Why empires ended

| Cause | What it meant |
|---|---|
| World War II | Europe was exhausted and nearly bankrupt. Japan had defeated European armies in Asia, showing they could be beaten |
| Nationalism | Educated leaders in the colonies demanded the same self-rule Europeans claimed for themselves |
| The new superpowers | Both the United States and the Soviet Union opposed the old European empires |
| The United Nations | Its charter supported the self-determination of peoples |

## India

The Indian National Congress had campaigned for self-rule since 1885. Its most famous leader, Mohandas Gandhi, led mass {{nonviolence|nonviolent}} protest. In 1930 he led the Salt March, walking about 380 km to the sea to make salt in defiance of a British tax.

After World War II, Britain agreed to leave. But many Muslims, led by Muhammad Ali Jinnah, feared being a minority in a Hindu-majority India. On 15 August 1947 British India was divided, the {{partition|Partition}}, into India and a new Muslim state, Pakistan. Millions fled across the new borders, and Hindus, Muslims and Sikhs attacked each other.^[n_partition] Gandhi was assassinated in 1948 by a Hindu extremist.

## Asia

| Country | Ruler | Independence |
|---|---|---|
| The Philippines | United States | 1946 |
| Indonesia | Netherlands | Declared 1945; won after four years of war, 1949 |
| Vietnam | France | War from 1946; France defeated at Dien Bien Phu in 1954. The country was divided, leading to the Vietnam War |

## Africa

| Country | Ruler | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Ghana | Britain | Led by Kwame Nkrumah, became one of the first black African colonies to win independence, in 1957 |
| 17 countries | Mostly France | 1960, called the "Year of Africa" |
| Algeria | France | A brutal war from 1954 to 1962; independence in 1962 |
| Kenya | Britain | The Mau Mau uprising from 1952 to 1960 was crushed harshly; independence in 1963 |
| The Congo | Belgium | Independence in 1960 was followed by crisis; its prime minister, Patrice Lumumba, was murdered in 1961 |
| Angola, Mozambique | Portugal | Independence in 1975 after long wars |

In South Africa, a white minority government enforced {{apartheid|apartheid}}, strict racial separation, from 1948. It ended only in 1994, when Nelson Mandela was elected the country's first black president.

## New nations, old problems

Many new countries kept colonial borders that joined rival groups, and economies built to export raw materials. Some fell under military rule. In the Cold War, the superpowers competed for their support, and some leaders tried to stay neutral in the Non-Aligned Movement, founded in 1961.

:::practice
q: Why was the Partition of India so violent?
answer: The British left quickly and drew the new border in a few weeks. Millions of Hindus, Sikhs and Muslims suddenly found themselves on the "wrong" side and fled. Fear and old tensions exploded into mass killings, and governments were too new and weak to protect people. Hundreds of thousands, perhaps more than a million, died.
:::
