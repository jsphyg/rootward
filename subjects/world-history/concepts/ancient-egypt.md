## The gift of the Nile

Egypt is almost all desert. But through it runs the Nile, the longest river in Africa. Every summer, rains far to the south made the Nile rise and spread over its banks. When the water went down, it left a layer of rich black mud. Farmers planted in it and harvested before the next flood.

The flood was regular and usually gentle. That made Egyptian farming dependable, and it made the surplus large. The Egyptians called their country Kemet, "the black land", after that soil.

The Nile was also a highway. The current flows north, and the wind usually blows south. Boats could drift one way and sail back the other.

## Pharaohs

Around 3100 BCE, Upper Egypt (the south) and Lower Egypt (the north) were united under one king. Egypt's rulers came to be called {{pharaoh|pharaohs}}. Egyptians believed the pharaoh was a god on earth, who kept order in the world and made sure the Nile rose.

Ruling families passed power down for generations. Each family is a {{dynasty|dynasty}}. Historians group Egypt's long history into three great periods of strength:

| Period | About when | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Old Kingdom | 2700 to 2200 BCE | The great pyramids |
| Middle Kingdom | 2050 to 1650 BCE | Trade, literature, a strong government |
| New Kingdom | 1550 to 1070 BCE | An empire abroad; pharaohs such as Hatshepsut and Ramesses II |

*Notice: Egypt lasted so long that its own history has ancient and modern parts.*^[n_cleopatra]

## Pyramids and the afterlife

Egyptians believed in life after death. They preserved bodies as {{mummy|mummies}} and filled tombs with things the dead would need.

The pyramids were tombs for pharaohs. The Great Pyramid at Giza was built for the pharaoh Khufu around 2560 BCE. It was the tallest human-made structure in the world for about 3,800 years.

The pyramids were not built by slaves, as people once believed. Archaeologists have found the villages and tombs of the workers. They were paid laborers and farmers, organized by the state, many working in the flood season when their fields were under water.

## Protected by desert

Deserts to the east and west and the sea to the north made Egypt hard to invade. For long stretches it was left alone to develop in its own way, unlike open Mesopotamia. Egypt was eventually conquered by Persia in 525 BCE, by Alexander the Great in 332 BCE, and by Rome in 30 BCE.

**Why it matters.** Egypt shows how much geography can shape a civilization. A reliable river and protective deserts gave it unusual stability, and that stability let one way of life last for three thousand years.

:::practice
q: Who built the pyramids, and how do we know?
answer: Paid workers and farmers, organized by the state, many working while their fields were flooded. We know because archaeologists have excavated the workers' villages, bakeries and tombs near the pyramids at Giza. Workers buried near the king's tomb would not have been slaves.
