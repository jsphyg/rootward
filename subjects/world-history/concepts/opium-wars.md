## A trade problem

The British loved Chinese tea. But as the Qianlong emperor had said, China wanted little that Britain made. So Britain had to pay in silver, and lots of it.

British merchants found something Chinese buyers would pay for: opium, an addictive drug. The East India Company grew it in India, and merchants smuggled it into China, even though the Chinese government had banned it.

| Before opium | After opium |
|---|---|
| Silver flowed from Britain into China | Silver flowed out of China to pay for opium |
| Few Chinese used opium | Millions became addicted |

## The First Opium War

In 1839 the emperor sent an official, Lin Zexu, to stop the trade. He seized and destroyed more than 20,000 chests of British opium. Britain sent warships.

China's armies and old ships were no match for British steamships and guns. In 1842 China signed the Treaty of Nanjing, and in 1843 a follow-up treaty, the Treaty of the Bogue, added more terms.

| What China gave up | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Hong Kong | Became a British colony until 1997 |
| Five ports opened to British trade | Foreign merchants could trade freely |
| A large payment | For the destroyed opium and the cost of the war |
| Legal control over foreigners (1843 treaty) | British subjects in China were tried in their own courts, not Chinese ones |

China called these {{unequal-treaties|unequal treaties}}, because they were forced on it by war and gave rights to foreigners without anything in return. Other powers, such as France and the United States, soon demanded the same rights.

## More defeats, more rebellion

| Dates | Event |
|---|---|
| 1856 to 1860 | Second Opium War. Britain and France take Beijing and burn the emperor's Summer Palace. China legalizes opium and opens more ports |
| 1850 to 1864 | Taiping Rebellion. A huge civil war against the Qing kills tens of millions of people |
| 1894 to 1895 | Japan defeats China and takes Taiwan |
| 1900 | The Boxer Rebellion against foreigners is crushed by an alliance of eight foreign powers |
| 1911 to 1912 | Revolution ends the Qing dynasty, and China becomes a republic |

*Notice: in 1793 the Qing had told Britain China needed nothing. Fifty years later, industrial weapons had made China powerless to say no.*

:::practice
q: Why does China call the period after the Opium Wars the "century of humiliation"?
answer: From 1839 China was defeated again and again by foreign powers and forced to sign unequal treaties. It lost territory such as Hong Kong and Taiwan, opened ports to foreigners, paid huge sums and let foreigners live outside Chinese law. A once-proud empire was bullied by outsiders until about the 1940s, a memory that still shapes Chinese views of the West.
:::
