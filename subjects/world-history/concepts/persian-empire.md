## Cyrus the Great

The Persians came from what is now Iran. Around 550 BCE their king, Cyrus, began a series of conquests. In 539 BCE he took Babylon, the great city of Mesopotamia.

Instead of punishing the conquered, Cyrus often respected their gods and customs. He allowed the Jews exiled in Babylon to return home and rebuild their temple.^[n_cyrus_cylinder]

Under the kings who followed, the empire stretched from Egypt and Turkey in the west to the Indus River in the east. It was the largest empire the world had yet seen.

## Darius and the art of running an empire

King Darius I (who ruled 522 to 486 BCE) organized the empire so it could be governed.

| Tool | How it worked |
|---|---|
| Provinces | The empire was divided into about 20 provinces, each run by a governor called a {{satrap|satrap}} |
| Inspectors | Officials called "the eyes and ears of the king" checked on the satraps |
| The Royal Road | A highway of about 2,700 km linked the west of the empire to the capital, with relay stations for fast messengers^[n_couriers] |
| Coins and taxes | Standard gold coins and fixed taxes made trade and payment easier |

*Notice: almost every tool on this list is about control at a distance. That was the central problem of a huge empire.*

The Persians followed a religion called {{zoroastrianism|Zoroastrianism}}, which taught that the world is a struggle between good and evil.

## War with Greece and the end

Persia tried to conquer the Greek city-states, but was defeated, at Marathon in 490 BCE and at Salamis in 480 BCE. A century and a half later, Alexander the Great of Macedonia turned the tables. By 330 BCE, he had conquered the entire Persian Empire.

**Why it matters.** Persia showed how a huge empire could be run: through local governors, good roads, standard money and tolerance. Later empires, including Alexander's and Rome's, followed a similar model.

:::practice
q: Why might tolerance have been a smart policy for Persian kings, not just a kind one?
answer: People who keep their own gods, customs and local leaders have fewer reasons to revolt. Fewer revolts meant fewer costly wars and a steady flow of taxes. Tolerance made a huge, varied empire cheaper and safer to rule.
:::
