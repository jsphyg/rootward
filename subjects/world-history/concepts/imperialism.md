## A race for empire

European countries had held colonies since the 1500s. But from about 1870 they grabbed land faster than ever. Historians call this the new {{imperialism|imperialism}}.

| Motive | What it meant |
|---|---|
| Economic | Factories needed rubber, cotton, copper, palm oil; industrialists wanted new markets |
| Political | Colonies brought prestige and naval bases. Each country feared rivals would grab land first |
| Nationalist | Empire became a sign of national greatness |
| Ideological | Many Europeans believed they were superior and had a duty to "civilize" others. Missionaries wanted to spread Christianity |

*Notice: nationalism fed imperialism. Each nation measured its greatness partly by its empire, so every new colony for a rival felt like a loss.*

## The tools of empire

Industry gave Europeans new advantages.

| Tool | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Steamships | Could go up rivers into the interior |
| Quinine | A medicine that protected against malaria, which had killed many Europeans in Africa |
| Railways and telegraph | Moved troops and orders fast |
| The Maxim gun, from 1884 | A machine gun that let a few soldiers kill hundreds of attackers |

## British India

Britain's East India Company had taken over much of India since the 1750s. In 1857 Indian soldiers in the Company's army rebelled, and the revolt spread across northern India. After crushing it, the British government took direct control in 1858. This period of British rule is called the {{raj|Raj}}. Indian cotton and other goods fed British industry, and India became the "jewel in the crown" of the British Empire.

## A world divided

By 1914, European powers controlled most of Africa and much of Asia.^[n_empire_share] The United States took the Philippines, Puerto Rico and Guam from Spain in 1898, and Japan took Taiwan and Korea.

## Resistance

Colonized peoples resisted in many ways: armed revolt, refusing to work, keeping their own cultures, and building nationalist movements. In India, educated Indians founded the Indian National Congress in 1885, which later led the fight for independence.

:::practice
q: How did nationalism help drive the new imperialism?
answer: Nationalists saw colonies as a measure of national power and pride. Each country feared that if it did not grab territory, a rival would. Governments used empire to win popular support at home. So rivalry between nations turned the search for raw materials and markets into a race to claim as much of the world as possible.
