## Allies become rivals

The United States and the Soviet Union had fought together against Hitler. But they had very different systems.

| | United States | Soviet Union |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Capitalist: private ownership, markets | Communist: state ownership, central planning |
| Government | Democracy with elections and free press | One-party state with censorship and secret police |
| Aim | Contain communism, open markets | Secure Soviet borders, spread communism |

At the end of the war, Soviet armies occupied Eastern Europe and set up communist governments there. In 1946 Winston Churchill said an "Iron Curtain" had come down across Europe.

## Two blocs

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1947 | The Truman Doctrine: the United States promises to help countries resist communism. This policy was called {{containment|containment}} |
| 1948 | The Marshall Plan: American aid to rebuild western Europe |
| 1948 to 1949 | The Soviets block land routes to West Berlin; the West flies in supplies for almost a year |
| 1949 | The Western alliance, NATO, is formed. The Soviet Union tests its first atomic bomb. Communists win China's civil war |
| 1955 | The Soviet bloc forms the Warsaw Pact |
| 1961 | East Germany builds the Berlin Wall to stop people fleeing west |

## The nuclear arms race

Both sides built thousands of nuclear weapons, many far more powerful than the bombs dropped on Japan. Each knew that attacking the other would mean its own destruction too. This was called {{mutually-assured-destruction|mutually assured destruction}}.

In October 1962 the Soviet Union secretly placed nuclear missiles in Cuba, close to the United States. For 13 days the world came close to nuclear war. Finally the Soviets agreed to remove the missiles, and the United States quietly agreed to remove its own missiles from Turkey.

## Hot wars

The superpowers fought {{proxy-war|proxy wars}}, backing opposite sides in other countries' conflicts.

| War | Dates | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Korea | 1950 to 1953 | Communist North Korea invaded the South; US-led UN forces and China joined in. It ended where it began, divided |
| Vietnam | US combat troops 1965 to 1973 | The United States fought to stop communist North Vietnam taking over the South. It failed; Vietnam was united under communism in 1975 |
| Afghanistan | 1979 to 1989 | The Soviet Union fought rebels backed by the United States, and withdrew |

## The space race

In 1957 the Soviet Union launched Sputnik, the first artificial satellite, and in 1961 sent the first person, Yuri Gagarin, into space. The United States answered by landing astronauts on the moon in 1969.

:::practice
q: What was the policy of containment, and how did the Marshall Plan support it?
answer: Containment meant stopping communism from spreading to new countries. The Marshall Plan gave billions of dollars to rebuild western Europe after the war. The idea was that prosperous countries, with jobs and food, would be less likely to turn to communism, so economic aid was a way to contain Soviet influence.
:::
