## China reunited

After the Han dynasty fell in 220 CE, China was divided for more than 350 years. The short-lived Sui dynasty reunited it in 589 and built the Grand Canal, linking north and south by water. Then came two great dynasties.

| Dynasty | Dates | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| Tang | 618 to 907 | Chang'an (today Xi'an) |
| Song | 960 to 1279 | Kaifeng, then Hangzhou after 1127 |

## The Tang: an open empire

The Tang reached deep into Central Asia and controlled much of the Silk Roads. Chang'an had perhaps a million people. Merchants, monks and envoys came from Persia, India, Korea and Japan.

Buddhism, which had come from India, reached its height. Poets such as Li Bai and Du Fu wrote verses still learned by Chinese schoolchildren. Wu Zetian ruled as emperor from 690 to 705, the only woman in Chinese history to do so in her own name.

A great rebellion from 755 to 763 badly weakened the dynasty, and it collapsed in 907.

## The Song: a world of invention

The Song never matched the Tang's military power. Nomad peoples took the north, and in 1127 the Song court fled south to Hangzhou. But at home Song China boomed.

| Field | What happened |
|---|---|
| Government | Officials were chosen mainly by {{civil-service-exam|civil service exams}} on Confucian classics |
| Farming | A fast-ripening rice from Southeast Asia allowed two harvests a year. The population roughly doubled, to about 100 million |
| Printing | Woodblock printing, already used under the Tang, spread widely, and Bi Sheng invented {{movable-type|movable type}} in the 1040s^[n_printing] |
| Weapons | {{gunpowder|Gunpowder}}, discovered under the Tang, was used in fire arrows, bombs and early guns |
| Navigation | Sailors used the magnetic compass to steer at sea |
| Money | The government issued paper money, the first in the world |

*Notice: many of these inventions later reached Europe and changed it. Printing, gunpowder and the compass helped launch Europe's own age of change after 1450.*

## Confucian revival

Song scholars renewed Confucian thought, blending in ideas from Buddhism and Daoism. This "Neo-Confucianism" shaped Chinese government and family life for the next 700 years.

:::practice
q: Why do historians call the Song dynasty an age of invention, even though it was militarily weak?
answer: The Song lost northern China to nomad invaders, but its economy, cities and learning flourished. It made movable type, gunpowder weapons, the ship's compass and paper money part of everyday life, and new rice doubled its population. Military weakness did not stop it from being one of the richest and most advanced societies in the world.
:::
