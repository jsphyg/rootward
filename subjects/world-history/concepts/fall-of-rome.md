## A slow collapse

The western Roman Empire did not fall in a day. Its decline lasted about two centuries. The date usually given for the end, 476 CE, is when a Germanic general named Odoacer removed the last western emperor, a teenager called Romulus Augustulus. Odoacer did not bother to name a new emperor.

## Long-term causes

| Problem | What happened |
|---|---|
| Unstable government | In the 200s CE, emperors were made and murdered by their own armies; about 25 ruled in 50 years |
| Money troubles | Emperors put less silver in coins to pay soldiers, so prices soared; taxes became crushing |
| An overstretched army | The frontiers were long, and Rome relied more and more on Germanic soldiers |
| A divided empire | After 395 the richer, stronger east went its own way, leaving the west weaker |

*Notice: each problem made the others worse. Unstable rulers spent money on armies; armies made and unmade rulers.*

## The trigger: peoples on the move

In the late 300s the Huns, horse-riding warriors from Central Asia, swept west and drove Germanic peoples, such as the Goths, into Roman lands.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 376 | The Goths cross the Danube into the empire, fleeing the Huns |
| 378 | The Goths destroy a Roman army at Adrianople and kill the emperor |
| 410 | The Visigoths sack the city of Rome |
| 455 | The Vandals sack Rome again |
| 476 | Odoacer removes the last western emperor |

*Notice: Rome had beaten invaders for centuries. This time it was too weak to recover.*

## The east survives

The eastern half of the empire, ruled from Constantinople, was richer, more populous and easier to defend. It survived for almost another thousand years, until 1453. Historians call it the Byzantine Empire.

## How historians see it

Historians still debate the fall.^[n_gibbon] Some stress decline and catastrophe: cities shrank, trade collapsed, and life became poorer and more dangerous in the west. Others stress transformation: Germanic kingdoms kept many Roman laws, the Latin language and the Christian Church. Both are partly true.

:::practice
q: Why did the eastern half of the Roman Empire survive when the western half fell?
answer: The east was richer, with more cities, trade and tax income. Its capital, Constantinople, was very hard to capture, protected by water and massive walls. It could pay invaders to go elsewhere, and some of them moved west instead.
:::
