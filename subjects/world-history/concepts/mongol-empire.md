## Nomads of the steppe

The steppe is a vast belt of grassland stretching across Central Asia. The Mongols who lived there were {{nomad|nomads}}, moving with their herds of horses, sheep and goats. Children learned to ride almost before they could walk. Every man was a mounted archer.

## Genghis Khan

A Mongol chief named Temujin spent years uniting the warring tribes. In 1206 they proclaimed him Genghis Khan, meaning roughly "universal ruler." A {{khan|khan}} is a ruler.

| Mongol strength | Why it worked |
|---|---|
| Speed | Each rider had several horses, so armies could cover huge distances fast |
| Discipline | Soldiers were organized in units of 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 |
| Terror | Cities that resisted were often destroyed and their people killed, so others surrendered |
| Learning | The Mongols hired Chinese and Persian engineers to build siege weapons |

He died in 1227, after conquering northern China and Central Asia.

## The largest land empire

His sons and grandsons kept going.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1240 | Mongols destroy Kiev and rule the Russian lands |
| 1241 | They defeat armies in Poland and Hungary, then turn back, probably because the Great Khan had died |
| 1258 | They sack Baghdad and kill the caliph |
| 1279 | Kublai Khan, Genghis's grandson, completes the conquest of Song China and founds the Yuan dynasty |
| 1274, 1281 | Attempts to invade Japan fail, partly because of storms |

*Notice: at its height the empire stretched from Korea to Hungary. No land empire has been larger.*

The conquests killed millions of people.^[n_mongol_deaths] Some regions, such as parts of Persia, took centuries to recover.

## Four khanates

The empire was too big for one ruler. By the 1260s it had split into four {{khanate|khanates}}: the Golden Horde in Russia, the Ilkhanate in Persia, the Chagatai Khanate in Central Asia, and the Yuan dynasty in China.

## The Mongol peace

Once the fighting stopped, the Mongols wanted trade and taxes. They protected merchants, built relay stations with fresh horses for messengers, and allowed many religions. Historians call this the Pax Mongolica, the "Mongol peace."

Travelers crossed Asia in safety. The Venetian Marco Polo said he served Kublai Khan for years.^[n_marco_polo] Chinese inventions, Persian astronomy and new foods moved along the routes. So, in the 1340s, did the plague.

:::practice
q: How did the Mongol Empire both harm and help trade along the Silk Roads?
answer: The conquests destroyed cities, killed millions and wrecked farmland, which hurt trade at first. But afterward the Mongols ruled almost the whole route under one power. They protected merchants, kept roads safe and welcomed traders of all religions, so for about a century trade between China and Europe grew easier than ever before.
:::
