## Why go?

Spices such as pepper, cinnamon and cloves were wildly popular in Europe and very expensive. They came from India and Southeast Asia, passing through Arab and Ottoman lands and Venetian ships, and each middleman added to the price.

| Motive | What explorers wanted |
|---|---|
| Wealth | Spices, gold, silk and new lands |
| Religion | To spread Christianity, and to find allies against Muslim powers |
| Glory | Fame for themselves and their kings |

People summed it up as "God, gold and glory."

## How they did it

Europe borrowed and improved tools from many places.

| Tool | What it did | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|
| The {{caravel|caravel}} | A small, fast ship that could sail well into the wind | Portugal, using triangular sails like those of the Indian Ocean |
| The magnetic compass | Showed direction at sea | China, through the Islamic world |
| The {{astrolabe|astrolabe}} | Measured the height of the sun or stars to find how far north or south a ship was | Ancient Greece, improved by Muslim scholars |
| Printed maps | Shared new knowledge quickly | The Renaissance printing press |

## Portugal sails around Africa

Prince Henry of Portugal, called "the Navigator," paid for voyages down the African coast from the 1420s. Each trip went a little farther.

| Year | Voyage |
|---|---|
| 1488 | Bartolomeu Dias rounds the Cape of Good Hope, the southern tip of Africa |
| 1498 | Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut, India. He returns with spices worth many times the cost of the voyage |
| 1510 to 1511 | Portugal seizes Goa in India and Malacca in Southeast Asia, using cannons to control trade |

*Notice: the Portuguese did not discover the Indian Ocean trade. They broke into a rich network that was already centuries old, often by force.*

## Spain sails west

Christopher Columbus, an Italian sailor, believed he could reach Asia by sailing west. He guessed the Earth was much smaller than it is.^[n_columbus] Spain's rulers paid for his voyage. In October 1492 he reached an island in the Bahamas, then Cuba and Hispaniola. He died believing he had reached Asia.

In 1494 Spain and Portugal signed the Treaty of Tordesillas, drawing a line down the Atlantic and dividing the newly found lands between them. That is why Brazil, east of the line, speaks Portuguese.

From 1519 to 1522, an expedition led by Ferdinand Magellan made the first voyage around the world. Magellan was killed in the Philippines, and only one of five ships and 18 of about 270 men made it home.

:::practice
q: Why did Vasco da Gama's voyage of 1498 matter so much?
answer: It opened a direct sea route from Europe to India around Africa, so Europeans could buy spices without paying Ottoman, Arab and Venetian middlemen. The voyage made a huge profit, and Portugal soon built forts and trading posts across the Indian Ocean. It was the start of European power in Asian trade.
:::
