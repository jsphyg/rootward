## Gold for salt

The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world. Crossing it took about two months. Camels, which can go many days without water, made regular crossings possible in the early centuries CE.

| Region | Had plenty of | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| West Africa, south of the Sahara | Gold | Salt, for health and to preserve food |
| The Sahara and North Africa | Salt, horses, cloth | Gold |

Merchants from North Africa crossed the desert in {{caravan|caravans}} of hundreds or even thousands of camels. Many were Muslims, and over time they brought Islam, Arabic writing and new ideas south.

## Ghana

The first great empire was Ghana. It has no link to the modern country of Ghana, which took its name later.

By about 800, Ghana's kings controlled the trade routes. They taxed every load of salt coming in and gold going out. An Arab writer in 1068 described a king who sat in splendor among horses wearing gold cloth. Ghana declined in the 1100s.

## Mali

Around 1235, a leader named Sundiata founded the empire of Mali. His story is still told by {{griot|griots}}, West Africa's storytellers and keepers of history.

Mali's most famous ruler was Mansa Musa, who ruled from about 1312 to 1337. {{mansa|Mansa}} means king. A Muslim, he made the pilgrimage to Mecca in 1324 with a huge caravan carrying gold. In Cairo he gave away and spent so much gold that its value there fell for years. Europe and the Middle East heard of Mali's wealth.^[n_mansa_musa]

He brought scholars and an architect home and built mosques. Under Mali, the city of Timbuktu grew into a center of trade and Islamic learning, with scholars and large collections of handwritten books.

## Songhai

As Mali weakened, the Songhai people, based at Gao on the Niger River, built an even larger empire in the late 1400s. Its rulers took Timbuktu in 1468 and set up an organized government with an army and tax officials.

In 1591 a Moroccan army armed with guns crossed the desert and defeated Songhai. The empire broke apart. By then, European ships were trading on Africa's west coast, and gold no longer had to cross the Sahara.

| Empire | Height | Center |
|---|---|---|
| Ghana | about 800 to 1050 | Koumbi Saleh |
| Mali | about 1235 to 1400 | Niani (its exact site is debated) |
| Songhai | about 1464 to 1591 | Gao |

*Notice: each empire rose by taking control of the same trade. When the trade routes shifted, power shifted with them.*

:::practice
q: Why did trans-Saharan trade decline after about 1500?
answer: Portuguese and other European ships began trading directly along Africa's west coast, so gold and other goods could go by sea instead of crossing the desert. Then in 1591 a Moroccan army destroyed the Songhai Empire, which had kept the desert routes organized and safe. With the trade moving to the coast, the inland empires lost their main source of wealth.
:::
