## From border state to empire

Around 1300 a Turkish leader named Osman ruled a small state in Anatolia, on the border of the weakened Byzantine Empire. His followers were named Ottomans after him.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| about 1300 | Osman founds the state |
| 1350s | The Ottomans cross into Europe |
| 1389 | They defeat the Serbs at the Battle of Kosovo |
| 1453 | Sultan Mehmed II captures Constantinople with huge cannons. It becomes the capital, Istanbul^[n_istanbul] |
| 1516 to 1517 | They conquer Syria, Egypt and the holy cities of Mecca and Medina |
| 1529 | Suleiman besieges Vienna but fails to take it |

*Notice: the Ottomans spread into Europe a century before they took Constantinople. By 1453 the city was an island in Ottoman land.*

## Suleiman the Magnificent

The empire reached its height under Suleiman, who ruled from 1520 to 1566. Europeans called him "the Magnificent." The Ottomans called him "the Lawgiver," because he reorganized the empire's laws. His architect, Sinan, built great mosques in Istanbul.

## How the empire worked

The ruler was the {{sultan|sultan}}. He governed through a large, organized government.

| Feature | How it worked |
|---|---|
| Janissaries | Elite soldiers. Christian boys were taken from Balkan families, converted to Islam and trained to serve the sultan. Some rose to the highest posts in the empire |
| Gunpowder | Ottoman armies used cannons and handguns early and well |
| Religious communities | Christians and Jews paid extra tax but could keep their faith and run their own community affairs under their religious leaders |

The {{janissary|Janissary}} system meant the sultan's closest servants owed everything to him, not to noble families.

*Notice: the Ottomans, like the caliphs before them, ruled a mix of peoples and faiths. Tolerance, within limits, helped them hold it together.*

## A long decline

After a second failed siege of Vienna in 1683, the Ottomans began to lose land to Austria and Russia. In the 1800s Europeans called the empire "the sick man of Europe." Balkan peoples, such as the Greeks and Serbs, won independence one by one. The empire ended after the First World War, and modern Turkey was founded in 1923.

:::practice
q: How did the Janissary system make the sultan more powerful?
answer: Janissaries were taken as boys from Christian families, converted and trained to serve only the sultan. They had no noble family or local power base, so their loyalty and success depended entirely on him. This gave the sultan an elite army and officials who could not easily turn against him.
:::
