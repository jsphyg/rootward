## Capitalism

In {{capitalism|capitalism}}, private people and companies own businesses and run them to make a profit. Prices are set by supply and demand in the market.

Adam Smith argued in 1776 that when people pursue their own interest in free markets, an "invisible hand" makes society as a whole richer. Many argued that governments should stay out of the economy, an idea called laissez-faire, French for "let it be."

Industrial capitalism made goods such as cloth and iron far cheaper and created great wealth. But in the early factory towns, that wealth went mostly to owners, while workers faced low pay, long hours and dangerous conditions.

## Socialism

{{socialist|Socialists}} believed the problem was private ownership itself. If factories, mines and land were owned by the public or the workers, they argued, wealth could be shared fairly.

| Thinker | Idea |
|---|---|
| Robert Owen, Britain | Built a model factory town at New Lanark, Scotland, with schools, shorter hours and decent housing |
| Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, Germany | The Communist Manifesto (1848): history is a struggle between classes, and workers will overthrow the owners in a revolution |

## Marx's predictions

Marx said industrial society had split into two classes: the owners of factories, the bourgeoisie, and the workers, the proletariat. He believed capitalism would make workers ever poorer until they rose up, seized the factories, and built a classless society he called {{communism|communism}}.

*Notice: Marx expected revolution in the most industrial countries, such as Britain and Germany. Instead, the first communist revolution came in 1917 in Russia, one of the least industrial.*

## Reform instead of revolution

In most industrial countries, workers' lives improved through reform rather than revolution.

| Change | Example |
|---|---|
| Labor unions | Workers organized to bargain together and strike; unions were made fully legal in Britain in 1871 |
| Workers' parties | Germany's Social Democrats, Britain's Labour Party, founded 1900 |
| Social insurance | Germany, under Otto von Bismarck, created health, accident and old-age insurance in the 1880s |
| Votes | Most European men won the vote by 1914; women's suffrage came mostly after |
| Factory laws | Limits on hours and child labor; safety rules |

Most of today's democracies are mixed economies: mostly capitalist, with government rules, public services and welfare that grew out of these struggles.

:::practice
q: Why did Marx's prediction of revolution in industrial countries not come true there?
answer: Workers' lives improved instead of getting worse. Unions won higher wages and shorter hours, men won the vote and formed their own parties, and governments passed factory laws and social insurance. With peaceful ways to improve their lives, most workers in Britain, Germany and similar countries chose reform rather than revolution.
:::
