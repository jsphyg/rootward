## An unfair society

Before 1789, France was an absolute monarchy under King Louis XVI. Society was split into three {{estates|estates}}.

| Estate | Who | Share of people | Privileges |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | The clergy | Under 1 percent | Paid almost no taxes; the Church owned much land |
| Second | The nobles | About 2 percent | Paid few taxes; held top jobs in the army and government |
| Third | Everyone else: peasants, workers, merchants, lawyers | About 97 percent | Paid most of the taxes |

## Why 1789?

| Cause | What happened |
|---|---|
| Debt | Wars, including helping the Americans, had left the government nearly bankrupt |
| Hunger | A bad harvest in 1788 sent bread prices soaring |
| Ideas | Enlightenment writers had taught people to question privilege and absolute rule |
| A weak king | Louis XVI could not get the nobles to accept new taxes |

To raise money, the king called the Estates-General, a meeting of all three estates that had not met since 1614.

## Revolution

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| June 1789 | The Third Estate declares itself the National Assembly, speaking for the nation |
| 14 July 1789 | Crowds in Paris storm the Bastille, a royal fortress and prison |
| August 1789 | The Assembly ends noble privileges and issues the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen |
| 1792 | France goes to war with Austria and Prussia. The monarchy is abolished and France becomes a republic |
| January 1793 | Louis XVI is executed by guillotine |

The revolution's motto became "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity."

## The Terror

At war with much of Europe and facing revolts at home, the government turned to terror. Led by Maximilien Robespierre, it arrested anyone suspected of opposing the revolution. From 1793 to 1794, about 17,000 people were officially executed, and many more died in prison or without trial.^[n_terror] In July 1794 Robespierre himself was overthrown and executed.

*Notice: a revolution for liberty ended up killing thousands of its own citizens. Fear of enemies, inside and out, drove it to extremes.*

## Napoleon

In 1799 a successful young general, Napoleon Bonaparte, seized power. In 1804 he crowned himself emperor.

- His law code, the Napoleonic Code, made all male citizens equal before the law and is still the basis of law in many countries.
- His armies conquered much of Europe, spreading revolutionary ideas such as equality before the law.
- His invasion of Russia in 1812 ended in disaster. He was finally defeated at Waterloo in 1815.

## Why it matters

The French Revolution spread the idea that power belongs to the nation, the people, not a king. Peoples under Napoleon's rule took in this idea and turned it against France, which gave birth to modern {{nationalism|nationalism}}. The words "left" and "right" in politics come from where members sat in the revolutionary assembly.

:::practice
q: How did Napoleon both continue and betray the French Revolution?
answer: He kept some of its key gains: equality before the law, an end to noble privilege, and careers open to talent, written into his law code and spread across Europe. But he betrayed its ideal of liberty by ruling as a dictator and making himself emperor, censoring the press, and replacing the republic with a new monarchy.
:::
