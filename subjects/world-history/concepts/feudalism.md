## After Rome

Without Rome, western Europe became poorer and more dangerous. Roads decayed, trade shrank and cities emptied. Germanic peoples set up their own kingdoms. Historians call the time from about 500 to 1500 the Middle Ages, or medieval period, because it sits in the middle between ancient Rome and modern times.

The strongest of the new kingdoms was that of the Franks. Their king Charlemagne ruled much of western Europe, and on Christmas Day in 800 the {{pope|pope}} crowned him emperor. After he died, his empire was divided among his grandsons.

Then came new raiders. From the late 700s, Vikings from Scandinavia attacked coasts and rivers in fast longships. Magyars raided from the east and Muslim pirates from the south.

## Land for loyalty

Kings could not defend every village, and they had little money to pay soldiers. What they had was land. So a system grew up that historians later called feudalism.^[n_feudalism_word]

| Who | Gave | Got |
|---|---|---|
| King | Large grants of land to great lords | Their loyalty and soldiers in war |
| Lord | Smaller pieces of land to {{knight|knights}} | Their service as fighters on horseback |
| Knight | Protection to the peasants on his land | Their labor and part of their crops |

A person who received land was a {{vassal|vassal}}, and the land was a {{fief|fief}}. Many people were both: a lord could be the vassal of a greater lord.

## Life on the manor

Most people were peasants who farmed. Many were {{serf|serfs}}, who could not leave the land without their lord's permission. They worked his fields as well as their own, and paid him part of their harvest. In return they got a plot of land and protection.

The basic unit was the {{manor|manor}}: the lord's house, the village, the fields, a mill and a church. A manor produced almost everything it needed.

## The Church

One institution reached across all of western Europe: the Roman Catholic Church. Its head, the pope, lived in Rome. Every village had a priest, and the Church taught that salvation came through its sacraments.

Monasteries were communities of monks who lived by strict rules of prayer and work. Monks copied books by hand and kept reading and writing alive when few others could. The Church also owned huge amounts of land and was very powerful.

*Notice: kings, lords and the Church often competed for power. Popes and kings argued over who had the right to choose bishops.*

:::practice
q: Why was the Church so powerful in medieval Europe?
answer: It was the one institution that reached every part of western Europe, with a priest in every village and a single leader, the pope. People believed it controlled the path to salvation. It owned vast lands, and its monks and clergy were among the few people who could read and write, so kings needed them to run their governments.
:::
