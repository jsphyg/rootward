## Winds that change with the seasons

The {{monsoon|monsoon}} is a seasonal wind. Over the Indian Ocean it blows from the southwest from about April to September, and from the northeast from about November to March.

Sailors learned to time their trips. A ship could sail from Arabia to India on the summer wind and come home on the winter wind. The pattern was so reliable that trade ran on a yearly schedule.

*Notice: unlike the Silk Roads, which crossed deserts and mountains, this was a sea route. Ships could carry far heavier loads, so bulky goods like cloth, timber and rice could be traded, not only luxuries.*

## Who traded what

No single empire controlled the ocean. Many peoples traded in stages, like a relay.

| Region | Goods it sold |
|---|---|
| East Africa | Gold, ivory, timber, enslaved people |
| Arabia and Persia | Horses, incense, dates |
| India | Pepper, cotton cloth, gems |
| Southeast Asia | Cloves, nutmeg and other spices |
| China | Silk, porcelain, iron goods |

Arab and Persian sailors used the {{dhow|dhow}}, a ship with large triangular sails. Chinese merchants sailed large ships called junks.

## Port cities

Trade made port cities rich.

- On the East African coast, cities such as Kilwa and Mombasa grew wealthy. Their people developed the {{swahili|Swahili}} language and culture, mixing African and Arab ways.
- Calicut in southwest India was a great pepper market.
- Malacca, on the narrow strait between Malaysia and Sumatra, controlled the passage between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea. It became one of the busiest ports in the world in the 1400s.

## Islam follows trade

Many merchants were Muslims. They married local women, built mosques and won converts, often without any conquest. Islam spread this way to the Swahili coast and to Southeast Asia. Indonesia, reached largely through trade, has more Muslims today than any other country.

## Zheng He's fleets

Between 1405 and 1433, the Chinese Ming dynasty sent the admiral Zheng He on seven great voyages. His fleets had hundreds of ships and tens of thousands of men, and some of his ships were very large.^[n_zheng_he] They reached India, Arabia and East Africa. Then the government stopped the voyages, and China turned inward.

*Notice: a few decades later, in 1498, a much smaller Portuguese fleet arrived in India. Europeans would soon fight to control this trade.*

:::practice
q: How did Islam spread to Southeast Asia and East Africa without armies?
answer: Muslim merchants from Arabia, Persia and India traded in ports around the Indian Ocean. They settled, married local women, built mosques and formed communities. Local rulers and traders often converted, partly to share in the Muslim trading network. Over generations, Islam spread from the ports to the wider population.
:::
