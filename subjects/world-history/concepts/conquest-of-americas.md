## The fall of the Aztecs

In 1519 Hernán Cortés landed in Mexico with about 500 Spanish soldiers. He marched on Tenochtitlan, the island capital of the Aztec Empire, ruled by Moctezuma II.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1519 | Cortés arrives. Peoples who paid tribute to the Aztecs, and the Tlaxcalans, old enemies the Aztecs had never conquered, join him |
| 1520 | Moctezuma dies while a prisoner. Smallpox sweeps through Tenochtitlan |
| 1521 | After a long siege, Tenochtitlan falls. Mexico City is later built on its ruins |

## The fall of the Inca

The Inca Empire had just fought a civil war between two brothers. Francisco Pizarro arrived in 1532 with fewer than 200 men. He captured the winner, Atahualpa, at Cajamarca. Atahualpa filled a room with gold and silver to buy his freedom, but the Spanish killed him in 1533. Fighting went on for decades, but the empire was broken.

## How so few won

| Reason | Why it mattered |
|---|---|
| Disease | {{smallpox|Smallpox}} and other diseases killed many native people, including leaders, and spread chaos |
| Native allies | Thousands of native warriors, who resented Aztec or Inca rule or, like the Tlaxcalans, were long-time enemies, fought alongside the Spanish |
| Weapons | Steel swords and armor, horses and guns gave an edge in battle |
| Timing | The Inca had just been weakened by civil war |

*Notice: the conquest was not only a Spanish victory. Without native allies and disease, a few hundred soldiers could not have defeated empires of millions.*

## Disease and a disaster

People in the Americas had lived apart from Europe, Africa and Asia for thousands of years. They had no immunity to smallpox, measles or flu. In the century after 1492, epidemics, war, forced labor and hunger killed a huge share of the native population, perhaps most of it.^[n_native_deaths] It was one of the greatest disasters in human history.

## Spanish rule

The {{conquistador|conquistadors}} were followed by officials, settlers and priests. Spain divided the Americas into large colonies ruled by viceroys.

- Under the {{encomienda|encomienda}} system, Spanish settlers were granted native people's labor and tribute, in return for supposedly protecting them and teaching them Christianity. In practice it was often close to slavery.
- In 1545 the Spanish found a mountain of silver at Potosí, in today's Bolivia. Forced native workers mined it, and many died. American silver flowed to Spain and on around the world.
- A priest, Bartolomé de las Casas, wrote about the cruelty and argued for native rights. Spain passed laws to protect native people, but they were often ignored.

Portugal, meanwhile, claimed Brazil in 1500.

:::practice
q: Why do historians say disease was more important than weapons in the conquest of the Americas?
answer: European diseases such as smallpox, which native peoples had never met, spread faster than the Spanish could march, killing huge numbers and many leaders. Weakened, grieving and divided societies were much easier to conquer. Weapons and horses won battles, but disease did far more to destroy the empires and their people.
:::
