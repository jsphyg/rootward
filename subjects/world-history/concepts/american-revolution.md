## Trouble after a war

Britain fought France around the world in the Seven Years' War, from 1756 to 1763, and won. In North America it took Canada from France. But the war left Britain in debt, and it decided the American colonies should help pay for their own defense.

| Year | British action | Colonial reaction |
|---|---|---|
| 1765 | The Stamp Act taxes printed papers | Protests and boycotts; it is repealed |
| 1767 | Taxes on imported goods such as tea and glass | More boycotts |
| 1773 | The Tea Act helps a British company sell tea | Colonists dump tea into Boston harbor: the Boston Tea Party |
| 1774 | Harsh laws punish Boston | Colonies form a Continental Congress |

*Notice: each side's step made the other more angry. Neither side wanted war at first.*

## War and independence

Fighting broke out at Lexington and Concord, near Boston, in April 1775. George Washington led the colonial army.

On 4 July 1776 the Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence, written mostly by Thomas Jefferson. Its words come straight from the Enlightenment: all men are created equal, with rights to "life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness," and governments get their power "from the consent of the governed."

## A new government

The first plan of government was too weak. In 1787 delegates wrote the Constitution. It created a {{republic|republic}} with three branches, Congress, a President and courts, following Montesquieu's separation of powers. In 1791 the Bill of Rights added protections such as freedom of speech and religion.

## Equal rights for whom?

The new country did not live up to its own words. Slavery stayed legal, and about one in five Americans was enslaved.^[n_jefferson] Women could not vote, and most states let only men with property vote. Native Americans lost more land as settlers moved west. Later movements, against slavery and for women's rights, used the Declaration's words to demand change.

## Why it matters

The American Revolution showed that a people could overthrow a king and build a government on Enlightenment ideas. It inspired revolutionaries in France, Haiti and Latin America. And France's huge war spending helped push it toward its own revolution.

:::practice
q: How did the Declaration of Independence use John Locke's ideas?
answer: Locke said people have natural rights and that governments rule only with their consent, so people may replace a government that abuses them. The Declaration said all men have rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, that governments get their power from the consent of the governed, and that the king had abused his power, so the colonies had the right to break away.
:::
